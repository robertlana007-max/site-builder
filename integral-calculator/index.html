    What is Integral Calculator?

    The Integral Calculator finds antiderivatives (indefinite integrals) and the value of definite integrals. For common integrals it shows the steps: the sum and constant rules, the power rule, standard integrals and simple substitutions.

    For harder integrals, such as those needing integration by parts or partial fractions, it uses a built-in algebra engine and checks the answer by differentiating it. Definite integrals are also computed numerically, so you get an accurate number even when no formula exists, including infinite limits.

    It is honest about its limits: if no antiderivative in standard functions is found, it says so. Everything runs in your browser.

    What you can use it for

    Calculus homework

    Check antiderivatives and see the rules used for basic integrals.

    Area under a curve

    Find the exact or numerical area between a curve and the x-axis, shaded on the graph.

    Improper integrals

    Use inf as a limit, for example the integral of 1/x² from 1 to infinity.

    Physics and engineering

    Get work, distance or total quantity from a rate function.

    Probability

    Integrate a density function, such as e^(−x²), numerically.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Integral Calculator

    1. Type the function

      Enter the integrand, for example x e^x or 1/(1 + x^2). You can leave out * between a number and a letter.

    2. Choose the type

      Indefinite gives the antiderivative + C. Definite asks for a lower limit a and upper limit b.

    3. Enter limits

      Limits can be numbers or expressions such as pi/2, sqrt(2) or e, and inf or -inf for infinity.

    4. Read the answer

      For indefinite integrals you get F(x) + C, checked by differentiation. For definite integrals you get the exact value when one is found, and always a decimal value.

    5. Read the steps

      For integrals solved with basic rules, each step shows the rule and the result. For harder ones, the tool says which technique is usually needed and shows that the answer was verified.

    6. Look at the graph

      In definite mode the area is shaded; parts below the x-axis count as negative. In indefinite mode the dashed curve is F(x) with C = 0.

    Features

    • Steps for common integralsSum, constant multiple, power, reciprocal, exponential, trigonometric and hyperbolic rules, with linear substitution.
    • Harder integralsIntegration by parts, partial fractions and more through the nerdamer algebra engine, loaded only when needed.
    • Verified answersEvery antiderivative is differentiated numerically and compared with your function.
    • Accurate numbersAdaptive Gauss–Kronrod integration with an error estimate, also for infinite limits.
    • Honest resultsTells you when no antiderivative is found or when an integral diverges.
    • Graph with shadingSee the area you are calculating.

    Worked example: ∫ from 0 to 3 of x² dx

    The power rule gives the antiderivative F(x) = x³/3. The definite integral is F(3) − F(0) = 27/3 − 0 = 9, and the numerical check also gives 9. The graph shades the area under the parabola from 0 to 3. Switching to indefinite shows ∫ x² dx = x³/3 + C.

    Tips and tricks

    • Always add + C to an indefinite integral: any constant gives a valid antiderivative.
    • ln|x| is used for ∫ 1/x dx so the answer also works for negative x.
    • Some functions, like e^(−x²) and sin(x)/x, have no antiderivative in elementary functions; the tool may express them with erf or Si, or you can use a definite integral for a number.
    • If a function goes to infinity inside your interval, the tool splits the integral at that point by itself: it gives the value when the area is finite (like ln|x| from −1 to 1) and warns you when it is infinite (like tan x from 0 to π).
    • Antiderivatives can look different but differ only by a constant, for example −cos²(x)/2 and sin²(x)/2.

    Problems and how to fix them

    No antiderivative was found

    The integral may have no formula in standard functions, or it may be beyond this tool. Definite integrals still work numerically.

    It says the integral does not converge

    The area is infinite, or the function blows up inside the interval, as with 1/x from −1 to 1.

    The answer looks different from my book

    Antiderivatives can differ by a constant or be written in another form. Differentiate both or compare values to check.

    Working on it… stays for a moment

    Harder integrals load the algebra engine the first time, which takes a second on slow connections.

    Questions and answers

    Does it show steps?

    Yes, for integrals solved by basic rules and substitution. For harder ones it names the usual technique and shows the answer is verified by differentiation.

    Can it do improper integrals?

    Yes. Use inf or -inf as a limit. The value is computed numerically with an error estimate.

    Why is there no answer for e^(x^2)?

    Some integrals have no antiderivative in elementary functions. Use a definite integral for a numerical value.

    How accurate are the decimal values?

    Usually to 10 or more significant digits. The tool reports the estimated error.

    Is my work sent anywhere?

    No. All calculations run in your browser.
