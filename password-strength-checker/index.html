    What is Password Strength Checker?

    The Password Strength Checker shows how hard a password would be to guess. It runs only on your device: the password is never sent, saved or logged, and the page works offline.

    It looks for the patterns that real cracking tools try first: the 10,000 most common passwords, dictionary words and names, keyboard patterns like qwerty, repeats, sequences like abc or 1234, dates and years, and look-alike swaps like @ for a. From these it estimates the number of guesses needed and the time to crack it in four kinds of attack.

    You also get the raw entropy from the characters used, the real-world entropy after patterns are found, a list of what was found, and clear tips to make it stronger.

    What you can use it for

    Check a password before using it

    Make sure a new password is not on the common list or full of patterns.

    Learn what makes passwords weak

    See exactly which part is a word, a year or a keyboard pattern.

    Teach good habits

    Show students or colleagues why P@ssw0rd is weak and a long passphrase is strong.

    Compare ideas

    Try a few passphrases and pick one that is both strong and memorable.

    Review old passwords

    Find out which old passwords should be changed first.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Password Strength Checker

    1. Type a password

      Enter it in "Password to check". It is hidden by default and checked as you type.

    2. Read the meter

      Five bars and a label from Very weak to Very strong, with a one-line summary.

    3. Check the numbers

      Length, character types, raw entropy, real-world entropy and guesses needed.

    4. See the crack times

      The table shows how long an attack would take online with and without rate limiting, and offline against slow and fast hashes.

    5. See what was found

      "What we found" lists each part: common password, word, keyboard pattern, date and so on. Press Show to see the parts themselves instead of their positions.

    6. Follow the tips

      "How to make it stronger" gives advice for this password, plus general tips. Press Clear when you are done.

    Features

    • Runs locallyNo network request is made while you type. The field is never saved by the site and is cleared when you leave the page.
    • Common password listChecks the 10,000 most common passwords (SecLists, MIT licence) and 12,000 common English words, first names and surnames.
    • Pattern searchFinds dictionary words (also backwards, with capitals or with l33t swaps), QWERTY and number pad patterns, repeats, sequences, dates and years.
    • Guess estimateSplits the password into the cheapest set of patterns, as the zxcvbn method does, and multiplies their guesses. Other characters count as 10 to 22 options each, depending on the mix of types.
    • Crack times100 guesses per hour (online, rate limited), 10 per second (online), 10,000 per second (bcrypt) and 10 billion per second (fast hash).
    • ScoreVery weak under 1,000 guesses, Weak under 1 million, Fair under 100 million, Strong under 10 billion, Very strong above. Any password on the common list is Very weak.

    Worked example: Summer2023!

    Summer2023! looks complex, with capitals, numbers and a symbol, so its raw entropy is 72 bits. But the checker finds "Summer", a common password with a capital first letter, the year 2023, and one symbol. Together that is only about 470,000 guesses, about 19 bits: rated Weak, cracked in under a second offline. A passphrase like "tulip cactus orbit fence" made of random words scores far higher.

    Tips and tricks

    • Length beats complexity: 4 or more random words make a strong, memorable passphrase.
    • Never reuse a password on more than one site.
    • Use a password manager to create and store long random passwords.
    • Turn on two-step verification for email, banking and social media.
    • Do not use names, birthdays, pets or sports teams; they are easy to find.

    Problems and how to fix them

    It says the list could not be loaded

    Reload the page. Until the list loads, only patterns are checked.

    My complex-looking password is Weak

    Swaps like @ for a and a year at the end are well known to cracking tools. Make it longer and less predictable.

    The results show positions, not text

    Press Show to see the matching parts; they are hidden while the password is hidden.

    A strong score but short offline time

    Strong scores assume the site stores passwords safely. For important accounts aim for Very strong.

    Questions and answers

    Is it safe to type my password here?

    Yes. The check runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent, saved or logged, and the page works offline. Even so, consider testing a similar password rather than your real one.

    How is strength measured?

    It looks for common passwords, words, names, keyboard patterns, repeats, sequences, dates and look-alike swaps, then estimates how many guesses an attacker would need.

    What is the difference between raw and real-world entropy?

    Raw entropy assumes every character is random. Real-world entropy accounts for the patterns found, which is what attackers exploit.

    Why is P@ssw0rd weak?

    It is the common password "password" with well-known swaps. Cracking tools try these first.

    What makes a strong password?

    Length and unpredictability: 4 or more random words, or 16+ random characters from a password manager. Use a different password for every site.
