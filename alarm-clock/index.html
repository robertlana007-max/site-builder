    What is Online Alarm Clock?

    The Online Alarm Clock is a big live clock with as many alarms as you need. Each alarm can have a label, repeat on chosen days of the week, and its own sound.

    The sounds are made by your browser, so nothing needs to download. You can set the volume, have the alarm start quietly and grow louder, and snooze it for 5 to 20 minutes.

    It is handy at a desk, in a classroom or for reminders while you work. The alarm runs inside the page, so the tab must stay open and the computer must stay awake.

    What you can use it for

    Wake-up alarm

    Use your computer or laptop as a bedside alarm with a gentle sound that slowly gets louder.

    Medicine reminders

    Set daily alarms labelled with the medicine name so you never miss a dose.

    Work and study breaks

    Remind yourself to stand up, take a break or join a meeting at a set time.

    Prayer, class or shift times

    Create repeating alarms for the same time every weekday or on selected days.

    Cooking and chores

    Set an alarm for the time the oven, laundry or a delivery will be ready.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Online Alarm Clock

    1. Check the clock

      The big clock at the top shows your computer's time with seconds. Switch between 24-hour and 12-hour time with the buttons under it.

    2. Set the time

      In Add an alarm, click the Time box and choose the hour and minutes.

    3. Add a label and sound

      Type a label such as Wake up or Take medicine, and choose one of six sounds. Click Test sound to hear it.

    4. Choose repeat days

      Tap the days the alarm should repeat, for example Mon to Fri. Leave all days off for an alarm that rings once and then switches itself off.

    5. Add the alarm

      Click Add alarm. A message tells you how long until it rings, and the alarm appears in the list with an on/off switch.

    6. Keep the page open

      Leave this tab open and your computer awake. Click once anywhere on the page so your browser allows sound.

    7. Snooze or stop

      When the alarm rings, a large window appears and the tab title flashes. Click Snooze to hear it again after the snooze time, or Stop. Escape also stops it.

    Features

    • Several alarmsAdd as many alarms as you need, each with its own label, sound and repeat days.
    • Six soundsClassic beep, digital watch, chimes, gentle rise, siren and birds, all generated in your browser.
    • Gentle wake-upThe alarm can start quietly and get louder over 30 seconds.
    • SnoozeSnooze for 5, 9, 10, 15 or 20 minutes.
    • Accurate timingThe time is checked four times a second against the real clock, even when the tab is in the background.
    • Desktop notificationsOptionally show a pop-up when the alarm rings, useful when the browser is behind other windows.
    • Saved on your deviceYour alarms and settings are still there when you open the page again.

    Worked example: weekday wake-up and a medicine reminder

    It is Sunday evening. Set the time to 06:30, type the label Wake up, choose Gentle rise, tap Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu and Fri, and click Add alarm. The message says the alarm rings in about 10 hours. Then add a second alarm at 21:00 labelled Vitamin D, with no repeat days, so it rings once tonight and switches itself off. Keep the laptop plugged in with sleep turned off and the tab open. At 06:30 on Monday the sound starts softly and grows louder; click Snooze 9 min for a little more sleep, then Stop.

    Tips and tricks

    • Turn off sleep mode on your computer, or plug in your laptop, so it stays awake through the night.
    • Click anywhere on the page once after opening it. Browsers block sound from pages you have not touched.
    • Use Test sound and set the volume before you rely on an alarm.
    • Turn on desktop notifications to see a pop-up even when you are working in another program.
    • If your computer was asleep at alarm time, the alarm still rings when it wakes if it is less than 10 minutes late. Otherwise you see a Missed message.
    • For important alarms on a phone, use the phone's built-in clock app, because phones often pause web pages when the screen locks.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The alarm did not make a sound

    Check your volume and that the tab is not muted. Click on the page once after opening it, then use Test sound. A red note at the top tells you when the browser is still blocking sound.

    The alarm did not ring at all

    The tab was probably closed or the computer went to sleep. Keep the tab open, keep the computer awake, and check that the alarm's switch is on.

    The alarm rang a few minutes late

    Your computer was asleep or very busy. The alarm rings as soon as it can and tells you how late it was.

    I do not see notifications

    Click Turn on desktop notifications and allow them. If you blocked them earlier, allow them again from the lock icon next to the address bar.

    My alarms disappeared

    Alarms are saved in this browser on this device. They are lost if you clear your browsing data or use a private window.

    Questions and answers

    Will the alarm ring if I close the tab?

    No. The alarm runs in this page, so the tab must stay open and the computer must stay awake. If it was asleep, the alarm rings when it wakes if less than 10 minutes late, or shows as missed.

    Does it work when the tab is in the background?

    Yes. The alarm checks the time four times a second using a background timer that browsers do not slow down, and the tab title flashes when it rings.

    Why is there no sound?

    Browsers block sound until you click the page once. Click anywhere on the page after opening it, check your volume, and use Test sound.

    Are my alarms saved?

    Yes. Alarms and settings are saved in your browser on this device and come back when you open the page again.

    Can I use it on my phone?

    Yes, but phones often pause web pages when the screen locks. For important alarms on a phone, use the phone’s own clock app.
