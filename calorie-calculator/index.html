What is Calorie Calculator?

The Calorie Calculator estimates how many calories you burn in a day and how many to eat to keep, lose or gain weight. You enter your sex, age, weight, height and activity level, and it shows your daily calories at rest (BMR) and for maintenance, slow weight loss, weight loss and weight gain.

It helps adults plan meals, set a realistic diet goal or understand how activity changes their energy needs. It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a widely used formula, and works in metric or imperial units.

The calculation runs in your browser. Results are estimates and not medical advice. Talk to a doctor before a big change in diet.

What you can use it for

Know your maintenance calories

Find about how many calories keep your weight the same.

Plan weight loss

Use the "Slow weight loss" or "Weight loss" figures as a daily target.

Plan weight gain

Use the "Weight gain" figure if you want to build up.

See the effect of exercise

Change the "Activity" level to see how much more you can eat.

Understand your BMR

See how many calories your body uses at full rest.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Calorie Calculator

  1. Choose units and sex

    In "Units", pick "Metric (kg, cm)" or "Imperial (lb, ft, in)". In "Sex", choose Male or Female, because the formula differs slightly.

  2. Enter your age

    Type your age in "Age (years)". It must be from 18 to 100, because the formula is made for adults.

  3. Enter weight and height

    Type your weight in kg or lb. For height, use "Height (cm)" in metric, or "Height (ft)" and "Height (in)" in imperial.

  4. Pick your activity level

    In "Activity", choose from "Little or no exercise" to "Very active or physical job". The default is "Moderate: 3-5 days a week".

  5. Read the results

    "Maintain weight" is your daily calories to stay the same. "Slow weight loss" is 250 less, "Weight loss" is 500 less, and "Weight gain" is 500 more. "At rest (BMR)" is what you burn with no activity.

Features

  • Mifflin-St Jeor equationBMR = 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age, then + 5 for males or − 161 for females.
  • Activity multipliersMaintenance = BMR × 1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725 or 1.9, depending on the activity level you choose.
  • Loss and gain targetsFixed steps of −250, −500 and +500 calories a day from maintenance.
  • Metric and imperialImperial values are converted (1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 inch = 2.54 cm).
  • Rounded resultsAll values are rounded to whole calories.
  • Live updatesResults change as you edit any field.

Worked example: a 30-year-old woman

A 30-year-old female weighs 65 kg, is 165 cm tall and exercises 3-5 days a week. BMR = 10 × 65 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 30 − 161 = 650 + 1031.25 − 150 − 161 = 1370.25, shown as 1,370. Moderate activity multiplies by 1.55, so "Maintain weight" is 2,124. "Slow weight loss" is 1,874, "Weight loss" is 1,624 and "Weight gain" is 2,624 calories a day.

Tips and tricks

  • Be honest about your activity level; most people overestimate it.
  • Use the result as a starting point, then adjust after a few weeks based on real progress.
  • Very low calorie diets can be unsafe; do not go far below your BMR without medical advice.
  • Recalculate after a big change in weight, because your needs change too.
  • This formula is for adults and may not suit pregnancy, athletes or medical conditions.

Problems and how to fix them

All results show a dash

Enter a weight and height above 0 and an age from 18 to 100.

The numbers look far too high or low

Check the units. Pounds typed in metric mode, or meters in the cm box, give wrong results.

The height boxes changed

Switching "Units" swaps the cm box for the ft and in boxes. Enter your height again.

I am not losing weight on the target

Calorie needs vary from person to person. Track for a few weeks and adjust, or ask a doctor or dietitian.

Questions and answers

Which formula does this use?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation for calories burned at rest, multiplied by a standard activity factor.

How fast will I lose weight?

A deficit of about 500 calories a day is commonly linked with losing roughly half a kilogram, or one pound, a week. Real results vary.

How accurate is it?

It is an estimate for adults. Individual needs can differ by a few hundred calories. It is not medical advice.
