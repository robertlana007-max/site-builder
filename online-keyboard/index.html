What is Online Keyboard (All Languages)?

The Online Keyboard lets you type in 49 languages and writing systems from any computer, tablet or phone, without installing a keyboard or changing system settings. Choose a language and either tap the on-screen keys or press your physical keys, which are mapped to that language's standard layout.

It helps travellers using a shared computer, students learning a language, people writing to family in their home language, teachers preparing worksheets, and anyone who needs a few special characters such as ñ, ß, ç or ł. Right-to-left scripts like Arabic and Hebrew are shown right to left, Korean letters join into syllables, and Japanese romaji turns into kana.

The text is standard Unicode, so it pastes correctly into email, documents, chat apps and search boxes. Nothing you type is uploaded.

What you can use it for

Write in your own language on any computer

Type Arabic, Hindi, Russian, Greek and more on a computer at work, school, a library or a hotel.

Add accents and special letters

Type é, ñ, ü, ß, ç, ł, ș, ő or ð correctly instead of leaving them out.

Learn a new alphabet

See which key gives which letter and practise typing Cyrillic, Greek, Hangul, Thai or Devanagari.

Search and message in another language

Type a name, address or search in its original script and copy it.

Teaching and worksheets

Prepare text in learners' languages with correct letters and tone marks.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Online Keyboard (All Languages)

  1. Choose a keyboard

    The tool starts with your browser's language, or the keyboard you used last. Type in Find a language (English or native names both work) or pick one from the Keyboard list.

  2. Type with your keys

    Click in the text box and press your computer keys. For non-Latin languages each key types the letter shown on the on-screen key. Hold Shift for the second set of letters; AltGr (right Alt) gives extra characters where available.

  3. Or tap the on-screen keys

    Tap keys with a mouse or finger. Shift and AltGr apply to the next key. Tick Hide the phone keyboard on a phone so only the on-screen keys appear.

  4. Add accents and special letters

    For Latin languages, tap an accent key such as ´ or ^ and then a letter, or tap a ready-made letter in the row above the keyboard. Tick Accent keys on my keyboard to type ' then e for é on your own keyboard.

  5. Use the smart modes

    Korean joins letters into syllables automatically. Japanese turns romaji into hiragana or katakana. Vietnamese Telex adds marks and tones. Pinyin turns ni3 into nǐ. Amharic turns consonant plus vowel into Ge'ez syllables.

  6. Adjust and copy

    Choose native digits, a Nastaliq font for Urdu, Persian or Pashto, and the text size. Then press Copy text or Download .txt.

Worked example: three languages in a minute

Choose Korean and press the keys d k s s u d g k t p d y: the text box shows 안녕하세요, built syllable by syllable. Switch to Japanese (Hiragana) and type konnichiha, then a space: it becomes こんにちは. Switch to French, tap ^ and then e to get ê. Press Copy text and paste everything into your message.

Tips and tricks

  • Look at the small Latin letter in the corner of each on-screen key to learn which computer key gives that letter.
  • If your computer already has the language installed, choose the same language here and untick Type with my computer keys to keep your own layout.
  • In Korean, a space or punctuation ends the current syllable; clicking elsewhere in the text starts a new one.
  • For Japanese, type nn for ん at the end of a word, and double a consonant (kitte) for a small っ.
  • On phones, tick Hide the phone keyboard so the on-screen keys have the whole screen.

Problems and how to fix them

Letters show as empty boxes

Your device is missing a font for that script. Most phones and computers include them; on older systems, install a free Noto font for the language.

My keys type English letters

Make sure Type with my computer keys is ticked and the cursor is in the text box. For Latin languages your own keyboard layout is used as it is.

Ctrl or Cmd shortcuts type letters

They should not: shortcuts like copy, paste and select all are left alone. If a key still types something odd, check that AltGr is not held.

Accents do not combine

Press the accent first, then the letter. If a letter has no ready-made accented form, the accent is added as a combining mark, which still displays correctly in most apps.

Questions and answers

Which languages are included?

Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Bengali, Punjabi, Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Russian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian, Serbian, Kazakh, Greek, Hebrew, Georgian, Armenian, Thai, Korean, Japanese, Amharic, Vietnamese, Chinese Pinyin, Turkish, Azerbaijani, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, Czech, Romanian, Hungarian, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Icelandic, Dutch, Swahili, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and English.

How do my computer keys type another alphabet?

Each key position is mapped to the chosen language's standard layout (for example ЙЦУКЕН for Russian, InScript for Indian languages, Kedmanee for Thai, 2-set for Korean), whatever layout your computer uses. The small Latin letter on each on-screen key shows which key to press.

How do I type accents like é, ñ or ş?

Tap an accent key (´ ` ^ ¨ ~ ˇ and more), then a letter. Most Latin languages also have a row of their special letters, and AltGr (right Alt) with a letter gives common accented letters. You can also turn on accent keys for your own keyboard: ' then e gives é.

How do Korean, Japanese and Vietnamese work?

Korean jamo join into syllable blocks as you type. For Japanese, type romaji (konnichiha) and it turns into hiragana or katakana. For Vietnamese, Telex typing turns aa into â, dd into đ and s f r x j into tone marks; tone buttons are on screen too.

Is my text saved or uploaded?

No. Your text stays in your browser. Only your last chosen keyboard is remembered on this device.
