    What is Blur & Censor Image?

    Blur & Censor Image hides parts of a picture before you share it: faces of strangers or children, car number plates, names, addresses, card numbers or anything else private.

    Draw rectangles, ellipses or free brush strokes, and choose for each one whether it is blurred, turned into a pixel mosaic or covered with a solid bar. Every area can be moved, resized, changed or removed until you download, and you can also blur the whole image. The download keeps the full resolution of your picture.

    Everything runs in your browser, so the private image you are censoring is never uploaded.

    What you can use it for

    Protect children's privacy

    Blur faces of children in school or party photos before posting them.

    Hide number plates and house numbers

    Cover identifying details in photos of cars or homes.

    Redact screenshots

    Black out names, emails, account numbers or chat messages before sharing a screenshot.

    Share documents safely

    Hide ID numbers and signatures on photos of documents.

    Soft background or focus effect

    Blur the whole image for a backdrop, or blur everything except the subject with large areas.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Blur & Censor Image

    1. Open your image

      Click "Click or drop an image here", drop a file, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V.

    2. Choose a shape

      Click Rectangle, Ellipse or Brush. Brush is good for odd shapes; set its size with "Brush size".

    3. Choose the effect

      Under "Effect" pick Blur, Pixelate (mosaic) or Solid bar (safest), then set "Strength" or the bar colour.

    4. Draw over what to hide

      Drag across the picture. Each area appears in the list below the picture. Add as many as you need.

    5. Adjust areas

      Click an area in the list, or use "Select and move" and tap it. Drag it to move it, drag a corner square to resize it, change its effect or strength, or remove it with ✕ or the Delete key.

    6. Download

      Choose the format, then click "Download image". The file is saved at the original size and named like photo-blurred.jpg.

    Features

    • Three shapesRectangles, ellipses (great for faces) and brush strokes.

    • Paste supportPaste a screenshot straight from the clipboard.

    Worked example: sharing a street photo safely

    Omar wants to post a 4032 × 3024 px street photo that shows a parked car and two people. He picks Ellipse with Blur at 70% and drags over each face, then switches to Rectangle and Solid bar and covers the number plate. He notices the second face area is a little too small, clicks it in the list and drags its corner to enlarge it. He downloads street-blurred.jpg at the full 4032 × 3024 px.

    Tips and tricks

    • For text such as passwords, card numbers or IDs, use Solid bar. Blur and pixelation at low strength can sometimes be partly reversed.
    • Make areas a little larger than the thing you hide, so no edges peek out.
    • Ellipses suit faces; brush strokes suit diagonal text or irregular shapes.
    • Changing the effect or strength while an area is selected changes that area; picking a new shape tool starts fresh settings for the next area.
    • Check the downloaded file before sharing it.

    Problems and how to fix them

    Clicking does not create an area

    Drag across the picture rather than clicking. Tiny areas from a single click are removed automatically.

    Changing the strength changed the wrong area

    Settings apply to the selected (highlighted) area. Click a shape tool first to start a new area with new settings.

    The image will not open

    The format may not be supported by your browser (for example HEIC). Convert it to JPG or PNG first.

    Saving a very large image fails

    Very large images can exceed the browser's memory, especially on phones. Try a smaller copy.

    Questions and answers

    Is my image uploaded?

    No. The blurring happens in your browser and the image never leaves your device.

    Which effect is safest for passwords and ID numbers?

    Solid bar. Light blur or large pixel blocks can sometimes be partly undone, so use a solid bar, or high strength, for anything sensitive.

    Can I change an area after drawing it?

    Yes. Select it in the list or with Select and move, then drag it, resize it from a corner, change its effect or strength, or delete it.

    Does it find faces automatically?

    Only in browsers that have a built-in face detector, where a Find faces button appears. Always check the result and cover anything it missed by hand.

    Is the original image changed?

    No. You download a new copy; your original file stays as it was.
