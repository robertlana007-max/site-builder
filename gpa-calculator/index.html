What is GPA Calculator?

The GPA Calculator works out your grade point average on the common 4.0 scale. You add a row for each course, enter its credits and pick its letter grade, and the tool shows your GPA, your total credits and your total grade points.

It helps high school and college students check a term GPA, plan which grades they need, and see how a course with more credits changes the average. It uses a standard letter grade scale from A+ to F.

Everything is calculated in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere.

What you can use it for

Check your term GPA

Enter this term’s courses and see your GPA right away.

Plan target grades

Try different grades for courses still in progress to see what GPA you could reach.

See the weight of credits

Notice how a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than a 1-credit course.

Prepare applications

Check your GPA before filling in a scholarship or college form.

Compare two scenarios

Change one grade to see how much your average changes.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use GPA Calculator

  1. Look at the course rows

    The tool starts with 4 rows named Course 1 to Course 4. Each row has a name box, a "Credits" box (default 3) and a "Grade" list.

  2. Name your courses

    Type a name in each row, such as Math, if you like. The name is optional and does not change the result.

  3. Enter credits and grades

    Type the credit hours in "Credits" (steps of 0.5 are fine) and pick a grade from A+ to F in "Grade". Rows with no grade or 0 credits are skipped.

  4. Add more courses

    Click "+ Add course" to add another row at the bottom. Click the × at the end of a row to remove it; the remaining rows are renumbered.

  5. Read the results

    "GPA (4.0 scale)" shows your average with 2 decimals. "Total credits" adds up the counted credits and "Grade points" is the sum of credits × grade points.

  6. Start again

    Click "Clear all" to remove every row and start over with 4 empty rows.

Features

  • Weighted GPA formulaGPA = the sum of (credits × grade points) ÷ total credits. Courses with more credits count more.
  • Standard 4.0 scaleA+ and A = 4.0, A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B- = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C- = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, F = 0.
  • Unlimited coursesAdd as many rows as you need with "+ Add course".
  • Half creditsCredits can be decimals, such as 1.5.
  • Skips empty rowsA row only counts when it has a grade and credits above 0.
  • Live resultsThe GPA updates every time you change a credit or grade.

Worked example: a four-course term

You took four courses: Biology with 3 credits and an A (4.0), Chemistry with 4 credits and a B+ (3.3), English with 3 credits and a B (3.0), and Art with 2 credits and an A- (3.7). Grade points are 3 × 4.0 + 4 × 3.3 + 3 × 3.0 + 2 × 3.7 = 12 + 13.2 + 9 + 7.4 = 41.6. Total credits are 12. The tool shows Grade points 41.6, Total credits 12 and GPA 41.6 ÷ 12 = 3.47.

Tips and tricks

  • Check your school’s scale; some give A+ a 4.3, but this tool gives it 4.0, as the note under the results says.
  • Leave the grade as "–" for courses with no grade yet, and they will not be counted.
  • Pass/fail courses usually do not count toward GPA, so leave them out.
  • To find your cumulative GPA, add every course from all terms.
  • There is no button to delete one row; set its grade to "–" to ignore it.

Problems and how to fix them

The GPA shows a dash

No row has both a grade and credits above 0. Pick a grade in at least one row.

My school’s GPA is different

Your school may use another scale, weighted honors grades or a different way to count retakes.

A course is not being counted

Check that its credits are above 0 and a grade is selected.

Clear all removed my names

"Clear all" resets everything. Type the names again if you need them.

Questions and answers

How is GPA calculated?

Each grade is turned into points, multiplied by the course credits, added up, then divided by the total credits.

What points does each grade get?

A and A+ are 4.0, A- 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B- 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C- 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0 and F 0.

My school uses a different scale. Is this right for me?

Scales differ between schools. Check your school's own table if your result does not match your transcript.
