    What is PowerPoint to PDF?

    PowerPoint to PDF turns a .pptx presentation into a PDF with one page per slide. It draws the slide text, bullet lists, pictures, shapes, tables, SmartArt and backgrounds, using the layouts and theme colours stored in your file.

    A PDF is easier to share than a presentation: it opens on any phone or computer, keeps its look, and is small enough to email. Teachers, students and office workers use it to hand out slides, submit coursework or print notes.

    The conversion runs completely in your browser. The .pptx file is opened with JSZip and drawn with pdf-lib on your device, so it is never uploaded.

    What you can use it for

    Hand out slides

    Give your audience a PDF of the talk instead of the editable presentation.

    Submit coursework

    Many schools and portals accept only PDF files.

    Print slides

    Print one slide per page in a format every printer understands.

    Share on phones

    PDFs open instantly on any phone, even without PowerPoint installed.

    Archive a deck

    Keep a version that cannot be changed by accident.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use PowerPoint to PDF

    1. Add the presentation

      Click the box that says "Click or drop a PowerPoint file (.pptx) here", or drag the file onto it. The name and size appear below.

    2. Choose about hidden slides

      Hidden slides are skipped. Tick "Include hidden slides" if you want them in the PDF too.

    3. Make the PDF

      Click "Make PDF". The message counts the slides as they are drawn, for example "Drawing slide 4 of 20…".

    4. Check the previews

      Small pictures of the pages appear under the buttons so you can check the result.

    5. Read any notes

      If something could not be drawn, such as a chart, a short list tells you which slide it was on.

    6. Download

      Click "Download PDF". The file has the same name as your presentation.

    Features

    • One page per slidePages have the exact size of your slides, whether 16:9, 4:3 or custom.
    • Real, selectable textTitles, bullets and numbered lists are real text in the PDF, so you can search and copy them.
    • Layouts and themesPlaceholder positions, master text styles, theme colours and backgrounds are taken from your file.
    • Pictures and shapesJPEG, PNG, GIF, BMP, WebP and SVG pictures, cropped pictures, common shapes, arrows, lines, groups and rotated items are drawn.
    • Tables and SmartArtTables with cell colours and borders, and SmartArt graphics are included.
    • Honest about gapsCharts and other objects that cannot be drawn are marked on the page and listed by slide number.

    Worked example: a 25-slide class presentation

    Kenji has biology-talk.pptx with 25 slides, two of them hidden. He drops it in and clicks Make PDF. After a few seconds the message reads "Your PDF is ready: 23 pages", and the list says "Slide 9: chart could not be drawn". He checks the previews, sees slide 9 has a grey box where the chart was, pastes a screenshot of the chart into that slide in PowerPoint, and converts again.

    Tips and tricks

    • If a chart matters, replace it with a picture of the chart in your presentation before converting.
    • Fonts are replaced with Noto Sans. If a title wraps onto an extra line, make the text box a little wider.
    • For handouts, keep the default and leave hidden slides out.
    • Old .ppt files must be saved as .pptx first in PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice.
    • For a pixel-perfect copy with your own fonts, use File, Export, PDF in PowerPoint or Keynote.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The message says it is an old .ppt file

    Open the file in PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice Impress and save it as .pptx.

    The message says the file is password protected

    Remove the password in PowerPoint (File, Info, Protect Presentation), save, and try again.

    A grey box says Chart (not shown)

    Charts cannot be drawn by this tool. Paste the chart as a picture into the slide, then convert again.

    Text is a little bigger or wraps differently

    The PDF uses the Noto Sans font, which is wider than some fonts. Shorten the text or widen the box.

    Questions and answers

    Is my presentation uploaded?

    No. The .pptx file is opened and turned into a PDF inside your browser, on your own device.

    Will the PDF look exactly like my slides?

    Very close, but not always identical. Text uses the Noto Sans font, so lines can wrap a little differently from your fonts. For a perfect copy, use File, Export, PDF in PowerPoint, Keynote or LibreOffice.

    What is not included?

    Animations, transitions, sound and video are left out (videos show their cover picture). Charts, 3D models, equations and some effects cannot be drawn; the tool tells you which slides had them.

    Can I convert old .ppt files?

    No, only .pptx (also .ppsx and .potx). Open an old .ppt in PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice and save it as .pptx first.

    Is the text in the PDF selectable?

    Yes. Text is real text, so you can search and copy it.

    What about hidden slides?

    They are skipped unless you tick Include hidden slides.
