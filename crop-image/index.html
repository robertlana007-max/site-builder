What is Crop Image?

Crop Image cuts away the parts of a picture you do not want. You drag a box over the part to keep, or type its exact size and position in pixels, then download the result as JPG, PNG or WebP.

Ready-made shapes make it easy to fit common places: a square for profile pictures, 4:5 for Instagram posts, 16:9 for YouTube thumbnails and slides, 9:16 for Stories and Reels, 3:2 for prints, and 35 × 45 mm for passport and visa photos. You can also rotate the picture by 90 degrees or flip it before you crop.

It works with a mouse, a finger or a pen, so it is just as easy on a phone. The picture is cropped in your browser and never uploaded.

What you can use it for

Make a profile picture

Choose Square 1:1 and place the box over your face for a neat avatar on any social network or work chat.

Fit an Instagram post or Story

Use 4:5 for a tall feed post or 9:16 for a Story or Reel, so nothing gets cut off by the app.

Prepare a passport photo

Pick Passport photo 35 × 45 mm and get a 413 × 531 pixel image that prints at the right size.

Remove clutter from a screenshot

Keep only the part of the screen that matters before you share it.

Exact sizes for a website

Type the width, height and position in pixels to cut banners and thumbnails to a precise size.

Straighten a sideways photo

Rotate a photo that was taken on its side, then crop it.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Crop Image

  1. Open your picture

    Click "Click or drop an image here" or drag a file onto it. You can also paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V. The picture appears with a crop box over it.

  2. Choose a shape

    In "Shape (aspect ratio)" pick Free to crop any shape, or one of the presets such as Square 1:1, Instagram portrait 4:5 or Story / Reel 9:16. The box jumps to the largest area of that shape.

  3. Move and resize the box

    Drag inside the box to move it. Drag a corner or edge handle to resize it. With a shape chosen, the box keeps that shape. You can also drag on the picture outside the box to draw a new one.

  4. Type exact numbers if needed

    "Width", "Height", "Left (X)" and "Top (Y)" show the box in pixels of the picture and update as you drag. Type a number and the box follows. With a fixed shape, changing the width also changes the height.

  5. Rotate or flip

    Use "Rotate left", "Rotate right", "Flip" and "Flip up/down" to turn the picture. "Reset" undoes all turning and selects the whole picture.

  6. Choose the file type

    In "Save as" pick JPG for photos, PNG for screenshots and pictures with transparency, or WebP for small web files. For JPG and WebP, set the Quality.

  7. Crop and download

    Press "Crop image". A preview appears with the size and file size, and "Download" saves it with a name like photo-cropped-1080x1350.jpg.

Features

  • Touch friendlyLarge handles and pointer events let you crop with a finger without the page scrolling under you.
  • Aspect ratio presetsFree, original shape, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9, 9:16, 3:2 and 35 × 45 mm passport.
  • Pixel-exact inputsWidth, height and position boxes stay in sync with the crop box in both directions.
  • Rotate and flipTurn by 90 degrees and mirror the picture, with the crop box following along.
  • Full resolutionThe crop is cut from the original picture, not the smaller preview, so no detail is lost.
  • Three output formatsJPG, PNG or WebP, with a quality setting for JPG and WebP.
  • Keyboard nudgingFocus the box and use the arrow keys to move it 1 pixel at a time, or 10 with Shift.

Worked example: an Instagram portrait post

Lena has a 4032 × 3024 landscape photo of a cake and wants a tall Instagram post. She opens it, chooses "Instagram portrait 4:5" and the box becomes 2419 × 3024 pixels, the tallest 4:5 area. She drags the box until the cake is in the middle, sets "Save as" to JPG with quality 90 and presses "Crop image". The note reads "2419 × 3024 px · JPG · 1.9 MB" and she downloads cake-cropped-2419x3024.jpg, ready to post without Instagram trimming it.

Tips and tricks

  • Choose the shape first, then position the box: switching shape afterwards resets the box to the largest area of the new shape.
  • For passport photos, check your country's rules for head size and background, then keep the 413 × 531 px option ticked so it prints at 35 × 45 mm.
  • Use PNG for screenshots with text: it stays sharp. Use JPG or WebP for photos to keep files small.
  • On a phone, use the corner handles for size and drag the middle of the box to move it.
  • To cut several pictures to exactly the same size, type the same width and height for each.

Problems and how to fix them

The box will not get bigger

With a fixed shape, the box stops when it reaches the edge of the picture. Move it towards the middle, or choose Free.

My iPhone photo will not open

HEIC photos cannot be opened by most browsers. Convert it to JPG first with the Image Converter, or set the camera to Most Compatible.

The downloaded file is a PNG instead of WebP

Some older browsers, including older Safari, cannot create WebP files. The tool saves a PNG instead and says so.

The transparent background turned white

JPG cannot store transparency. Choose PNG or WebP in "Save as" to keep it.

Questions and answers

Does cropping reduce the quality?

No. The crop is cut from your original picture at full resolution. Saving as PNG keeps every pixel exactly; JPG and WebP use the quality you choose.

Can I crop on my phone?

Yes. The crop box and its handles work with your finger, and the page does not scroll while you drag.

What size is the passport photo?

The passport shape is 35 × 45 mm. With the resize option ticked, the result is 413 × 531 pixels, which prints at exactly 35 × 45 mm at 300 dpi. Check your country's rules for head size and background.

Can I rotate or mirror the picture?

Yes. Rotate it left or right by 90 degrees, or flip it horizontally or vertically, before you crop.

Is my image uploaded?

No. Everything happens in your browser.
