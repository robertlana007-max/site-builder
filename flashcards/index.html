What is Flashcards?

Flashcards lets you make decks of question-and-answer cards and study them. Each card has a front, such as a word or question, and a back, such as the meaning or answer. Click or tap a card to flip it.

It uses spaced repetition with Leitner boxes: cards you know move up a box and come back less often, and cards you miss go back to box 1 and come back soon. You spend your time on what you have not learned yet.

You can type cards, paste a list such as “term - definition”, or open a CSV or TSV file. Everything is saved in your browser and nothing is uploaded.

What you can use it for

Vocabulary

Learn words for a new language, one word on the front and its meaning on the back.

Exam facts

Revise dates, formulas, definitions and key terms for any subject.

Medical and law terms

Work through large decks a little each day with spaced repetition.

Teaching

Make a deck for your class and share the .json or CSV file.

Moving from other apps

Bring in cards exported as CSV or copied from a spreadsheet.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Flashcards

  1. Make a deck

    Click “New deck” and give it a name. You can also start with the example deck of world capitals.

  2. Add cards

    In “Edit cards”, click “+ Add card” and type the front and back. Pressing Tab in the last back box adds another card.

  3. Or import a list

    In “Import and export”, paste lines such as “photosynthesis - how plants make food”, or copied spreadsheet rows, and click “Add these cards”. You can also open a CSV, TSV, TXT or JSON file.

  4. Study

    Open “Study”. Read the front, think of the answer, then press Space or tap the card to flip it.

  5. Mark your answer

    Click “Know it” (or press → or swipe right) if you got it, or “Still learning” (← or swipe left) if you did not. Missed cards come back before the round ends.

  6. Come back tomorrow

    With “Cards due today” chosen, you only see cards that are due. Known cards wait 1, 3, 7 and then 14 days.

Features

  • 3D flip cardsCards turn over with a flip animation. If your device asks for reduced motion, they switch without moving.
  • Leitner spaced repetitionFive boxes with waits of 0, 1, 3, 7 and 14 days, shown under the card.
  • Keyboard and touchSpace to flip, → know it, ← still learning, Backspace to go back; tap to flip and swipe on phones.
  • Shuffle and reverseShuffle the order, or show the back first to test yourself the other way round.
  • Easy importPaste “term - definition” lines, tab-separated spreadsheet rows or CSV, or open a file.
  • Export and backupDownload a deck as JSON (with your progress) or CSV, or back up all decks in one file.
  • Handles big decksLarge collections are stored in IndexedDB so thousands of cards still save.

Worked example: 20 Spanish words

Click “New deck” and name it “Spanish week 1”. Open “Import and export”, paste 20 lines such as “perro - dog” and “gato - cat”, and click “Add these cards”. The deck now shows (20). Go to “Study”: the bar shows 1 / 20. You flip “perro”, see “dog” and press →. You miss “gato”, so you press ←; it is added to the end of the round, so the counter now counts to 21. At the end you see “19 right the first time, 1 needed another go”. The boxes under the card now show 1 card in box 1 and 19 in box 2. Tomorrow, with “Cards due today”, those 19 come back for review.

Tips and tricks

  • Keep each card to one fact. Short cards are easier to remember than long ones.
  • Say the answer out loud before you flip. It makes the test honest.
  • Study a little every day with “Cards due today” rather than everything at once.
  • Download a backup (.json) now and then. Decks live in this browser only.
  • Turn on “Show the back first” once you know a deck, to practise the other direction.

Problems and how to fix them

“Nothing is due right now”

You have answered every due card. Choose “All cards” in the Study menu to practise anyway, or come back when the next card is due.

My pasted cards all went on the front

Each line needs a separator between the front and back: “ - ” with spaces, a tab, or a comma. Lines without one become cards with an empty back that you can fill in under “Edit cards”.

The CSV has a header row

A first row such as “term, definition” or “front, back” is skipped automatically. Other headers become a card you can delete.

My decks are missing

Decks are saved in the browser you made them in. A different browser, device or private window starts empty. Import your backup .json file there.

Questions and answers

How does the spaced repetition work?

It uses five Leitner boxes. A card you know moves up one box and waits 1, 3, 7 or 14 days before it is due again. A card you miss goes back to box 1 and comes back in the same round.

What can I import?

Pasted lines like term - definition, rows copied from a spreadsheet, CSV or TSV files, and the JSON files this tool exports. A header row such as term, definition is skipped.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

Yes. Space flips the card, the right arrow means Know it, the left arrow means Still learning and Backspace goes back one card. On touch screens, tap to flip and swipe right or left.

Where are my decks saved?

In this browser on your device. Large collections are kept in IndexedDB. Download a backup to move decks to another device.

Can I turn off the flip animation?

If your device is set to reduce motion, or animations are off in the site settings, cards switch sides without moving.
