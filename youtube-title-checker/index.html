    What is YouTube Title Checker?

    The YouTube Title Checker reviews your title and description before you publish. It checks the title length against YouTube's 100-character limit, whether your main keyword appears and how early, capital letters, repeated punctuation, emojis and numbers.

    It also checks the description: whether the keyword is in the first lines, the overall length, the hashtag count (more than 15 and YouTube ignores them all) and whether chapter timestamps are set up correctly. A score from 0 to 100 sums it up.

    A live preview shows your title as it appears in the phone home feed, computer search results, suggested videos and the watch page, in light or dark mode, with your own thumbnail if you add one, and tells you after how many characters the title is cut in each view.

    What you can use it for

    Before uploading

    Catch titles that are too long or miss the keyword.

    Improving old videos

    Rewrite titles and descriptions on videos with low views.

    Testing title ideas

    Compare versions and see which reads best when cut off.

    Setting up chapters

    Check your timestamps are in the right format.

    Teams and clients

    Review titles together before publishing.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use YouTube Title Checker

    1. Type the title

      Enter your planned title. The counter shows characters out of 100.

    2. Add the main keyword

      Type the phrase people would search for, such as "balcony tomatoes".

    3. Paste the description

      Include the opening lines, chapters, links and hashtags.

    4. Read the checks

      Green ticks are fine; ! marks are suggestions; ✕ marks are problems to fix.

    5. Check the previews

      Switch between Phone home feed, Computer search, Suggested videos and Watch page, and try Dark mode.

    6. Copy

      Copy the final title and description back into YouTube Studio.

    Features

    • Score out of 100A quick summary of all the checks.
    • Keyword checksIn the title, how early, and in the first lines of the description.
    • Four previewsPhone, search, suggested and watch page, light or dark.
    • Cut-off finderShows exactly how many characters fit in each view.

    • Your thumbnailAdd it to the preview; it stays on your device.

    Worked example: a long gardening title

    A title of 88 characters, "How I Grew 40 Tomatoes on a Tiny Balcony – Complete Beginner Guide for Small Spaces 2026", gets a ! for length. On a phone the preview cuts it after about 67 characters, so "for Small Spaces 2026" is hidden. Moving the keyword phrase to the start and shortening it to "Balcony Tomatoes: How I Grew 40 in a Tiny Space" fixes both checks.

    Tips and tricks

    • Put your keyword in the first few words of the title.
    • Write the first two lines of the description as a short summary; that is what people see.
    • Use one or two numbers or brackets, like "(Full Guide)", to stand out.
    • Avoid all capitals and "!!!", which can look like spam.
    • Keep titles honest; a title that matches the video keeps viewers watching.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The keyword is "only partly" in the title

    The words appear but not as the exact phrase. Use the phrase if it reads naturally.

    Chapters are marked with !

    Start with 0:00 on its own line and include at least three timestamps.

    The preview differs from my phone

    Phones differ in screen width and text size. Treat the cut-off point as a guide.

    Questions and answers

    How long can a YouTube title be?

    100 characters. On most screens about 60 to 70 show before the title is cut off, so put the important words first.

    How long can the description be?

    5,000 characters. Only the first two or three lines show before "more".

    How many hashtags can I use?

    If a description has more than 15 hashtags, YouTube ignores all of them. The first three can show above the title.

    How do chapters work?

    Start a list of timestamps with 0:00, include at least three, and make each chapter at least 10 seconds long.

    Is the preview exact?

    It is a close guide using YouTube's usual font sizes and widths. Real devices and window sizes vary a little.
