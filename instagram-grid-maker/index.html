What is Instagram Grid Maker?

Instagram Grid Maker cuts one picture into 3, 6, 9 or 12 tiles so that, once you post them, your profile grid shows the picture as one big image. It makes portrait 4:5 tiles (1080 × 1350) for the newer tall profile grid, or classic square 1:1 tiles (1080 × 1080).

You choose exactly which part of the photo goes into the grid by dragging and zooming it, see a profile preview, and download every tile as a high quality JPG, numbered in the order you should post them.

The photo is cut inside your browser, so it is never uploaded. This tool is not made by or connected to Instagram.

What you can use it for

Profile launch

Start a new account or rebrand with one large banner-style picture across the top rows.

Product reveals

Spread a product photo or poster over 3 or 6 posts to build excitement.

Event announcements

Turn an event poster into a grid so it stands out on your profile.

Portfolios

Show a wide landscape or panorama across a row of 3 posts.

Brand campaigns

Plan a puzzle-style feed where every row makes one picture.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Instagram Grid Maker

  1. Choose your photo

    Click "Click or drop a photo here", drop a picture on the box, or paste one with Ctrl+V. Tall photos suit 3 × 3 and 3 × 4 grids; wide photos suit 3 × 1.

  2. Pick the grid size

    In "Grid size" choose 3 × 1, 3 × 2, 3 × 3 or 3 × 4. Each row is 3 posts.

  3. Pick the tile shape

    In "Tile shape" choose Portrait 4:5 (1080 × 1350) or Square 1:1 (1080 × 1080). Portrait tiles fill more of the screen in the feed.

  4. Line up for the profile

    With portrait tiles, keep "Line up for the profile grid (3:4 thumbnails)" ticked. Profiles show portrait posts as slightly narrower 3:4 thumbnails, so the tool lets neighbouring tiles share the thin hidden strips and the picture joins up without gaps.

  5. Position the photo

    Drag the photo in the editor, use two fingers to zoom on a phone, or move the "Zoom" slider. The darker strips at the sides are posted but hidden on the profile. The "Profile preview" shows the end result.

  6. Make the tiles

    Click "Make tiles". Download everything with "Download all as ZIP", or tap a single tile to save just that one, which is handy on a phone.

  7. Post in order

    Post tile 1 first. It is the bottom-right tile. Then post 2, 3 and so on. The top-left tile is posted last, because new posts appear at the top-left of your profile.

Worked example: a 3 × 3 poster grid

Mia has a 3000 × 4000 px poster for her bakery opening. She chooses 3 × 3 and Portrait 4:5, keeps "Line up for the profile grid" ticked and drags the poster down a little so the logo sits in the middle tile. She clicks "Make tiles" and downloads bakery-grid-tiles.zip with nine 1080 × 1350 JPGs. She posts bakery-post-1-of-9.jpg first and bakery-post-9-of-9.jpg last, and her profile shows the whole poster.

Tips and tricks

  • Keep faces and text away from the lines between tiles, where the picture is cut.
  • Use a photo at least 3240 px wide for 1080 px tiles; smaller photos work but look softer.
  • Post all tiles in one session so other posts do not land in the middle of your grid.
  • Write captions in advance; each tile is a separate post with its own caption.
  • Platforms change how grids are shown from time to time. Check the profile preview against your own profile after posting the first row.

Problems and how to fix them

My grid looks shifted on the profile

Make sure you posted the tiles in number order, starting with 1, and that no other post was added in between.

There are small breaks between portrait tiles

Tick "Line up for the profile grid (3:4 thumbnails)". Without it, each tile loses a thin strip at both sides on the profile.

The tiles look blurry

Your photo is smaller than the grid. Use a larger original, or zoom out to 100%.

My iPhone photo will not open

HEIC photos cannot be read by every browser. Export the photo as JPG first, or take a screenshot of it.

Questions and answers

In which order do I post the tiles?

Post tile 1 first: it is the bottom-right one. The top-left tile is posted last, because each new post appears at the top-left of your profile. The file names are numbered for you.

Should I use 4:5 or square tiles?

Portrait 4:5 tiles (1080 × 1350) fill more of the screen in the feed and suit the taller profile grid. Square 1:1 tiles (1080 × 1080) are the classic look.

What does "Line up for the profile grid" do?

Profiles show 4:5 posts as slightly narrower 3:4 thumbnails, hiding about 34 px at each side. This option makes neighbouring tiles share those hidden strips, so the picture joins up without breaks.

Is my photo uploaded?

No. The photo is cut into tiles in your browser and never leaves your device.

Is this an official Instagram tool?

No. It is an independent free tool that makes images for Instagram; it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Instagram or Meta.
