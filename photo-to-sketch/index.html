What is Photo to Sketch?

Photo to Sketch turns photos into drawings and paintings. Pick one of six effects, move two sliders to get the look you want, and download the result at full size.

Pencil sketch and colour pencil look hand-drawn, cartoon adds bold outlines and flat colours, oil painting creates brush-like patches, posterize reduces the picture to a few bold colours, and line art leaves only clean black lines.

The effects run in your browser, so your photo is never uploaded and the tool works offline once the page is open.

What you can use it for

Gifts and cards

Turn a family photo into a sketch for a birthday card or frame.

Profile pictures

Stand out with a cartoon or pencil version of your photo.

Colouring pages

Line art turns any photo into outlines that children can colour in.

Art reference

See the main shapes and shadows of a photo before you draw or paint it.

Posters and covers

Posterize gives a bold, printed look for posters and album covers.

Social media

Make eye-catching versions of everyday photos.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Photo to Sketch

  1. Open your photo

    Click "Click or drop a photo here" and choose a JPG, PNG or WebP photo.

  2. Pick an effect

    Press Pencil sketch, Colour pencil, Cartoon, Oil painting, Posterize or Line art. The preview updates straight away.

  3. Adjust the sliders

    The two sliders change with each effect, for example "Darkness" and "Line softness" for pencil, or "Brush size" and "Colour levels" for oil painting. "Effect amount" mixes the effect with the original photo.

  4. Compare

    Drag across the picture, or use the slider under it, to see the original on the left and the effect on the right.

  5. Download

    Choose JPG, PNG or WebP in "Save as" and press "Download artwork". Big photos take a few seconds.

  6. Start again

    "Reset sliders" puts the sliders back to the middle.

Features

  • Six effectsPencil sketch, colour pencil, cartoon, oil painting, posterize and line art.
  • Two sliders per effectLabels change to match the effect, so you know what each one does.

Worked example: a pencil portrait for a card

Omar wants a pencil drawing of his grandmother for a birthday card. He opens a 3000 × 4000 portrait and picks Pencil sketch. The default looks a little light, so he moves "Darkness" to 70 and "Line softness" to 40 for finer lines. He drags across the picture to compare, then saves a JPG. Because the photo is 12 megapixels, it is saved at about 2449 × 3266 px, plenty for a printed card.

Tips and tricks

  • Photos with a plain background give the cleanest sketches and line art.
  • For line art colouring pages, raise "Line strength" and lower "Detail" for fewer, bolder lines.
  • Oil painting looks best with a large "Brush size" on landscapes and flowers.
  • Lower "Effect amount" to about 60% for a subtle artistic look that keeps the photo's colours.
  • Save as PNG for line art you will print or colour, so lines stay sharp.

Problems and how to fix them

The sketch is too light or too dark

Move the first slider ("Darkness" or "Line strength"), and try "Line softness" or "Detail".

The download is smaller than my photo

Photos over about 8 megapixels are saved at about 8 megapixels so phones do not run out of memory.

The cartoon has too many lines

Lower "Line strength" and raise "Colour smoothness".

My iPhone photo will not open

Convert HEIC photos to JPG first with the HEIC to JPG tool.

Questions and answers

Which effects are there?

Pencil sketch, colour pencil, cartoon, oil painting, posterize and line art. Each has two sliders, and "Effect amount" blends with the original photo.

Does it use AI?

No. The effects are classic image filters running in your browser, so they are fast and work offline.

What size is the result?

The same size as your photo, up to about 8 megapixels. Larger photos are saved at about 8 megapixels so phones do not run out of memory.

Which photos work best?

Clear, well-lit photos with a simple background. Portraits, pets and buildings look great as sketches and line art.

Is my photo uploaded?

No. Everything happens on your device.
