    What is Readability Checker?

    The Readability Checker tells you how easy your writing is to read. Paste an essay, article, report or web page text and it calculates six standard readability scores: Flesch Reading Ease, Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, Gunning Fog, SMOG, Coleman-Liau and the Automated Readability Index.

    It also highlights your text so you can see what to fix: long sentences in yellow and red, hard words of three or more syllables underlined, possible passive voice in purple and adverbs in blue. Plain-English tips explain what to change.

    It is useful for students checking essays, writers and bloggers aiming at a general audience, and anyone writing instructions, letters or health information that must be easy to follow. Your text never leaves your browser.

    What you can use it for

    Essays and assignments

    Make sure your writing is clear and not padded with long words and long sentences.

    Blog posts and web pages

    Aim for a Flesch score of 60 or more so most visitors read to the end.

    Business writing

    Check emails, reports and proposals before you send them.

    Health and public information

    Patient leaflets and public notices are often aimed at a reading age of 11 to 13.

    Learning English

    See which of your sentences are too long and practise splitting them.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Readability Checker

    1. Paste your text

      Paste or type into the box. Scores appear immediately and update as you edit. Click Try a sample text to see how it works.

    2. Read the headline scores

      Flesch Reading Ease runs from about 0 to 100; higher is easier. Average grade level combines five grade-level formulas into one US school grade, with the matching age.

    3. Check the counts

      Words, sentences, paragraphs, words per sentence, syllables per word and the share of hard words explain why the scores are high or low.

    4. Compare the formulas

      The table shows each score and what it means. Formulas that count syllables and those that count letters can differ by a grade or two.

    5. Find the problem sentences

      In the highlighted text, sentences of 21 to 30 words are yellow and those over 30 are red. On a computer, hover over a sentence to see its word count.

    6. Look at words and phrases

      Hard words are underlined. Purple marks possible passive voice such as "was found by", and blue marks adverbs ending in -ly. Untick any highlight type to hide it.

    7. Apply the tips

      Follow the suggestions under Tips to make it clearer, edit your text in the box, and watch the scores improve.

    Example: improving one sentence

    The sample text includes this 60-word sentence: "It was found by researchers that students who planned their essays were considerably more likely to finish them before the deadline, and that the quality of their arguments was generally judged to be significantly higher..." It is shown in red, with "was found" and "was generally judged" in purple. Rewriting it as "Researchers found that students who planned their essays were more likely to finish on time. Examiners also rated their arguments higher." removes both passives, two adverbs and the red sentence, and the Flesch score rises.

    Tips and tricks

    • Aim for an average of 15 to 20 words per sentence, and vary the length.
    • Swap long words for short ones where the meaning is the same: use for utilise, help for facilitate, start for commence.
    • Use active voice by default: say who did what.
    • Paste at least 100 words; scores on a few sentences can swing a lot.
    • Do not chase a perfect score. Technical words are fine when your readers need them; explain them once.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The scores look very different from each other

    Each formula measures different features. Use the average grade level and look at what the highlights show.

    A word's syllables look wrong

    On a computer, hover over an underlined word to see its count. English spelling has exceptions, so a few words will be off by one, which barely changes the scores.

    My heading joined the next sentence

    Put headings on their own line. A line without a full stop that ends with a line break is treated as a separate sentence.

    A passive phrase was not marked, or something else was

    Detection looks for forms of "be" followed by a past participle, so it is a guide. Questions like "Was it written..." and adjectives such as "tired" can be missed or marked.

    Questions and answers

    What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?

    Higher is easier. 60 to 70 is plain English that most teenagers and adults read comfortably. Academic writing is often 30 to 50, and anything below 30 is very hard going.

    Why do the grade levels differ?

    Each formula measures something slightly different. Flesch-Kincaid and SMOG count syllables, Coleman-Liau and ARI count letters, and Gunning Fog counts long words. Looking at the average gives a balanced picture.

    How are syllables counted?

    With rules for English spelling plus a list of tricky words. Checked against a pronouncing dictionary it agrees on about 97 out of 100 words, which is accurate enough for the formulas.

    Is passive voice always wrong?

    No. It is useful when the person doing the action is unknown or unimportant. The tool marks possible passives, such as "was written", so you can decide whether an active sentence would be clearer.

    Is my essay uploaded?

    No. Everything is worked out in your browser and your text is never sent anywhere.
