What is Before and After Slider?

The Before and After Slider puts two images on top of each other with a divider you can drag across, so the difference between them is easy to see. It is the classic way to show a photo edit, a renovation, a makeover, a cleaning job or a design change.

Add your two images, set the labels and the direction, and try the slider right away. Then export it the way you need: as an HTML snippet with both images built in that you can paste into a website or blog, as one side-by-side image, or as an animated GIF or video in which the divider sweeps across by itself.

Everything is made in your browser. Your images are not uploaded, and the embed code needs no account, no plugin and no other files.

What you can use it for

Photo editing and retouching

Show the original and the edited photo in a portfolio or a tutorial.

Renovation and cleaning

Builders, decorators, gardeners and cleaners can show their work on a website or social media.

Beauty, fitness and dental results

Present a before and after transformation in one interactive picture (with the person's permission).

Product and design changes

Compare an old and new logo, website design or packaging.

Maps and old photos

Compare an old photo with a modern one of the same place, or two satellite images.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Before and After Slider

  1. Add your two images

    Click or drop the first image on "Before" and the second on "After". You can also paste them with Ctrl+V, one after the other. "⇄ Swap images" switches them round. Press "Try with sample images" to see how it works.

  2. Set the labels and direction

    Change "Before" and "After" to any words in any language, or untick "Show labels on the images". "Slider direction" chooses a left and right or a top and bottom divider.

  3. Handle different sizes

    The first image sets the shape. If the second has a different shape, "Crop the second image to match" fills the frame, and "Fit the second image inside" shows all of it with bars.

  4. Try the slider

    Drag across the preview, or tab to it and use the arrow keys, Home and End.

  5. Embed it on a website

    Choose a width and press "Create embed code". Copy the code into an HTML or embed block on your website or blog, or press "Download .html page" for a page you can open or share on its own.

  6. Make an image, GIF or video

    Under "Side-by-side image", choose next to each other or one above the other and download. Under "Animated wipe", choose GIF or video, a width and a length, and press "Create animation" to get a looping wipe for social media or chat.

Worked example: a kitchen renovation for a website

Carlos, a builder, has a photo of a kitchen before his work and one after, taken from the same spot. He drops them into Before and After, keeps the labels and checks the slider. He picks a width of 800 px, presses "Create embed code" and pastes the code into an HTML block on his website, where visitors can drag the divider across the kitchen. He also makes a 480 px wide, 4-second GIF to post on social media.

Tips and tricks

  • Take both photos from the same spot, with the same zoom and in similar light, so the two images line up exactly.
  • Choose a smaller embed width to keep the code light; 800 px suits most blog layouts and still looks sharp on phones.
  • Some website builders limit the size of HTML blocks. If the code is refused, choose a smaller width.
  • GIFs get large quickly. Use 360 or 480 px for chat apps, and the video option for sharper, smaller files.
  • Always get permission before posting before and after photos of people.

Problems and how to fix them

The two images do not line up

They were taken from different positions or have different shapes. Crop both to the same area first with the Crop Image tool, then add them again.

My website will not accept the code

Some platforms remove scripts from posts. Use an HTML or custom code block, try a smaller width, or use the GIF or side-by-side image instead.

The video option is missing or fails

Some browsers, such as older Safari versions, cannot record video from a picture. Choose GIF instead.

The GIF is too big

Choose a smaller width or a shorter length, or use the video option.

My iPhone photo will not open

HEIC photos cannot be opened by most browsers. Convert them with the HEIC to JPG tool first.

Questions and answers

How do I put the slider on my website?

Press Create embed code, copy it and paste it into an HTML or custom code block. The code contains both images and a tiny script, so it needs no other files.

Are my images uploaded?

No. Everything is made in your browser. The images are only inside the code or files you download.

What if the two images are different sizes?

The first image sets the shape. The second is cropped to match, or fitted inside with bars if you prefer.

Can I share it on social media?

Yes. Make an animated GIF or a video in which the divider sweeps across by itself, or download a side-by-side image.

Does the slider work on phones?

Yes. It works with a finger, a mouse and the keyboard arrow keys.
