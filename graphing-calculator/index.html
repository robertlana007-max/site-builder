    What is Graphing Calculator?

    The Graphing Calculator draws up to six functions of x on one grid, so you can see their shape, where they cross the x-axis and where they meet each other. You type a function such as x^2 - 3 or sin(x), and the curve appears straight away.

    It is made for students checking homework, teachers preparing examples, and anyone who wants to see how a formula behaves. You can drag to move around, zoom with the mouse wheel, a pinch or the buttons, and hover or tap to read exact values.

    Everything is drawn in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

    What you can use it for

    Checking homework answers

    Plot the equation from your exercise and see whether the roots you worked out by hand match where the curve crosses the x-axis.

    Solving equations graphically

    Plot both sides of an equation, such as y = 2x + 1 and y = x^2 - 2, and find where they meet.

    Learning how functions change

    Compare x^2, 2x^2 and (x - 3)^2 side by side to see stretches and shifts.

    Trigonometry

    Draw sin(x), cos(x) and tan(x) together and see their periods, peaks and the gaps in tan(x).

    Exam revision

    Quickly sketch a cubic, a reciprocal or a logarithm to remember its shape before you draw it on paper.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Graphing Calculator

    1. Type your first function

      In the y₁ box type a function of x, for example x^2 - 4. Use ^ for powers, sqrt(x) for square roots and pi for π. You may leave out the times sign: 2x and 3(x + 1) work.

    2. Add more functions

      Press "+ Add function" to get another box, up to six. Each function has its own colour. Press the circle button to hide one for a moment, or × to remove it.

    3. Move and zoom

      Drag the graph to move it. Zoom with the mouse wheel, by pinching with two fingers on a phone, or with the + and − buttons. Press ⟲ to go back to the starting view.

    4. Read values

      Move the mouse over the graph, or tap it on a phone. A dot appears on each curve and the line under the graph shows x and every y value at that point.

    5. Find roots and intersections

      Press "Find roots & intersections". The tool lists every place in the current view where a curve crosses zero and where two curves meet, and marks them on the graph.

    6. Set an exact window

      Open "Set the viewing window" and type the x and y range you want, for example x from −2 to 6. Press "Apply window".

    7. Save the picture

      Press "Download PNG" to save the graph, with a small key showing each function, as an image you can put in notes or homework.

    Features

    • Up to six functionsEach in its own colour, with show, hide and remove buttons.
    • Clever drawingCurves are not joined across gaps, so tan(x) and 1/x show their asymptotes correctly, and sqrt(x) starts exactly at 0.
    • Roots and intersectionsFound to about seven significant figures in the visible area, including touching roots like x^2.
    • Touch friendlyDrag, pinch and tap work on phones and tablets.
    • Live value tracingSee x and every y value as you move across the graph.
    • PNG downloadA sharp, high-resolution picture of the graph with a legend.
    • Friendly inputImplicit multiplication (2x, 3sin(x)), brackets, pi, e, sqrt, abs, ln, log, exp and the trig functions.

    Worked example: where does a parabola meet a line?

    Type x^2 - 2 in y₁ and x in y₂. The parabola and the line appear. Press "Find roots & intersections". The list shows y₁ crossing zero at x = −1.414214 and x = 1.414214 (that is ±√2), and y₁ and y₂ meeting at (−1, −1) and (2, 2). You can check: at x = 2, x² − 2 = 2, the same as y = x. So the equation x² − 2 = x has the solutions x = −1 and x = 2.

    Tips and tricks

    • Use brackets to be clear: 1/(2x) is different from 1/2x, which this tool reads as (1/2)·x.
    • Write powers of trig functions as sin(x)^2, not sin^2(x).
    • Angles are in radians, as in all maths graphing. One full sine wave is 2π ≈ 6.28 wide.
    • Roots and intersections are only searched in the part you can see. Zoom out first if you expect more.
    • log(x) is base 10 and ln(x) is the natural logarithm. For log base 2 type ln(x)/ln(2).
    • On a phone, tap once to read values and drag with one finger to move.

    Problems and how to fix them

    My function shows a red message

    The message explains what is wrong, for example a missing bracket or an unknown name. Check that you used x as the variable and that every "(" has a ")".

    Nothing appears on the graph

    The curve may be outside the view. Press ⟲ to reset, zoom out, or set a bigger window. Also check that the circle button has not hidden it.

    A root I expected is missing

    If the curve only just touches the axis, or two roots are very close, zoom in on that area and press Find again.

    The page scrolls instead of moving the graph

    Put your finger or mouse on the graph itself. Outside the graph, the page scrolls as normal.

    Questions and answers

    What can I type?

    Use x as the variable with + − * / and ^, brackets, pi, e, sqrt, abs, ln, log (base 10), exp, sin, cos, tan and their inverses. You can write 2x or 3(x + 1) without a times sign.

    Are angles in degrees or radians?

    Radians, as in all function graphing. One full sine wave is 2π, about 6.28, wide.

    How are asymptotes handled?

    The graph checks for jumps between points and does not join the curve across a pole or a step, so tan(x), 1/x and floor(x) draw correctly.

    How accurate are the roots?

    Roots and intersections in the visible area are refined to about seven significant figures. Zoom in to separate roots that are very close together.

    Does it work on a phone?

    Yes. Drag with one finger to move, pinch to zoom and tap to read values.

    Is anything uploaded?

    No. Everything is drawn in your browser.
