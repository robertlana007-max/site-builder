What is Mic Test?

Mic Test checks that your microphone works and shows how it sounds. A live level meter, a sound wave and frequency bars move as you speak, and a red light warns you when the sound is so loud that it gets cut off (clipping).

You can pick between microphones, turn the browser's noise reduction on or off, and record a 5-second clip to play back and hear exactly how you sound to others.

The sound is analysed on your device and is never uploaded.

What you can use it for

Before a call or meeting

Make sure people will hear you on Zoom, Teams, Discord or Google Meet.

Find a mute problem

See whether the microphone picks up anything at all, or whether the wrong one is selected.

Set your volume

Adjust the input level so speech sits in the green and never reaches clipping.

Compare microphones

Switch between a headset, a USB mic and the laptop mic and listen to each recording.

Check a new microphone

Confirm it works and see its sample rate and channels.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Mic Test

  1. Press "Start microphone"

    Allow the microphone when your browser asks.

  2. Speak or clap

    The bar and the waves move. Normal speech should reach the green part; loud sounds the yellow.

  3. Watch for clipping

    If the CLIPPING light turns red, the sound is too loud. Move back or lower the input volume in your computer's sound settings.

  4. Choose a microphone

    After permission is given, the Microphone menu lists all inputs. Picking another restarts the test.

  5. Record 5 seconds

    Press "Record 5 seconds" and say something. The recording plays back when it ends, and you can download it.

  6. Stop

    Press Stop when you are done so the microphone turns off.

Worked example: a quiet headset

Priya's colleagues say she is too quiet. She opens Mic Test, presses Start microphone and speaks: the level only reaches about -45 dB. The Microphone menu shows the laptop mic was chosen instead of her headset. She picks the headset, the bar now reaches -18 dB, and her 5-second recording sounds clear.

Tips and tricks

  • Speak at your normal distance from the microphone while testing.
  • Aim for speech peaks around -20 to -10 dB and no clipping.
  • Turn on the noise reduction option to hear what a video call will do to your voice.
  • Use headphones when playing back the recording so you can hear small noises.
  • If you hear an echo in calls, it usually comes from speakers, not the microphone.

Problems and how to fix them

Nothing moves

Check the mute switch on your headset, choose the right microphone, and raise the input volume in your computer's sound settings.

"Microphone access was blocked"

Click the lock or microphone icon next to the address, allow the microphone and try again. On phones, also check the app permission in the phone settings.

The CLIPPING light keeps turning red

The input is too loud. Lower the microphone level in your sound settings or move further away.

The recording sounds robotic

Close other apps using the microphone and turn off any sound enhancements in your computer's settings.

Questions and answers

Is my voice uploaded?

No. The sound is analysed on your device and the recording is only saved if you download it.

What does clipping mean?

The sound is so loud that the top of the wave is cut off, which sounds distorted. Lower the input volume or move back.

What level is good?

Normal speech should peak around -20 to -10 dB, in the green, without the CLIPPING light.

Why does nothing move?

The microphone may be muted, the wrong one may be selected, or its input volume may be at zero in your sound settings.

Should I turn on noise reduction?

Leave it off to hear your microphone as it is. Turn it on to hear what a video call does to your voice.
