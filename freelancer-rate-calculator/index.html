What is Freelancer Rate Calculator?

The Freelancer Rate Calculator works backwards from the money you want to take home to the rate you need to charge. It includes the things employees never see: unpaid admin time, holidays, business costs, tax, platform fees and payment fees.

You get an hourly rate, a day rate and monthly and yearly billing targets, plus a breakdown of where every unit you bill goes. A project tool turns estimated hours into a fixed price.

What you can use it for

Set your first rate

Start freelancing with a rate that covers real costs.

Raise your prices

See how much more you need after costs rise.

Compare platforms

See how a 20% fee changes the rate versus direct clients.

Quote a fixed-price project

Turn hours into a price with a margin for risk.

Plan time off

See the cost of extra weeks of holiday.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Freelancer Rate Calculator

  1. Set your income goal

    Choose the currency, type the take-home income you want and choose per year or per month.

  2. Enter your time

    Type the hours you can bill each week, weeks off per year for holidays and sickness, and how many hours make a working day.

  3. Add costs and tax

    Type yearly business costs such as software and equipment, and the share of profit you expect to pay in tax and social charges.

  4. Pick the platform

    Choose where you find clients. The platform fee fills in, and you can change it. Add payment fees for bank or wallet charges.

  5. Add a buffer

    A safety buffer covers quiet months and late payments.

  6. Read your rates

    See the hourly rate, day rate and monthly and yearly targets. The table shows how the money is split.

  7. Price a project

    Type the estimated hours and a risk margin to get a fixed price to quote.

Worked example: 50,000 take-home

You want 50,000 a year, bill 25 hours a week and take 6 weeks off (1,150 billable hours). With 3,000 of costs, 25% tax, no platform fee, 3% payment fees and a 10% buffer, you need to bill about 79,003 a year, so about 68.70 an hour or about 550 for an 8-hour day. On a platform with a 20% fee the hourly rate rises to about 85.87.

Tips and tricks

  • Be honest about billable hours; most freelancers bill far fewer than 40 a week.
  • Raise rates for new clients first, then for existing ones with notice.
  • Quote projects by value when you can, and use this rate as your floor.
  • Revisit your rate every year as costs and skills grow.

Problems and how to fix them

The results show a dash

Billable hours must be above 0, weeks off below 52, and tax and fees below 100%.

The rate seems too high

Check the hours: few billable hours or many weeks off raise the rate. That is the real cost of freelancing.

The platform changed to Other

That happens when you type a fee that does not match a preset.

Questions and answers

How is the rate calculated?

It starts from your take-home goal, adds tax on profit and your business costs, then grosses up for platform and payment fees and a safety buffer. That total is divided by your billable hours in a year.

What are billable hours?

Hours you can charge a client for. Most freelancers bill 20 to 30 hours a week, because admin, sales and learning are unpaid.

Are the platform fees exact?

They are typical figures (for example Fiverr 20%, Upwork around 10%) and platforms change them. Check your platform and type the exact fee.

Should I include tax?

Yes. Type an estimate of income tax plus any self-employed social charges as a share of profit. Rules differ by country.

How do I price a fixed project?

Type the estimated hours in Price a project. The tool multiplies by your rate and adds a margin for risk and changes.
