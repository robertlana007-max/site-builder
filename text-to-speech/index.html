What is Text to Speech?

Text to Speech reads any text aloud using the voices built into your browser and device. Paste an article, an email, homework or a story, choose a voice, and listen while each word is highlighted.

It helps people who prefer listening to reading, language learners who want to hear pronunciation, writers who want to proofread by ear, and anyone with tired eyes or reading difficulties.

It uses the Web Speech feature of your browser, so the voices you see depend on your device. Browsers do not let websites record these voices, so the speech can be played but not saved as an audio file.

What you can use it for

Listen to articles and emails

Paste a long text and listen while you do something else.

Proofread your writing

Hearing your text read back makes missing words and clumsy sentences easy to notice.

Learn pronunciation

Pick a voice for the language you are learning and slow the speed down.

Reading support

Follow the highlighted words to help with dyslexia, tired eyes or long documents.

Practise for a talk

Hear how your speech sounds and roughly how long it takes.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Text to Speech

  1. Add your text

    Type or paste text into "Text to read aloud". Below, "Words" counts the words and "Listening time" estimates how long reading will take at the current speed.

  2. Choose a voice

    Open the "Voice" menu. Voices are grouped by language, with your own language at the top. Voices marked (online) come from Google or Microsoft and need an internet connection; the others run on your device. The tool remembers your choice.

  3. Set speed, pitch and volume

    Move the "Speed" slider from 0.5× (slow) to 2× (fast), "Pitch" from low to high, and "Volume" from 0 to 100%. Changes apply from the next sentence.

  4. Press "Play"

    The text box turns into a reading view. The sentence being read is shaded and, with most voices, the current word is highlighted. The view scrolls by itself.

  5. Pause, resume or jump

    Press "Pause" to stop for a moment and "Resume" to continue. Click any sentence in the reading view to jump to it.

  6. Stop and edit

    Press "Stop" to end reading and get the text box back so you can change the text.

Features

  • Voices for many languagesEvery voice on your device, grouped by language, with your choice remembered.

  • Speed, pitch and volumeFine control with sliders, from slow and clear to quick listening.

Worked example: proofreading a cover letter

Priya pastes her 320-word cover letter. The stats show 320 words and about 2 min. She picks "Samantha" in the English (United States) group and sets Speed to 1.2×. She presses Play and follows the highlight. In the third paragraph she hears "I am a experienced" and presses Stop, fixes it to "an experienced", then presses Play again and clicks the first sentence of the third paragraph to jump straight to it.

Tips and tricks

  • Try several voices: online voices often sound more natural, while local voices work offline and highlight every word.
  • Use 0.8× speed for difficult texts or a new language, and 1.5× or faster to get through long articles.
  • Add full stops and paragraph breaks; voices pause at them, which makes listening easier.
  • Numbers, dates and abbreviations are read the way the voice understands them, so write out anything important in words.
  • To keep spoken audio as a file, you need a dedicated text-to-speech app or service; a browser cannot record these voices.

Problems and how to fix them

It says no voices are installed

Your device has no speech voices. On Windows add them in Settings › Time & language › Speech; on Android install or update "Speech Services by Google"; on Linux install speech-dispatcher and espeak-ng. Chrome and Edge usually include voices.

Reading stops after a while

Some voices stop when the screen locks or the tab is in the background. Keep the page open and in front, then press Play again.

The word highlight does not move

That voice does not report which word it is saying, which is common for online voices. The current sentence is still shaded. Choose a voice without (online) for word-by-word highlighting.

Pause does not work on my phone

Some phone browsers cannot pause speech. Press Resume and the tool restarts the current sentence.

The voice sounds wrong for my language

Pick a voice from your language's group. An English voice reading French text will sound strange.

Questions and answers

Can I download the speech as an MP3?

No. Browsers play these voices through your device's speech system and do not let websites record them, so no browser tool can save them as a file. Sites that offer a download make the audio on their own servers from your text.

Why are the voices different on each device?

The voices come from your browser and operating system. Chrome includes Google voices, Edge includes Microsoft voices, and Safari uses the voices of your Mac, iPhone or iPad.

Which languages are supported?

Any language your device has a voice for. The voice menu is grouped by language, with your own language first.

Is my text sent anywhere?

Voices marked (online) are made by Google or Microsoft, so the browser sends the text to them to speak it. Other voices run fully on your device.

Why does the highlight not move with some voices?

Some voices, especially online ones, do not report which word they are saying. The current sentence is still highlighted.

Is there a length limit?

No. Long text is read sentence by sentence, which also avoids the cut-off some browsers have after about 15 seconds.
