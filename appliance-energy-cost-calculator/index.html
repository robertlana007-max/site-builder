What is Appliance Energy Cost Calculator?

The Appliance Energy Cost Calculator shows how much electricity each of your appliances uses and what it costs to run per day, month and year. List your devices with their watts, how many you have, hours per day and days per week, type your price per kWh, and see the total and which appliance uses the most.

Quick-add buttons put in typical wattages for common appliances, from LED bulbs and fans to air conditioners and water heaters, and every value can be edited.

What you can use it for

Lower your bill

Find the appliances costing you the most.

Before you buy

Compare the running cost of an old and a new appliance.

Solar and backup planning

Get your daily kWh for solar or UPS sizing.

Shared homes

Split costs fairly by usage.

Teaching

Show how watts, hours and kWh relate.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Appliance Energy Cost Calculator

  1. Enter your price

    Type the price per kWh from your bill and choose your currency.

  2. Add appliances

    Press a quick-add button or "Add appliance" and type the name and watts.

  3. Set how you use them

    Fill in how many, hours a day and days a week.

  4. Read each line

    The monthly cost of each appliance appears with a bar showing its share.

  5. Read the totals

    See energy and cost per day, month and year, and the biggest user.

  6. Copy the table

    Press "Copy table" to paste it elsewhere.

Worked example: a small home

Six 10 W LED bulbs for 6 hours, two 75 W fans for 10 hours, a fridge averaging 150 W all day and a 100 W TV for 4 hours use 5.86 kWh a day. At 0.15 per kWh that is 0.88 a day, 26.74 a month and 320.84 a year; the fridge is the biggest user at 61%.

Tips and tricks

  • Check the rating plate for real watts.
  • Fridges and ACs cycle on and off, so their average watts are lower than the label.
  • Standby power adds up; include chargers and TVs on standby.
  • Use the average price from your bill if you have slabs.

Problems and how to fix them

The total is higher than my bill

Some appliances run less than you think. Lower hours or average watts for fridges and ACs.

It says a day has only 24 hours

Hours a day must be 24 or less.

Costs show a dash

Enter your electricity price per kWh.

Questions and answers

How is the cost worked out?

kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. Cost = kWh × your price per kWh.

Where do I find the watts?

On the label or rating plate, usually on the back or bottom, or in the manual.

Why does my fridge or AC use less than shown?

They switch on and off by themselves, so they often run only 30–60% of the time. Lower the hours to match.

Is my list saved?

It stays in this browser if remembering inputs is on in Settings. Nothing is uploaded.
