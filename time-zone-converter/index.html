    What is Time Zone Converter?

    The Time Zone Converter shows one moment in many places at once. Pick a date, a time and the time zone it belongs to, and every zone in your list shows its local time, date, UTC offset and how far ahead or behind it is.

    It is made for people who work, study or keep in touch across borders: planning a call between Lahore, Dubai and London, catching a live match in another country, or checking when a deadline in New York falls for you.

    Daylight saving time is worked out from the time zone rules built into your browser for the exact date you choose, so March and November in the US or March and October in Europe are always right. Nothing is sent to a server.

    What you can use it for

    Scheduling meetings

    Find a time that works for colleagues in Karachi, Berlin and Chicago before you send the invite.

    Calling family abroad

    Check whether it is a reasonable hour for relatives in Saudi Arabia, the UK or Australia before you ring.

    Online events and live sport

    Convert a webinar, exam or match start time from the organiser's zone into yours.

    Travel planning

    See arrival times in local time and spot when a flight lands on the next day.

    Deadlines

    Work out exactly when a deadline given in US Eastern or UTC falls where you live.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Time Zone Converter

    1. Start from now or pick a time

      When the page opens, the live clock shows the current time everywhere and ticks every second. To convert a different moment, change the Date or Time box; the live clock stops and the list shows your chosen time.

    2. Choose the source time zone

      Use "In this time zone" to say where your time belongs. Your own zone is first, then popular zones, then every time zone your browser knows.

    3. Read the list

      The highlighted row is your source time. Every other row shows the local time, the date, the UTC offset (for example UTC+5 or UTC−4), the zone abbreviation where there is one, and the difference from the source, such as +4h or −9h 30m.

    4. Watch for +1 day and −1 day

      A small +1 day tag means that zone has already moved to the next date; −1 day means it is still the previous date.

    5. Add and remove zones

      Type a city, country or zone name in "Add a time zone", for example Toronto, Nepal or Asia/Kathmandu, and click a result to add it. Click × on any row to remove it. Your list is remembered on this device; Reset zone list brings back the defaults.

    6. Daylight saving edge cases

      If you pick a time that is skipped when clocks jump forward, the tool says so and shows the same moment on the new clock time. If you pick a time that happens twice when clocks go back, it shows the first one and offers a tick box to use the second.

    7. Copy and share

      Click Copy summary to copy one line per zone with time, date and offset, ready to paste into an email or chat. Switch Clock to 12-hour if you prefer AM and PM.

    Worked example: a call from New York during the US-Europe gap

    You want a call at 12:00 noon in New York on Friday 20 March 2026. Choose US & Canada Eastern and enter 2026-03-20 and 12:00. The US moved its clocks forward on 8 March, so New York is UTC−4. The UK does not change until 29 March, so London is still UTC+0 and shows 16:00, a 4-hour gap instead of the usual 5. Germany shows 17:00 (UTC+1), Pakistan 21:00 (UTC+5), India 21:30 (UTC+5:30) and Sydney shows 03:00 with a +1 day tag, because it is already Saturday morning there.

    Tips and tricks

    • Always set the date, not just the time: the gap between two zones can change by an hour around daylight saving changes.
    • The US and Europe change their clocks on different weekends, so for a few weeks in March and in late October or early November the usual gap is one hour shorter.
    • Pakistan, India, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, China, Japan and Bangladesh do not use daylight saving, so their offsets stay the same all year.
    • Search by country if you do not know the city: try "Brazil", "Nepal" or "Qatar".
    • Use 12-hour mode if the people you send the summary to are used to AM and PM.

    Problems and how to fix them

    My own time zone shows the wrong time

    The converter trusts your device's time zone setting. Check the date, time and time zone in your phone or computer settings.

    The tool says my time does not exist

    That date is when the clocks jump forward in your chosen zone, so that hour is skipped. Pick a time an hour later, or use the moment shown.

    I cannot find a city

    Not every city has its own entry. Search for the nearest big city in the same country, or the country name.

    The live clock stopped

    Editing the date or time stops it so you can convert a fixed moment. Press Now to start it again.

    Questions and answers

    Does it handle daylight saving time?

    Yes. The converter uses the time zone rules built into your browser, so changes like the US clocks moving in March and November or Europe in March and October are applied for the exact date you pick.

    What happens with a time that does not exist?

    When the clocks jump forward, an hour is skipped, for example 2:30 AM in New York on the second Sunday of March. The tool tells you and shows the same moment on the new clock time.

    What about a time that happens twice?

    When the clocks go back, one hour happens twice. The tool shows the first one and lets you switch to the second.

    What does +1 day mean?

    It means that in that zone it is already the next calendar day. −1 day means it is still the previous day.

    Is my zone list saved?

    Yes. The zones you add or remove are remembered in this browser on this device, so the list is ready next time.
