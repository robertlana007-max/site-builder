What is Profit Margin Calculator?

The Profit Margin Calculator shows how much you make on a sale. Type what an item costs you and what you sell it for, and it shows the profit, the profit margin and the markup. A second section works backwards: give it a cost and a target margin and it tells you the price to charge.

It is made for small business owners, online sellers, freelancers and students learning business maths. It also makes the difference between margin and markup clear, because the two are shown side by side.

The maths runs in your browser as you type. Results are estimates and not financial advice.

What you can use it for

Price a new product

Use "Find the price for a target margin" to get a selling price that gives the margin you want.

Check a current price

See the real margin on items you already sell.

Compare suppliers

Enter different costs to see how a cheaper supplier changes your margin.

Explain margin vs markup

See both numbers for the same sale to understand the difference.

Plan a sale

Enter the discounted price to check you still make a profit.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Profit Margin Calculator

  1. Enter the cost

    In the first section, type what one item costs you in "Cost", for example 60. A cost of 0 is allowed. The tool starts with an example of cost 60 and price 100.

  2. Enter the selling price

    Type the price the customer pays in "Selling price", for example 100. It must be above 0.

  3. Read profit, margin and markup

    "Profit" is price minus cost. "Profit margin" is profit as a percent of the price. "Markup" is profit as a percent of the cost; it shows a dash when the cost is 0.

  4. Find a price for a target margin

    Scroll to "Find the price for a target margin". Type the item’s cost in its "Cost" box and the margin you want in "Target margin (%)".

  5. Read the suggested price

    The box below shows "Sell at" and the price. The target must be from 0 to less than 100%, otherwise a message explains the limit.

Features

  • ProfitProfit = selling price − cost.
  • Profit marginMargin = profit ÷ selling price × 100. It tells you how much of each sale is profit.
  • MarkupMarkup = profit ÷ cost × 100. It tells you how much you added on top of the cost.
  • Target price formulaPrice = cost ÷ (1 − target margin ÷ 100). For a 40% margin you divide the cost by 0.6.
  • Two separate sectionsThe target price section has its own cost box, so you can use both parts at once.
  • Negative marginsIf the price is below the cost, the profit and margin show as negative numbers, so you can see a loss.

Worked example: cost 60, price 100

An item costs you 60 and you sell it for 100. The "Profit" is 40.00. The "Profit margin" is 40 ÷ 100 = 40.00%, and the "Markup" is 40 ÷ 60 = 66.67%. Now you have a new item that costs 45 and you want a 40% margin. Type 45 and 40 in the target section and the tool shows "Sell at 75.00", because 45 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = 75.

Tips and tricks

  • Do not confuse margin and markup: a 50% markup is only a 33.33% margin.
  • Include shipping, packaging and platform fees in the cost if you want a truer margin.
  • This is gross margin; rent, wages and other overheads are not included.
  • A margin can never reach 100%, because that would need a cost of 0 and a price above 0.
  • Use the target section to set prices before you list a product.

Problems and how to fix them

The first results show a dash and a red message

Fill in "Cost" with 0 or more and enter a "Selling price" above 0. The message tells you which to fix.

Markup shows a dash

Markup cannot be calculated when the cost is 0, because you cannot divide by 0.

It says the margin must be from 0 to less than 100%

Your target margin is 100 or more, or negative. Enter a value like 40.

The target section shows a dash or asks for a cost

The cost in that section must be above 0 and the target margin box must not be empty.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Markup is profit as a share of the cost. A product that costs 60 and sells for 100 has a 40% margin and a 66.67% markup.

How do I price for a target margin?

Divide the cost by 1 minus the margin as a decimal. For a 40% margin on a cost of 60: 60 ÷ 0.6 = 100.

Is this gross or net margin?

It depends on what you include in cost. Using only the cost of the goods gives gross margin.
