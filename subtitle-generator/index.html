What is Subtitle Generator?

Subtitle Generator listens to the speech in a video or audio file and writes it down as timed captions. You get a list of subtitles with start and end times that you can check, correct and save as an SRT or VTT subtitle file or as plain text.

It uses Whisper, an open-source speech recognition model made by OpenAI, running inside your browser through WebAssembly. The model is downloaded once and kept by your browser, and your file is never uploaded anywhere.

Whisper understands about 100 languages and can detect the spoken language by itself. It can also translate speech from other languages into English subtitles.

What you can use it for

Social media videos

Add captions to Reels, Shorts and TikToks so people can follow without sound, and burn them into the picture.

YouTube and course videos

Upload the SRT file to YouTube, Vimeo or your learning platform for accurate, editable captions.

Interviews and meetings

Get a timed transcript of a recording and download it as text.

Accessibility

Make your videos usable for people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

Language learning

See the words of a foreign-language clip, or translate them into English subtitles.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Subtitle Generator

  1. Choose your file

    Click the box or drop a video or audio file onto it: MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV, MP3, WAV, M4A and more. The tool reads the sound and shows the file in a player.

  2. Set the language

    Leave "Spoken language" on "Detect automatically", or pick the language yourself, which is more reliable for short clips and less common languages.

  3. Choose the accuracy

    "Fast" uses a small model (41 MB download) and suits clear speech. "Better" uses a larger model (77 MB) that makes fewer mistakes, especially in languages other than English, but takes about twice as long.

  4. Pick the caption style

    Under "Subtitles in" keep the spoken language, or choose English to translate. "Caption length" sets how long each caption can be: one short line, up to two lines, or whole sentences.

  5. Generate

    Press "Generate subtitles". The first time, the model downloads with a progress bar. Captions then appear while it listens, with the time remaining. Press "Stop" at any time; the captions so far are kept.

  6. Check and correct

    Play the video: the captions show in the player and the current one is highlighted in the list. Click into any caption to fix words or times, delete a caption with ×, add one with "+ Add caption", or move all of them with "Shift all times".

  7. Download

    Press "Download SRT" (works almost everywhere), "Download VTT" (for web players) or "Download text". "Copy text" copies the words without times.

  8. Burn into the video (optional)

    For videos, choose a text size and style under "Burn the subtitles into the video" and press "Make video with subtitles". You get an MP4 with the captions drawn on the picture.

Worked example: captions for a cooking video

Lucía has a 3-minute cooking video, receta.mp4, in Spanish. She drops it on the box, leaves the language on "Detect automatically", chooses "Better" accuracy and presses "Generate subtitles". After the one-time model download, the stats show "Spanish" and captions appear as it listens; it finishes in about a minute and a half with 41 captions. Playing the video, she notices "pimiento" was heard as "pimienta" in one caption, clicks into it and corrects it. She presses "Download SRT" and gets receta.es.srt for YouTube, then presses "Make video with subtitles" with Medium white text on a dark box to get receta-subtitled.mp4 for Instagram.

Tips and tricks

  • Clear audio gives the best captions: one speaker close to the microphone, little music or noise. Clean noisy recordings first with the Noise Remover.
  • For short clips or less common languages, choose the language yourself instead of Detect automatically.
  • Use the Better model for Hindi, Urdu, Arabic and other languages besides English, Spanish and French.
  • Leave the tab open and the device plugged in for long files; a computer is much faster than a phone.
  • Upload the SRT file to YouTube instead of burning captions in: viewers can turn them on or off and YouTube can translate them.
  • To get subtitles in another language than English, download the text and translate it with the Offline Translator.

Problems and how to fix them

Some words or names are wrong

That is normal for speech recognition, especially with names, technical words, noise or accents. Correct them in the caption list, or try the Better model.

The wrong language was detected

Choose the spoken language yourself in the list and press Generate subtitles again.

It is very slow

The model runs on your device's processor. Use the Fast model, close other tabs, or use a computer instead of a phone. You can stop and keep what is done so far.

It says it could not read the sound

The file may have no sound track, or use a sound format the browser cannot decode. For videos the tool tries its video engine; otherwise convert the file to MP3 first.

Making the burned-in video fails

Very long or 4K videos can be too much for the browser's memory. Trim the video first, or use the SRT file in a desktop video editor.

Questions and answers

Is my video uploaded?

No. The speech model is downloaded to your browser once and the recognition happens on your device, so your file never leaves it.

Which languages work?

Whisper understands about 100 languages, including English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Bengali, Chinese, Japanese, Indonesian and Swahili. Accuracy is best for widely spoken languages with clear audio; use Better accuracy for others.

How long does it take?

With the Fast model a typical computer needs about 10 to 30 seconds per minute of speech; Better is about twice as slow, and phones are slower. The first use also downloads the model (41 or 77 MB).

Can it translate the subtitles?

Yes, into English: set Subtitles in to English (translate). For other target languages, download the text and use the Offline Translator.

Can I put the subtitles into the video?

Yes. Make video with subtitles draws the captions onto the picture and saves an MP4. It re-encodes the whole video on your device, so it can take one to three times the video length.

Why are some words wrong?

Speech recognition makes mistakes with names, noise, music, strong accents and several people talking at once. Click into any caption to correct it before downloading.
