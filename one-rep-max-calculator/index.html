What is One Rep Max Calculator?

The One Rep Max Calculator estimates the most you could lift for one repetition, based on a heavier set you have already done. It uses seven published formulas: Epley, Brzycki, Lombardi, Lander, Mayhew et al., O'Conner et al. and Wathan.

It shows the average, the range across formulas and a table of training weights by percentage, with the reps most people can do at each.

What you can use it for

Plan a program

Many programs use percentages of your 1RM.

Track strength

Compare estimates over time without max testing.

Pick working weights

See what 75% or 85% of your max is.

Convert units

Switch between kg and lb.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use One Rep Max Calculator

  1. Choose units

    Pick kg or lb. A typed weight is converted.

  2. Enter your set

    Type the "Weight lifted" and "Reps done".

  3. Choose rounding

    Pick how to round training weights (for example 2.5 kg or 5 lb).

  4. Read the estimate

    See the average one-rep max, Epley, Brzycki and the range.

  5. Use the tables

    The training table lists 50–100% with typical reps; the formulas table shows every equation.

Features

  • Seven formulasEpley, Brzycki, Lombardi, Lander, Mayhew, O'Conner and Wathan.
  • Average and rangeSee how much the formulas agree.
  • Percentage table50% to 100% of 1RM with typical reps.
  • Plate roundingRound to 1, 2.5 or 5.
  • kg or lbDefaults to your region and converts when you switch.

Worked example

100 kg for 5 reps: Epley 100 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 116.7 kg, Brzycki 100 × 36 ÷ 32 = 112.5 kg, Lombardi 117.5 kg, Lander 113.7 kg, Mayhew 119.0 kg, O'Conner 112.5 kg and Wathan 116.6 kg. The average is about 115.5 kg, so 80% is about 92.5 kg.

Tips and tricks

  • Use a set taken close to failure with good form.
  • Sets of 3–6 reps give the most reliable estimates.
  • Re-estimate every few weeks as you get stronger.
  • Always warm up and use a spotter or safety bars for heavy lifts.

Problems and how to fix them

A message about more than 20 reps

Use a heavier weight for fewer reps.

Results look too high

Check the unit; kg entered as lb gives a smaller number and vice versa.

1 rep gives the same weight

One rep is already your max, so no formula is applied.

Questions and answers

What is a one-rep max?

The heaviest weight you can lift once with good form for a given exercise.

Which formula is most accurate?

Studies find they are all close for 10 reps or fewer. Epley and Brzycki are most common; the average of several is a good estimate.

Why limit reps to 20?

Above about 10 reps the formulas disagree more, and above 20 they are unreliable.

Is it safe to test a true 1RM?

Only with good technique, a proper warm-up and a spotter. An estimate avoids the risk of a max attempt.

What does rounding do?

It rounds the training weights to the nearest 1, 2.5 or 5 so they match the plates you have.
