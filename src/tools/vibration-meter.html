Calibrate

Put the phone on a solid table, screen up, nobody touching it, and press Calibrate. In 3 seconds the tool measures the sensor's own noise (removed from RMS) and checks gravity reads 9.81 m/s² (fixes the scale). Saved on this device.

Recorded sessions

Give each session a name, for example "Washing machine spin, before levelling". Sessions are saved only in this browser.

How to measure

Washing machineLay the phone flat on top during the spin. If it rocks or "walks", adjust the feet until they all touch the floor, then measure again and compare RMS.
Car at idlePut the phone on the seat or a flat part of the dashboard with the engine running and nothing else on. Compare with another day or another car. A rough or uneven idle shows as a jumpy graph.
Fan wobblePut the phone on the fan's base or, for a ceiling fan, on a firm spot on the housing only if it is safe and the fan is off when you place it. The main frequency usually matches the blade speed (rpm ÷ 60).

Feeling guide for people (based on ISO 2631-1, used for comfort in vehicles; phone readings are unweighted, so treat this as rough): below 0.32 m/s² not uncomfortable · 0.32–0.63 a little uncomfortable · 0.5–1 fairly uncomfortable · 0.8–1.6 uncomfortable · 1.25–2.5 very uncomfortable · above 2 extremely uncomfortable.

Limits: phones usually report motion only 50–100 times a second, so they can only see vibrations up to about half of that (25–50 Hz, about 1,500–3,000 rpm). Faster vibrations can show up at a wrong, lower frequency. A phone is not a calibrated vibration meter; use it to compare before and after.
