    What is Cron Expression Generator?

    The Cron Expression Generator helps you write and understand cron schedules, the short codes like 0 9 * * 1-5 that tell servers, CI pipelines and cloud schedulers when to run a job. You can build an expression with simple menus or paste one to get a plain-English explanation.

    Developers, system administrators, DevOps engineers and data teams use it to schedule backups, reports, emails and maintenance tasks without guessing. The list of the next five run times, in your own time zone or in UTC, confirms the schedule before you deploy it.

    It follows standard (Vixie) cron rules, also understands seconds and @ shortcuts, and runs in your browser.

    What you can use it for

    Schedule server jobs

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Cron Expression Generator

    1. Start with a preset

      Tap a preset like Every 15 minutes or Weekdays at 9:00 to fill in a common schedule.

    2. Or type an expression

      Type or paste five fields separated by spaces into Cron expression. The labels underneath show which field is which.

    3. Read the explanation

      The coloured box describes the schedule in plain English, or explains exactly what is wrong if the expression is not valid.

    4. Build step by step

      Under Build it step by step, choose for each field: Every, Every N, Specific values (tap the numbers or names), or From … to. The expression updates as you go.

    5. Check the next runs

      Next 5 runs lists the coming run times with how far away they are. Switch between My time and UTC.

    6. Copy it

      Press Copy expression and paste it into your crontab, pipeline file or scheduler.

    Worked example: business-hours checks

    You need a health check every 5 minutes during office hours on weekdays. Type */5 9-17 * * 1-5. The explanation reads "Every 5 minutes, between 09:00 and 17:59, on Monday through Friday." Next 5 runs shows the next weekday at 09:00, 09:05, 09:10 and so on. If your server runs in UTC and your office is in another zone, switch to UTC to see the times the server will use.

    Tips and tricks

    • Most servers run cron in UTC; convert your local times before scheduling.
    • Avoid scheduling many jobs at exactly 00:00; spread them out, for example at 03:17.
    • Do not set both day of month and day of week unless you want either one to trigger the job.
    • Jobs set between 02:00 and 03:00 can be skipped or run twice when daylight saving time changes in some zones; UTC avoids this.
    • Check the tool or service you use: some (like Quartz or certain cloud schedulers) use slightly different syntax.

    Problems and how to fix them

    It says the expression needs 5 fields

    Separate fields with single spaces. A sixth field at the start is read as seconds; a seventh (a year) is not standard.

    It says L, W or # are not supported

    Those are Quartz features. In standard cron, use a script check for "last day of the month" instead.

    The schedule never runs

    The date may not exist, like 30 February or 31 April. Check day of month and month.

    The builder shows Custom

    That field uses a pattern the menus cannot show, such as 9-17/2. Edit it in the expression box, or choose a simpler option.

    Questions and answers

    What do the five fields mean?

    In order: minute (0-59), hour (0-23), day of the month (1-31), month (1-12 or JAN-DEC) and day of the week (0-7 or SUN-SAT, where 0 and 7 are both Sunday). * means every value.

    What do */5, 1-5 and 1,15 mean?

    */5 means every 5th value (every 5 minutes), 1-5 is a range (Monday to Friday), and 1,15 is a list (the 1st and 15th). They can be combined, like 0-30/10.

    Why does my job run on more days than expected?

    When both day of the month and day of the week are set, standard cron runs the job when either one matches, not only when both do. The explanation points this out.

    Does it support seconds and @daily?

    Yes. Six fields are read as seconds first (as in many job libraries), and shortcuts like @hourly, @daily, @weekly, @monthly, @yearly and @reboot are explained. Quartz extras such as L, W and # are not part of standard cron and are flagged.

    Which time zone does cron use?

    The time zone of the server where it runs, often UTC. Switch Next 5 runs to UTC to check a server schedule, or to My time to see it in your local time.
