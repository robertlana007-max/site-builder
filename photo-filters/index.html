What is Photo Filters?

Photo Filters gives your pictures a new look in one tap. Choose from 13 filters: black and white, sepia, vintage, warm, cool, invert, duotone, noir, vivid, faded, bright, dramatic and forest.

Every filter has an intensity slider, and you can fine-tune brightness, contrast and saturation on top. Press and hold the preview to compare with the original. Apply the same look to up to 100 photos at once and download them as JPG, PNG or WebP.

All the work happens in your browser; your photos are not uploaded.

What you can use it for

Consistent social feeds

Apply one look to a whole set of photos.

Old-photo effect

Make pictures look vintage or sepia-toned for invitations and memories.

Black and white portraits

Create classic monochrome or high-contrast noir images.

Design work

Make duotone images in brand colours for posters and websites.

Quick fixes

Warm up a cold photo or brighten a dark one.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Photo Filters

  1. Add photos

    Click or drop one or more photos, or try the sample. With several photos, small pictures appear so you can switch between them.

  2. Pick a filter

    Tap a filter button. Each button shows a mini preview made from your own photo.

  3. Set the intensity

    Use the Intensity slider to blend the filter with the original. 100% is the full effect.

  4. Fine-tune

    Adjust Brightness, Contrast and Saturation. Press Reset adjustments to go back.

  5. Duotone colours

    With Duotone selected, pick the shadow and highlight colours.

  6. Compare

    Press and hold the picture to see the original.

  7. Download

    Choose JPG, PNG or WebP and the quality, then download the photo or all photos as a ZIP.

Worked example: a vintage set for an anniversary card

Grace has eight family photos for an anniversary card. She drops them all in and taps Vintage. The effect is a little strong, so she sets Intensity to 70% and Brightness to +10. She taps through the small pictures to check each one, then presses Download all (ZIP) and gets eight matching photos for the card.

Tips and tricks

  • Lower the intensity for a natural look; full strength suits artistic edits.
  • Duotone works best with a dark shadow colour and a light highlight colour.
  • Use PNG if your picture has see-through areas you want to keep.
  • Raise contrast slightly after Black & white for more punch.
  • Hold the picture often to make sure the filter is helping, not hiding detail.

Problems and how to fix them

The saved photo looks different from the preview

The preview is a smaller copy; the saved photo is full size, so fine detail can look a little different. The colours are the same.

A very large photo was scaled down

Photos over 24 megapixels or 8000 pixels are scaled down so the tool works on phones.

See-through areas became white

JPG cannot be see-through. Choose PNG or WebP.

Questions and answers

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Filters are applied in your browser and your photos stay on your device.

What does intensity do?

It blends the filter with your original photo. 100% is the full effect, 50% is half way, 0% is the original.

How do I make a duotone?

Choose Duotone, then pick a colour for the shadows and one for the highlights. Dark parts take the first colour and light parts the second.

Does it keep the full resolution?

Yes, up to 24 megapixels. Bigger photos are scaled down so they work on phones.

Can I apply one filter to many photos?

Yes. Drop up to 100 photos; the chosen filter and settings are used for all of them in Download all (ZIP).
