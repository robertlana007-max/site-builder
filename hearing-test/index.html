What is Hearing Test?

Hearing Test is a simple self-check that plays beeps at different pitches into each ear through headphones. The beeps get quieter until you stop hearing them, so you can see how well each ear picks up quiet sounds at low, middle and high pitches.

Results are shown in a chart and a table, and the tool points out big differences between your ears and pitches you could not hear at all. A separate test finds the highest pitch you can hear.

It is not a medical test: results depend on your headphones, device volume and the room, and are given relative to a reference tone rather than in clinical dB HL. If you notice hearing changes, see a doctor or hearing professional.

What you can use it for

Comparing your ears

See whether one ear hears quiet sounds less well.

High-pitch hearing

Find out how high you can hear, and how it compares with typical ranges for your age.

Checking headphones

Test whether left and right channels and high pitches play properly.

Before and after noise

Compare results before and after loud events (with the same headphones and volume).

Science lessons

Show how hearing changes with pitch and age.

Deciding to get a real test

Notice possible problems early and book a professional check.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Hearing Test

  1. Prepare

    Go to a quiet room, put on headphones and remove noise-cancelling transparency modes. Use "Play in LEFT ear" and "Play in RIGHT ear" to check the sides.

  2. Set the volume

    Press "Play the reference tone" and set your device volume so the tone is clear but comfortable. Do not change it during the test.

  3. Choose the test

    The Full check tests 7 pitches from 250 Hz to 8 kHz per ear; the Quick check tests 4. "Highest pitch" runs a rising tone.

  4. Respond to the beeps

    Press "I hear it" or the space bar each time you hear beeps, even faint ones. If you hear nothing, do nothing. Sometimes no beep is played on purpose.

  5. Highest pitch test

    Press "I can't hear it" the moment the rising tone disappears. It runs twice.

  6. Read the results

    The chart shows the right ear (O) and left ear (X). Higher on the chart means you heard quieter beeps. Use Copy results to keep a note.

Worked example: comparing both ears

Ben, 42, sometimes struggles to hear on the phone with his left ear. He puts on his headphones, checks left and right, sets the reference volume and runs the Full check. The chart shows both ears around −45 dB at 1 and 2 kHz, but the left ear only reaches −20 dB at 4 and 6 kHz while the right reaches −40 dB. The tool says the ears differ by 15 dB or more at those pitches, so Ben books a proper hearing test.

Tips and tricks

  • Use the same headphones and volume each time if you want to compare results over time.
  • Close windows and turn off fans; background noise hides quiet beeps.
  • Do not guess: only press when you really hear something.
  • Take the test when your ears have rested, not straight after a concert.
  • Wired headphones avoid the extra noise and volume changes some wireless ones add.

Problems and how to fix them

I hear the beeps in the wrong ear

Your headphones are the wrong way round, or a mono or balance setting is on in your device's accessibility settings.

I cannot hear the reference tone

Raise your device volume, check silent mode and that the headphones are connected.

Very high pitches are "not heard"

Many headphones cannot play above about 16 kHz, and high-pitch hearing naturally falls with age.

The test stopped

It stops when you leave the page, to keep the results fair. Press Start the test to begin again.

Questions and answers

Is this a real hearing test?

No. It is a rough self-check. A real hearing test (audiometry) uses calibrated equipment in a quiet booth and gives results in dB HL. This tool cannot diagnose hearing loss.

What do the numbers mean?

They show the quietest beeps you heard, in dB compared with the reference tone you set. −40 dB means you heard a beep 40 dB quieter than the reference. Lower (more negative) is better.

Why do I need headphones?

To test each ear separately and to keep the volume steady. Speakers let both ears hear the beeps.

What is the highest pitch test?

A tone slowly rises from 8 kHz. You press when it fades away. Young people often hear up to 17 to 20 kHz; the limit falls with age. Many headphones cannot play the highest pitches.

Why are my two ears different?

Small differences are normal and can come from headphone fit. Differences of 15 dB or more, especially if new, are worth checking with a hearing professional.

Is my data stored?

No. Results stay on screen only; nothing is uploaded or saved.
