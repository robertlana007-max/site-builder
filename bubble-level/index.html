What is Bubble Level?

Bubble Level turns your phone into a spirit level. Lay it on a shelf, table or floor to see how far the surface tilts in both directions, or stand it on its edge to straighten a picture frame, shelf or door post.

It shows the tilt in degrees, as a percentage slope and as a ratio, beeps or vibrates when the surface is level within the tolerance you choose, and can hold a reading so you can read it after taking the phone away.

Phone sensors are often a little off, so you can set zero on a surface you trust, or use the two-step calibration that needs no level surface. On computers without a tilt sensor, the built-in slope calculator works from measurements instead.

What you can use it for

Hanging pictures and mirrors

Stand the phone on the top of the frame and adjust until it beeps.

Shelves and furniture

Check that shelves, tables, beds and washing machines sit level.

DIY and building

Check tiles, paving, decking and fence rails, and read the slope in percent.

Ramps and drainage

Measure the fall of a path, gutter or ramp and compare it with the slope you need.

Camping and caravans

Level a caravan, camper van or tent platform before you set up.

Photography

Level a tripod head or the base of a camera.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Bubble Level

  1. Choose what you are levelling

    Pick Flat surface for shelves and tables, or Wall / picture to stand the phone on its edge against a frame or post.

  2. Start the level

    Press Start level. On iPhone and iPad, choose Allow when Safari asks about Motion and Orientation.

  3. Place the phone

    Take off a bumpy case if you can. Lay the phone screen-up, or stand it on its edge with the screen facing you.

  4. Read and adjust

    The bubble floats to the high side and the text tells you which side to raise. At level it turns green, beeps and vibrates.

  5. Hold the reading

    In a tight spot, press Hold reading, then take the phone out to read the angle.

  6. Calibrate once

    If a known-level surface does not read 0°, press Set zero there, or use Two-step calibration: measure, turn the phone around 180° on the same spot, measure again.

Worked example: a sloping shelf

Ana puts her phone on a new shelf and presses Start level. It reads 1.4°, 2.4% slope, left-right 1.4° (right high), and says "Raise the left side". She loosens the left bracket, packs it up a little and checks again: the bubble turns green at 0.2° and the phone beeps. She first did a two-step calibration on the kitchen table, which showed her phone was 0.6° off, so the reading she trusts is the corrected one.

Tips and tricks

  • Remove a thick or curved phone case: it can tilt the phone more than the surface does.
  • Check the camera bump: on some phones it rocks the phone. Lay the phone on a thin book or ruler if needed.
  • Do the two-step calibration once on a roughly flat table; it removes your sensor's own error.
  • Use High smoothing on a vibrating surface such as a running washing machine.
  • Percent slope is rise ÷ run × 100: a 2% fall drops 2 cm every metre.

Problems and how to fix them

Nothing happens when I press Start

On iPhone, Safari must be allowed to use Motion and Orientation. Close the tab, reopen the page and choose Allow. Computers usually have no tilt sensor; use the slope calculator.

A level table does not read 0°

Phone sensors are often a degree off. Use Set zero on a surface you trust, or the two-step calibration.

The reading jumps around

Choose High smoothing, keep the phone still, and do not hold it in your hand while measuring.

The left and right sides seem swapped

Readings follow how the screen is turned. If the screen did not rotate with the phone, turn screen rotation on or hold the phone upright.

Questions and answers

How accurate is a phone spirit level?

After calibration most phones are accurate to about 0.1 to 0.3 degrees on a firm surface. Without calibration they can be off by a degree or more, so use Set zero or the two-step calibration.

What is the two-step calibration?

Measure, turn the phone around 180 degrees on the same spot, and measure again. The error of the sensor cancels out, so you do not need a surface you know is level.

Why does it ask for permission on iPhone?

Safari on iPhone and iPad only lets websites read the motion sensors after you tap Allow. The readings stay on your phone.

Does it work on a laptop?

Most laptops and desktop computers have no tilt sensor. The tool tells you so and offers the slope calculator instead.

How do I convert degrees to percent slope?

Percent slope is the tangent of the angle times 100. For example 1 degree is about 1.75 percent and 45 degrees is 100 percent. The slope calculator does it for you.

Why does my case matter?

A curved back or a camera bump can tilt the phone. Take thick cases off, or calibrate with the case on and always measure the same way.
