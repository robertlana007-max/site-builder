What is First Aid Guide?

The First Aid Guide gives clear steps for 28 emergencies for adults, children and babies, including CPR, choking, severe bleeding, burns, stroke, heart attack, allergic reactions, bites and diabetic emergencies.

Each topic tells you when it is serious, what to do step by step, what NOT to do and when to call emergency services. It follows common guidance for people without medical training from the Red Cross and Red Crescent, St John, the European Resuscitation Council and the American Heart Association.

A CPR metronome beats 110 times a minute, and the emergency numbers panel shows the right number for your country. The guide works offline. It does not replace first aid training.

What you can use it for

Learning before an emergency

Read the main topics so you know what to do.

Parents and carers

Baby and child CPR, choking, fever seizures and burns.

CPR help

Keep the right speed with the metronome until help arrives.

Travel

Check the emergency number for each country you visit.

Workplaces and schools

Print topics for notice boards and first aid kits.

Car and holiday kits

Download the whole guide as a PDF.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use First Aid Guide

  1. Check your emergency number

    The panel at the top guesses your country. Pick another one if it is wrong. Tap a number to call.

  2. Find a topic

    Type what happened, such as "burn" or "choking", or pick a topic from the list.

  3. Filter by age

    Choose Adults, Children or Babies to see only the topics for them.

  4. Follow the steps

    Read "When it is serious", then "Do this" in order, and check "Do NOT".

  5. Use the CPR metronome

    Press Start and push in time with the beat. Choose 30:2 if you are trained to give breaths.

  6. Print or save

    Print a topic, download it as a PDF, or download the whole guide.

Worked example: someone collapses

A man collapses in a shop and is not breathing normally. Ana calls 112 on speaker, opens CPR: adult and presses Start metronome. She pushes hard in the centre of his chest in time with the beat while someone fetches the AED, and follows the AED's voice prompts until the ambulance crew takes over.

Tips and tricks

  • Read the CPR and choking topics now, before you need them.
  • Take a first aid course to practise CPR on a manikin.
  • Save the emergency number for your country in your phone.
  • Download the whole guide as a PDF for your car and first aid kit.
  • In an emergency, call first: the call taker can guide you.

Problems and how to fix them

The emergency number is for the wrong country

Pick your country from the list; your choice is remembered.

I cannot hear the metronome

Turn up the volume and tick "Beep on each push". The circle also flashes on each beat.

My country is not listed

Choose "Other". From a mobile phone, try 112 first, then 911 or 999, and find the local number as soon as you can.

The print has other parts of the page

Use the Print button on the topic; it prints only that topic.

Questions and answers

Does this replace a first aid course?

No. It is a reminder of the main steps from common guidance (Red Cross and Red Crescent, St John, European Resuscitation Council, American Heart Association). Take a course to practise, especially CPR.

How fast should CPR pushes be?

100 to 120 a minute, about 5–6 cm deep on an adult. The metronome beats at 110 a minute. If you are trained, give 2 breaths after every 30 pushes; if not, push without stopping (hands-only CPR).

How does it know my emergency number?

It guesses your country from your device's time zone and language. Check it and pick another country from the list if needed. On most mobile phones, 112 also works in many countries, and 911 in most of the Americas.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. All the topics are inside the page, so once it has loaded it keeps working offline. Download the PDF to keep a copy.

Is the advice the same for children and babies?

No. CPR and choking are different for babies under 1 year and for children, so they have their own topics. Use the Adults, Children and Babies filters.

Why does the screen stay on during the metronome?

So it keeps beating while you do CPR. The page asks the browser to keep the screen awake while the metronome runs.
