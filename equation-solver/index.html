What is Equation Solver?

The Equation Solver solves linear, quadratic and cubic equations and systems of two or three linear equations, and shows every step of the working. Answers are given as exact fractions and square roots where possible, with decimals beside them.

It is built for students who want to check homework and understand the method, not just see the answer. You can type an equation as you would write it, such as 2x + 3 = 7 or (x + 1)^2 = 2x + 9, or fill in the coefficients a, b, c and d.

All the maths runs in your browser, with no sign-up and nothing sent to a server.

What you can use it for

Checking homework

Type the exact equation from your worksheet and compare each step with your own working.

Quadratics with the formula

See the discriminant, both roots, the vertex and the factored form of any quadratic.

Complex roots

When a quadratic or cubic has no real roots, get the complex ones in a + bi form.

Simultaneous equations

Solve two equations in x and y, or three in x, y and z, with every row operation shown.

Teaching

Generate fully worked examples for a lesson, including special cases such as no solution or infinitely many.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Equation Solver

  1. Choose a type

    Use "Type it" to write any linear, quadratic or cubic equation in x. Or pick Linear, Quadratic or Cubic to enter coefficients, or 2 equations or 3 equations for a system.

  2. Type the equation

    Write it naturally: x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0, x/2 + 1/3 = 5 or x^3 = 8. Brackets are expanded for you and terms are moved to one side automatically.

  3. Read the answer

    The blue box shows the solutions. Fractions stay exact, such as x = 7/3, and irrational roots keep their square roots, such as x = −2 ± √3, with decimals alongside.

  4. Follow the steps

    Below the answer is the full working: the discriminant and quadratic formula, the rational root test and division for cubics, or each row operation for systems.

  5. Solve a system

    In 2 equations or 3 equations, type each equation on its own line, such as 2x + 3y = 12. The tool uses Gaussian elimination and checks the answer in the original equations.

  6. Copy the answer

    Press "Copy answer" to copy the result as plain text for your notes.

Features

  • Exact answersFractions and simplified square roots are kept exact, for example (5 ± √13)/2 is shown as 5/2 ± √13/2.
  • Quadratic detailsDiscriminant, nature of the roots, vertex, axis of symmetry, direction of opening, y-intercept and factored form.
  • Full cubic solverFinds rational roots first, then uses Cardano's formula or the trigonometric method, giving all three roots, real or complex.
  • Systems of equations2×2 and 3×3 systems with Gaussian elimination shown as matrices, plus detection of no solution and infinitely many solutions.
  • Natural typingImplicit multiplication, brackets, powers, fractions and decimals are all understood.
  • Clear error messagesTells you exactly what is wrong, for example a variable in a denominator or a power above 3.

Worked example: x³ − 15x − 4 = 0

Type x^3 - 15x - 4 = 0. The solver tries the possible rational roots ±1, ±2, ±4 and finds that x = 4 works, because 64 − 60 − 4 = 0. Dividing by (x − 4) leaves x² + 4x + 1. The discriminant is 16 − 4 = 12, so √12 = 2√3 and the other roots are x = −2 ± √3. The answer box shows x₁ ≈ −3.732, x₂ ≈ −0.268 and x₃ = 4, with the exact form −2 ± √3.

Tips and tricks

  • Use x as the variable for single equations, and x, y, z for systems.
  • If an equation has x in a denominator, multiply both sides by it first. For example 1/x = 2 becomes 1 = 2x.
  • Coefficient boxes accept fractions and expressions, such as 3/4 or sqrt(2).
  • A discriminant below zero means the parabola never touches the x-axis, so the roots are complex.
  • For a system, the answer is checked by putting it back into every original equation; look for the green ticks.
  • To solve equations of degree 4 or more, or with sin or e^x, use the Graphing Calculator to find the roots.

Problems and how to fix them

It says the equation is not linear

In a system, every term must be a number times x, y or z. Terms like x², xy or x·z cannot be solved by elimination.

It says a variable in a power is not supported

Equations like 2^x = 8 need logarithms. Graph y = 2^x and y = 8 in the Graphing Calculator to find the answer.

The answer shows only decimals

Exact forms are shown when all coefficients are whole numbers, fractions or decimals. If you used pi or sqrt, the answer is given in decimals.

I get "infinitely many solutions"

Your equations are multiples of each other, so they describe the same line or plane. The answer is given in terms of a free parameter t.

Questions and answers

Does it give exact answers?

Yes. Fractions stay exact and square roots are simplified, for example x = −2 ± √3, with decimals shown alongside.

Can it find complex roots?

Yes. Quadratics and cubics with no real roots get complex roots in the form a + bi.

How does it solve cubics?

It first tries the rational root theorem and divides out any root it finds. Otherwise it uses Cardano's formula or the trigonometric method for three real roots.

How are simultaneous equations solved?

With Gaussian elimination on the augmented matrix. Each row operation is shown, and it tells you when there is no solution or infinitely many.

Can it solve equations with sin or e^x?

No, only polynomial equations up to degree 3. Use the Graphing Calculator to find roots of other functions.
