    What is Add Text to Video?

    Add Text to Video puts titles, captions, labels or notes on top of your video. Each text has its own start and end time, so it appears exactly when you want it.

    Drag the text on the preview to place it, or use the Top, Middle and Bottom buttons. Choose a font, size, colour, alignment and a style: outline, shadow, a box behind the text, or plain. Texts can fade in and out.

    The text is drawn with your device's own fonts, so every language and emoji works, and the video is then encoded to MP4 in your browser with the FFmpeg engine.

    What you can use it for

    Titles and intros

    Put a title at the start of a video.

    Captions for social media

    Most people watch without sound; short captions help them follow.

    Tutorials

    Label steps such as "Step 1: open the lid".

    Memes

    Heavy white text with a black outline at the top and bottom.

    Translations

    Add a line in another language under the speaker.

    Credits and watermarks

    Put your name or website in a corner for the whole video.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Add Text to Video

    1. Open your video

      Click the box or drop a video onto it. A first text, "Your text here", is ready for the first 5 seconds.

    2. Type the text

      Replace it with your own words. Press Enter for a new line; long lines wrap automatically.

    3. Set the times

      Type "Show from" and "Until" times, or play the video and press Now next to each box at the right moment.

    4. Place it

      Drag the text on the preview, or press Top, Middle or Bottom (captions).

    5. Style it

      Choose the size, font, style, colours and alignment. Tick "Fade in and out" for a soft appearance.

    6. Add more texts

      Press "+ Add another text" or "Copy this text". Click a text in the list to edit it.

    7. Save

      Press "Add text to video" and then "Download MP4".

    Worked example: captions for a short recipe video

    Priya has a 30-second cooking clip. She opens it and types "Easy pancakes" for 0:00 to 0:03, presses Top and chooses the Heavy font. She presses "+ Add another text", types "Mix 1 cup flour + 1 egg", plays the video to 0:04 and presses Now for "Show from", then Now at 0:10 for "Until", and presses Bottom (captions) with the Box style. She adds two more steps the same way and saves pancakes-with-text.mp4.

    Tips and tricks

    • Keep captions short: one or two lines that can be read in the time they are shown.
    • Use the Outline or Box style so text stays readable on bright and dark scenes.
    • Keep text away from the very bottom on Reels and TikTok, where the app shows its own buttons.
    • A text size of 5 to 8 works for captions; 10 to 15 for titles.
    • Use "Copy this text" to keep the same style for every caption.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The preview shows no video

    Your browser cannot play this format, but the text is still added. The result is a standard MP4.

    A message says a text is empty or times are wrong

    Every text needs some words, and "Until" must be after "Show from" and inside the video length.

    Some characters show as boxes

    Your device has no font for that script. Try another device, or install the language's fonts.

    It is slow

    The whole video is re-encoded. Shorter or smaller videos are faster; keep the tab open until it finishes.

    Questions and answers

    Which languages can I use?

    Any language your device can show, including Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and emoji. Text is drawn with your device's fonts, so what you see in the preview is what you get.

    Can I show different texts at different times?

    Yes. Add as many texts as you like, each with its own start and end time, position and style.

    Is my video uploaded?

    No. The text is drawn and the video is encoded in your browser.

    Does it keep the sound?

    Yes. The original sound is kept.

    Can I make subtitles for a whole video?

    Yes, by adding one text for each line. For long subtitle files, the Subtitle Generator can create them automatically.

    Why does it re-encode the video?

    Text becomes part of the picture, so every frame has to be encoded again. "Good" quality keeps it sharp with a reasonable file size.
