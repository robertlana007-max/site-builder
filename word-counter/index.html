What is Word Counter?

The Word Counter counts the words, characters, sentences and paragraphs in any text and estimates how long it takes to read. All six numbers update instantly while you type or paste.

It helps students checking an essay against a word limit, writers and bloggers planning article length, job seekers keeping a cover letter short, and anyone filling a form with a character limit, such as a social media bio or a meta description.

Your text is counted in your browser and is not sent anywhere.

What you can use it for

Essays and assignments

Check that your essay is within the required range, for example 1,500 to 2,000 words, before you submit it.

Character limits

See exactly how many characters a post, bio, SMS or product title has, with or without spaces.

Blog and article planning

Aim for a target length and see how many minutes a reader will need.

Speeches and presentations

Use the word count to judge length. Most people speak about 130 words per minute, a little slower than the reading time shown.

Translation and writing jobs

Many freelancers are paid per word. Paste the text to get an exact count for an invoice or quote.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Word Counter

  1. Add your text

    Click in the "Your text" box and type, or paste text from Word, Google Docs, an email or a web page with Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on Mac).

  2. Read the six counters

    Below the box you see "Words", "Characters", "Without spaces", "Sentences", "Paragraphs" and "Reading time". They change with every key you press, so you can edit until you hit your target.

  3. Understand how words are counted

    A word is anything between spaces or line breaks. So "well-known" counts as one word, and "3.5" is one word too. Extra spaces and lone symbols such as a dash do not add words. In Chinese and Japanese, which do not use spaces, each character counts as one word.

  4. Check sentences and paragraphs

    A sentence ends with . ! or ? followed by a space or the end of the text, so a decimal like 3.5 or a web address like example.com does not end a sentence. A paragraph is a block of text separated by a line break; empty lines are not counted.

  5. Use the reading time

    Reading time is the word count divided by 200, rounded to whole minutes. Under 200 words it shows "< 1 min".

  6. Start again

    Click "Clear" to empty the box and reset all counts to zero, ready for the next text.

Features

  • Live countingAll numbers update as you type, with no button to press.
  • Two character countsGet the total including spaces and a second count without any spaces, tabs or line breaks.
  • Sentence and paragraph countHelps you see whether your sentences or paragraphs are too long.
  • Reading timeA quick estimate based on 200 words per minute.
  • Emoji and accent friendlyCharacters are counted as you see them, so an emoji or accented letter counts as one.
  • One-click clearEmpty the box and reset all counters instantly.

Worked example: checking a short article

You paste a blog draft into the box. The counters show Words 1,240, Characters 7,410, Without spaces 6,180, Sentences 68 and Paragraphs 14. Reading time shows 6 min, because 1,240 ÷ 200 = 6.2, which rounds to 6. The average sentence is about 18 words (1,240 ÷ 68), which is easy to read. If your editor wants under 1,200 words, you know to cut about 40.

Tips and tricks

  • Paste plain text if possible. Page numbers, headers or footnotes copied from a PDF are counted too.
  • Bullet symbols on their own, such as a lone dash or asterisk, are not counted as words, but numbered list markers like "1." are.
  • Abbreviations like "e.g." or "Dr." contain full stops, so the sentence count can be a little higher than you expect.
  • For a meta description, aim for about 150 to 160 characters; for a tweet-length post, watch the Characters number.
  • Keep an eye on the Paragraphs count: online readers prefer short paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences.

Problems and how to fix them

The count is different from Microsoft Word

Tools treat dashes, numbers and symbols a little differently. The difference is usually just a few words. Check that hidden text, footnotes or headers were not copied.

The sentence count looks too high

Full stops in abbreviations, decimal numbers such as 3.5, or lists ending with full stops can be counted as sentence ends. This is normal.

Paragraphs shows 1 for a long text

Your text has no line breaks, or the line breaks were lost when copying. Press Enter between paragraphs.

Reading time stays at "< 1 min"

Your text has fewer than 200 words. It changes to 1 min once you pass about 200 words.

Questions and answers

How is reading time calculated?

It assumes an average reading speed of 200 words per minute.

Do spaces count as characters?

The Characters figure includes spaces. Without spaces shows the count with all spaces and line breaks removed.

Is my text saved?

No. The text stays in your browser and disappears when you close the page.
