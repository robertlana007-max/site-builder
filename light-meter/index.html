How accurate is it?

Light sensor: a few phones let the browser read the real ambient light sensor (it sits next to the front camera). Then readings are fairly good. Most browsers do not allow it.

Camera: the tool measures how bright the camera picture is. When the browser also reports the camera's exposure time and ISO, it turns that into an exposure value (EV) and an estimated lux. When it does not, the camera keeps adjusting itself, so you only get a relative brightness, good for comparing spots in the same room. Expect errors of 2× or more unless you calibrate.

Best method: for the light falling on a desk or plant, lay the phone there with the front camera facing the light, and cover the camera with a thin piece of white paper. Keep your shadow off it.

What is Light Meter?

Light Meter estimates how bright it is where you are, in lux or foot-candles, and as a photographic exposure value (EV). It uses the phone's ambient light sensor where the browser allows it, and otherwise the camera.

Camera readings are approximate and clearly marked. When the browser reports the camera's exposure time and ISO, the tool works out EV and lux; when it does not, it shows a relative brightness for comparing spots. You can calibrate against a real lux meter.

Built-in guides show what the light level means for photography (with shutter speeds), for houseplants (low, medium, bright or direct light) and for reading and work spaces. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.

What you can use it for

Houseplants

Find the spot in your home with the right light for a plant.

Desks and study

Check a desk or classroom has enough light for reading (300–500 lux).

Photography

Get an EV and suggested shutter speed for an aperture and ISO.

Comparing bulbs

Compare lamps and positions in the same room.

Growing seedlings

Check that grow lights or a windowsill give enough light.

Video calls and filming

See if your face is lit well enough.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Light Meter

  1. Start the meter

    Press Start light meter and allow the camera if asked. Best available uses the light sensor if the browser allows it, otherwise the front camera.

  2. Place the phone

    For the light falling on a desk or plant, lay the phone there with the front camera facing up toward the light and cover the camera with thin white paper.

  3. Read the result

    The big number is the estimate. The Approximate or Relative only label tells you how much to trust it.

  4. Use the guides

    Open Photography, Plants or Reading and work to see what the level means. The matching photography row is highlighted.

  5. Calibrate (optional)

    Next to a real lux meter, type its reading and press Calibrate. The correction is saved for that camera or sensor.

  6. No sensor?

    Type a camera's aperture, shutter speed and ISO into the calculator to get EV and approximate lux.

Worked example: where to put a fern

Priya wants to move a fern. She lays her Android phone on the shelf with a tissue over the front camera and presses Start. It reads about 3,200 lux, "Medium light", which suits most ferns. On the windowsill it reads 18,000 lux, "Bright indirect", and in a corner only 400 lux, "Too dark". She keeps it on the shelf, knowing the numbers are approximate but good for comparing spots.

Tips and tricks

  • Measure plants around midday and on a typical day, not just once on a dull morning.
  • Cover the camera with thin white paper to measure the light falling on a spot, not the brightness of the scene.
  • Keep your own shadow off the phone.
  • If the result says Relative only, compare spots in the same room instead of reading lux.
  • Calibrate once against a real lux meter for the best accuracy.

Problems and how to fix them

It says Relative only

Your browser does not report the camera's exposure, so lux cannot be worked out. Compare spots, or try Chrome on Android.

The camera does not start

Allow camera access for this site and close other apps that use the camera.

The reading seems far too low in sunlight

The camera picture is saturated. Cover the camera with white paper or point it away from the direct sun.

The light sensor option does not work

Most browsers do not let websites read the light sensor. Choose a camera instead.

Questions and answers

How accurate is a phone light meter?

It is an estimate. With the ambient light sensor it is often within about 20 percent. With the camera, expect errors of 2 times or more unless you calibrate, and some browsers only allow a relative brightness.

Why does it say Relative only?

Your browser does not tell websites the camera exposure, and the camera keeps adjusting itself. Then only a relative brightness can be shown, which is still useful for comparing spots in the same room.

How much light do houseplants need?

Roughly: low light 500 to 2,500 lux, medium 2,500 to 10,000 lux, bright indirect 10,000 to 20,000 lux, and direct sun above 20,000 lux. Guides differ by plant.

How much light is needed for reading?

About 300 to 500 lux for reading and desk work, and more for detailed tasks or older eyes, based on common lighting standards.

What is EV?

Exposure value is a photography scale of light. Each step up doubles the light. A sunny day is about EV 15 at ISO 100, and a living room at night about EV 5 to 6.

Is the camera picture saved?

No. The tool only reads the average brightness in your browser. No picture is stored or sent.
