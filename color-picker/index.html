      What is Color Picker from Image?

      Color Picker from Image lets you click any spot in a picture and get its exact colour code in HEX, RGB and HSL. A round magnifying loupe follows your pointer and shows the pixels up close, so you can hit thin lines and small details.

      It also finds the main colours of the whole image and lists them from most to least common, which is a fast way to build a colour palette from a photo, a logo or a website screenshot. Every colour you pick is kept in a list that you can copy in one go.

      The image is read by your own browser and never uploaded.

      What you can use it for

      Match brand colours

      Get the exact HEX code of a logo colour from a screenshot or image.

      Web and app design

      Copy colours from a mock-up or a website screenshot into your CSS or design tool.

      Build a palette from a photo

      Extract the main colours of a landscape or product photo for a mood board.

      Craft and home projects

      Find the colour code of a fabric, paint or wall in a photo to compare with paint charts.

      Check accessibility

      Read the exact text and background colours to test their contrast.

      Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Color Picker from Image

      1. Add an image

        Click "Click or drop an image here", drop a file onto the box, or copy a screenshot and press Ctrl+V on the page.

      2. Find your colour with the loupe

        Move your mouse over the image. The loupe shows the pixels around the pointer and the colour code under it. On a phone, touch the image and slide your finger; the loupe sits above your finger.

      3. Pick it

        Click, or lift your finger, to pick the colour. The big swatch and the HEX, RGB and HSL boxes update.

      4. Copy the code

        Press "Copy" next to HEX, RGB or HSL. The button briefly shows "Copied!".

      5. Use the picked colours list

        Every pick is added under "Picked colours". Click one to show its codes again, or press "Copy all" to copy every HEX code.

      6. Get the main colours

        Under "Main colours in the image", choose how many colours (5 to 8). Each shows its HEX code and how much of the image it covers. Click a colour to copy it, or press "Copy palette".

      7. No image? Pick any colour

        Use the colour box at the top to choose any colour and see its codes. In Chrome or Edge on a computer, "Pick from screen" lets you pick a colour from anywhere on your screen.

      Features

      • Magnifying loupeZoomed pixels with a centre marker for exact picks, on desktop and touch screens.
      • HEX, RGB and HSLAll three formats, each with a copy button.
      • Averaged samplingRead 1 pixel, or the average of 3 × 3 or 5 × 5 pixels for steadier colours in photos.
      • Picked colours listKeeps up to 48 picks, with no duplicates, ready to copy.
      • Main colour paletteFinds the 5 to 8 most important colours with their share of the image.
      • Keyboard supportArrow keys move the pick point and Enter picks.
      • Paste and screen pickingPaste screenshots directly, or pick from anywhere on screen where supported.

      Worked example: matching a logo colour

      Sam needs the exact blue of a client's logo for a website. He takes a screenshot, presses Ctrl+V on the page and moves over the logo. The loupe shows #1565C0. He clicks, presses "Copy" next to HEX and pastes #1565C0 into his stylesheet. The main colours list shows #1565C0 at 94% and #FFFFFF at 5%, confirming the logo uses just blue and white.

      Tips and tricks

      • Zoom into the edge of text or a thin line with the loupe; edges are often a blend of two colours, so pick from the middle.
      • Photos and JPG files have tiny colour variations. Use "3 × 3 average" for a more typical colour.
      • Screenshots in PNG keep exact colours, which is best for picking web and app colours.
      • See-through parts of an image are read as if they were on white.
      • Very large images are shown at up to 3000 pixels wide, which keeps picking fast and does not change the colours.

      Problems and how to fix them

      The picked colour is a little off

      The image may be a compressed JPG, or you picked an edge pixel. Use the loupe to aim at the middle of the area, or switch to an averaged sample.

      I do not see the Pick from screen button

      Picking from the screen only works in Chrome and Edge on a computer. Everywhere else, take a screenshot and paste it instead.

      Pasting does nothing

      Make sure an image (not a file name) is on your clipboard, then click on the page and press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on Mac).

      The main colours list shows fewer colours than I chose

      The image has fewer distinct colours. Tiny specks under about half a percent of the image are left out.

      Questions and answers

      Is my image uploaded?

      No. The colours are read by your own browser and the image never leaves your device.

      How do I pick a colour on a phone?

      Touch the image and slide your finger. A loupe above your finger shows the exact pixel, and the colour is picked when you lift your finger.

      Why is the colour slightly different from my design file?

      Images saved as JPG change colours a little when compressed. Use the 3 × 3 average setting for a steadier reading of photos.

      How are the main colours found?

      The tool groups similar pixels together (k-means clustering) and lists the groups from most to least common, with the share of the image each covers.

      Can I pick a colour from outside the browser?

      In Chrome and Edge on a computer, the Pick from screen button lets you pick from anywhere on your screen.
