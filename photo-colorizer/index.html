What is Photo Colorizer?

The Photo Colorizer adds colour to black and white photos. An AI model that has learned from more than a million colour photos guesses likely colours for skies, skin, grass, wood, clothes and more, and paints them onto your picture while keeping all of its original detail.

The model runs inside your browser, so your family photos are never uploaded. Choose between two colour styles, Natural and Vivid, compare the result with the original using a slider, adjust the colour strength and download the full-size photo.

The colours are an educated guess, not a record of the real ones. It works best on clear photos of people, landscapes and everyday scenes, and gives old pictures a fresh, lively look in seconds.

What you can use it for

Bring family photos to life

Add colour to scans of grandparents' wedding pictures, childhood photos or old portraits.

History projects and presentations

Make historical photos more engaging for school work, slides or social media posts.

Restore the look of faded prints

Colourise faded, washed-out or sepia prints after scanning them.

Creative edits

Turn a black and white shot into a soft, painterly colour version for a poster or card.

Compare styles

See how a scene might have looked in colour before doing a careful manual restoration.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Photo Colorizer

  1. Choose a colour style

    "Natural" gives softer, realistic colours and is a good start. "Vivid" gives stronger, warmer colours that suit landscapes and objects. Each style downloads once (about 32 to 34 MB, plus about 15 MB for the AI engine) and is then kept by your browser.

  2. Add your photo

    Click the box or drop a black and white photo onto it. A progress bar shows the one-time download, then "Adding colour" with a timer. It usually takes a few seconds, longer on older phones.

  3. Compare with the original

    Drag across the photo to move the divider between the original on the left and the colour version on the right.

  4. Adjust the colour strength

    Move "Colour strength" to the left for subtle, faded colours or to the right for richer ones. The change is instant.

  5. Try the other style

    If the colours look wrong, switch "Colour style". Each style colours some scenes better than the other.

  6. Download

    Press "Download JPG" for a smaller file or "Download PNG" for the best quality. The photo keeps its full original size.

Features

  • AI colourisationUses the open-source colorization models by Zhang et al. (BSD licence), adapted to run in the browser.
  • Two colour stylesNatural for realistic results, Vivid for stronger colours.
  • Full resolutionColour is added to your original-size photo; all the fine detail of the scan is kept.
  • Colour strength controlFrom a gentle tint to bold colour, without running the AI again.
  • Before and after sliderCompare the original and the colour version side by side.
  • PrivateRuns on your device. Your photos are never uploaded.
  • Works offlineAfter the first download the models are kept by your browser.

Worked example: a 1950s wedding photo

Mei scanned her grandparents' black and white wedding photo at 2400 × 3000 pixels. She leaves Colour style on Natural and drops the scan in. After the one-time download, the tool shows "Adding colour… 6 s" and then the result: warm skin tones, a pale blue sky and green trees, with the white dress still white. She drags the divider to compare, sets Colour strength to 80% for a softer, period look and presses Download JPG, getting a full-size 2400 × 3000 colour photo to print for the family.

Tips and tricks

  • Scan prints at a good resolution and crop away the white border first; borders can pick up odd colours.
  • Photos with clear subjects (people, sky, trees, water, buildings) colour best. Unusual objects and clothes are guessed and may come out brown or grey.
  • Lower the colour strength if you see colour patches; it often looks more natural at 70 to 90%.
  • Try both styles: Vivid often suits landscapes and objects, Natural often suits portraits.
  • Colour photos can be used too. The tool ignores their colours and creates new ones, which is a fun way to see how the AI sees a scene.

Problems and how to fix them

The colours are wrong

The AI can only guess colours it cannot see. Try the other colour style, lower the strength, or accept that some objects such as clothes or cars may not match reality.

It is very slow

The first run downloads about 50 MB in total. After that, colouring takes a few seconds on a computer and longer on older phones. Closing other tabs helps.

The AI model could not run on this device

It needs a modern browser with WebAssembly and enough free memory. Try an up-to-date Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, and close other apps.

The result looks blotchy

This happens on damaged, very grainy or low-resolution scans. Lower Colour strength, or scan the print again at a higher resolution.

My iPhone photo will not open

HEIC photos cannot be opened by most browsers. Convert it with the HEIC to JPG tool first.

Questions and answers

Is my photo uploaded?

No. The AI model is downloaded to your browser and colours the photo on your device.

Are the colours the real ones?

No. The AI makes an educated guess from millions of colour photos it learned from. Skies, plants and skin are usually convincing; clothes, cars and painted objects may differ from reality.

How big is the download?

About 15 MB for the AI engine plus 32 to 34 MB for each colour style, only the first time. Your browser keeps them for next time.

Does it keep the full resolution?

Yes. The colour is added to your original photo, so the saved file has the same size and detail as your scan.

What is the difference between Natural and Vivid?

They are two different AI models. Natural gives softer, realistic colours; Vivid gives stronger, warmer colours. Try both on your photo.
