What is Chord Progression Generator?

The Chord Progression Generator gives you chord sequences that sound good, in any key. Choose a key, a scale (major, minor, harmonic minor or one of the modes) and a mood or style, and it picks a well-known pattern such as I–V–vi–IV.

Each chord is shown with its name, its Roman numeral and its notes, all spelled correctly for the key. You can see guitar shapes in standard tuning or piano keys, get a capo suggestion and transpose up or down.

Press Play to hear the progression as block chords or arpeggios at your tempo, type your own Roman numerals, and download a MIDI file with the chord names as markers.

What you can use it for

Songwriting

Find a progression for a verse or chorus in the key that suits your voice.

Learning theory

See how the same numerals give different chords in each key.

Guitar practice

Learn the shapes and find a capo position with easy chords.

Jamming

Loop a progression and improvise over it.

Music production

Download MIDI to start a track in your music app.

Teaching

Show students common patterns like ii–V–I and the 12-bar blues.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Chord Progression Generator

  1. Choose a key and scale

    Pick the key (for example G) and the scale: Major for bright songs, Minor for darker ones, or a mode for a special colour.

  2. Choose a mood

    Pick a mood or style such as Pop, Sad, Rock, Jazz or Blues. The Pattern list then shows matching progressions.

  3. Generate

    Press Generate progression for a random pattern, or choose one from the Pattern list. The chords appear with numerals and notes.

  4. Listen

    Press Play. Change the playing style (block chords or arpeggios), the sound, the tempo and the beats per chord. Tap ▶ Hear on a card to play just that chord.

  5. Look at the shapes

    Under Diagrams choose Guitar, Guitar with suggested capo, or Piano.

  6. Transpose or type your own

    Use − and + to move the whole progression by a semitone, or type numerals like "I vi ii7 V7" and press Use these.

  7. Export

    Download MIDI, copy the chord names, or print the page.

Worked example: a song in B♭ with easy guitar shapes

Mei sings best in B♭ major. She chooses B♭, Major and Pop, and picks I–V–vi–IV. The chords are B♭ F Gm E♭, which are barre chords on guitar. She sets Diagrams to "Guitar with suggested capo" and the tip says: put a capo on fret 3 and play G D Em C. She presses Play at 90 BPM with "Arpeggio up" to hear the feel, then downloads the MIDI to add drums in her music app.

Tips and tricks

  • Most pop songs use only four chords; try I–V–vi–IV and vi–IV–I–V first.
  • For a sad sound in a major key, start on vi.
  • Tick "Use 7th chords" for a jazzier, softer sound.
  • A capo keeps the key but lets you use open shapes that ring more.
  • In minor keys, V is often major (E in A minor) for a stronger pull home.

Problems and how to fix them

A numeral is not accepted

Use I to VII in upper or lower case, with ♭ or b, ♯ or #, and endings like 7, maj7, °, ø7, sus4, add9, 6 or 9. Secondary chords written as V/V are not supported; write II7 instead.

The chord names have odd letters like C♭ or E♯

That is the correct spelling in some keys (for example E♯ in F♯ major). Transpose by a semitone if you prefer simpler names.

There is no sound

Tap Play again, check your volume, and on iPhone turn off silent mode.

The guitar shape is hard

Try "Guitar with suggested capo", or transpose to a key such as G, C, D, A or E.

Questions and answers

What do the Roman numerals mean?

They number each chord by the scale step its root is on: I is the first step, V the fifth. Capitals are major chords, small letters are minor, ° is diminished. In C major, I–V–vi–IV is C–G–Am–F; in G major the same numerals give G–D–Em–C.

Are the chord names spelled correctly?

Yes. Every chord is built from note letters a third apart, so you get E♭ and B♭ in E♭ major and F♯ and C♯ in D major, never mixed spellings. Keys are named with the fewest sharps or flats (D♭ major, C♯ minor).

How does the capo suggestion work?

It checks capo positions from fret 1 to 7 and picks the one that turns your chords into easy open shapes. For example, B♭ F Gm E♭ becomes G D Em C shapes with a capo on fret 3.

Why does a minor key use a major V chord?

Many minor-key songs raise the seventh note to make a stronger pull back home, which makes V major (E major in A minor). The patterns use it where musicians usually do. Choose Harmonic minor to see that scale.

What are modes?

Scales that start from a different step of the major scale. Dorian sounds minor but bright, Mixolydian major but bluesy, Lydian dreamy, Phrygian dark and Spanish-sounding. The generator uses only the chords of the mode you pick.

Can I use the progressions in my songs?

Yes. Chord progressions are common building blocks of music and are free for everyone to use.
