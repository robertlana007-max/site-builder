What is Screenshot Beautifier?

Screenshot Beautifier turns a plain screenshot into a polished image ready for social media, presentations, documentation or a portfolio.

Place your screenshot on a gradient, solid colour or your own background image, with padding, rounded corners and a soft shadow. Add a simple browser window or phone frame drawn by the tool, tilt it slightly, add a small watermark, and choose a size made for Twitter/X, Instagram, LinkedIn or 16:9 slides. The PNG is exported at 2× by default so text stays crisp.

Everything is drawn in your browser, so private screenshots are never uploaded.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Screenshot Beautifier

  1. Add your screenshot

    Take a screenshot and press Ctrl+V (⌘+V on Mac) on the page, or drop or choose an image file. "Try with a sample" loads a demo screen.

  2. Pick a background

    Under "Background" choose Gradient and click a preset, or set two colours and the angle. You can also choose Solid colour, Your own image or Transparent.

  3. Style the screenshot

    Choose a "Frame": browser window (light or dark, with optional address bar text), title bar only, or phone. Set "Padding", "Corner rounding", "Shadow" and "Tilt".

  4. Choose the size

    Pick a "Canvas size" such as Twitter / X post, Instagram square, Instagram portrait, Story, LinkedIn post or 16:9, or Fit the screenshot.

  5. Add a watermark (optional)

    Type your name or handle in "Watermark text" and choose its corner.

  6. Download or copy

    Click "Download PNG". The button shows the exact pixel size. In supported browsers you can also click "Copy image" and paste it straight into a post.

Worked example: a feature post for X

Dev took a 1440 × 900 px screenshot of a new dashboard. He pastes it with Ctrl+V, picks the purple-to-pink gradient, chooses Browser window (light) and types app.example.com in the address bar. He keeps padding at 10%, sets corner rounding to 3%, a medium shadow and a tilt of 0°, chooses Twitter / X post (16:9) and types @devmakes as the watermark. Download PNG gives a 2400 × 1350 px image with crisp text.

Tips and tricks

  • Keep tilt small, 2 to 5 degrees, or none for text-heavy screens.
  • Crop your screenshot to the interesting part before adding it; less clutter looks better.
  • Use the dark browser frame with dark-mode screenshots, and the light one with light screens.
  • Choose Fit the screenshot for blog posts and docs, and a fixed size for social media so nothing is cropped by the platform.
  • Before sharing, check the screenshot for private details; use Blur & Censor Image first if needed.

Problems and how to fix them

Pasting does nothing

Make sure an image, not a file path, is on your clipboard: take the screenshot again and paste with Ctrl+V on the page. Some browsers block the Paste button; the keyboard shortcut still works.

Text looks blurry

Export at 2× or 3×, and start from a full-resolution screenshot rather than a scaled-down copy.

Copy image is missing

Your browser does not allow copying images. Use Download PNG instead.

The screenshot looks small on the canvas

Lower the Padding, or choose a canvas size closer to your screenshot's shape.

Questions and answers

Is my screenshot uploaded?

No. Everything is drawn in your browser, so private screens stay on your device.

Why export at 2×?

A 2× image has twice the pixels in each direction, so text stays crisp on phones and high-resolution screens. 1× gives a smaller file.

Which size should I pick for social media?

Twitter / X post (16:9) for X, Instagram square or portrait for Instagram, LinkedIn post for LinkedIn, and Fit the screenshot for docs and blogs.

Can I keep a transparent background?

Yes. Choose Transparent as the background type; the PNG then has see-through areas around the screenshot.

Are the browser and phone frames images of real devices?

No. They are simple generic frames drawn by the tool, not images of any brand of phone or browser.
