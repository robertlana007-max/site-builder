What is Chemical Equation Balancer?

The Chemical Equation Balancer finds the coefficients that make a chemical equation obey the law of conservation of mass: the same number of each kind of atom, and the same total charge, on both sides.

Type the reactants and products and it balances the equation instantly, then shows a table so you can check every element yourself. It handles brackets, hydrates, ions, electrons and state symbols, and is useful for students, teachers and anyone checking lab work.

It works out the answer exactly using linear algebra, in your browser.

What you can use it for

Homework and exam practice

Check your own balancing and see the atom counts that prove the answer.

Combustion reactions

Balance hydrocarbon and alcohol burning, such as propane or ethanol with oxygen.

Redox and ionic equations

Balance equations with ions and electrons, including charge, such as permanganate with iron(II).

Stoichiometry

Get the mole ratios you need for mass and yield calculations, plus the molar mass of each substance.

Lab preparation

Confirm an equation before working out reagent amounts.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Chemical Equation Balancer

  1. Type the equation

    Write reactants on the left and products on the right, separated by =, -> or →. Separate substances with +. Example: Fe + O2 = Fe2O3.

  2. Use correct capital letters

    Each element starts with a capital letter: Co is cobalt, CO is carbon monoxide. Numbers after a symbol are counts (subscripts).

  3. Write brackets, hydrates and ions

    Brackets: Ca(OH)2 or K4[Fe(CN)6]. Hydrates: CuSO4·5H2O or CuSO4*5H2O. Ions: Fe^3+, SO4^2-, Cr2O7{2-}, NH4+ or OH-. Electrons: e-. States like (aq) or (g) are kept.

  4. Read the balanced equation

    The coefficients appear in bold. A coefficient of 1 is not written, as is usual in chemistry.

  5. Check the atom table

    For every element, the Left side and Right side columns must match, and for ions the total charge must match too. A ✓ confirms each row.

  6. Copy the result

    Click Copy balanced equation. The Coefficients table also lists the molar mass of each substance for further calculations.

Features

  • Exact balancingSolves the atom-balance equations with exact fractions, so even large equations get the smallest whole numbers.
  • Ions and electronsCharge is balanced along with atoms, for ionic and half equations.
  • Brackets and hydratesNested (), [] and {} groups and · or * for water of crystallisation.
  • Independent checkAn atom-by-atom and charge table shows the equation really is balanced.
  • Helpful errorsTells you when an element appears on only one side or a symbol is misspelled.
  • Molar massesEach substance's molar mass, and a mass-conservation check.

Worked example: potassium permanganate and hydrochloric acid

Type KMnO4 + HCl = KCl + MnCl2 + H2O + Cl2. The result is 2KMnO4 + 16HCl → 2KCl + 2MnCl2 + 8H2O + 5Cl2. The table checks it: K 2 = 2, Mn 2 = 2, O 8 = 8, H 16 = 16 and Cl 16 = 4 + 2 + 10 = 16 on the right.

Tips and tricks

  • Do not type coefficients yourself; if you do, they are ignored and worked out again.
  • For an ion with more than one unit of charge, use ^ or braces, such as Fe^3+ or SO4{2-}. A plain Fe3+ is read as Fe³⁺ for a single element.
  • If an element appears on only one side, a reactant or product is missing or a formula is wrong.
  • Some equations can be balanced in more than one way because they mix two reactions; the tool shows the smallest version and says so.

Problems and how to fix them

It says the equation cannot be balanced

A formula is probably wrong (for example H2O2 instead of H2O) or a product is missing. Check each formula.

An element is reported on only one side

Atoms cannot appear or disappear. Add the missing substance or correct the formula.

A symbol is not recognised

Check capital letters: Cl, not CL; Mg, not MG. Lowercase first letters are not allowed.

My ion is read wrongly

Write the charge with ^ or braces, for example NH4^+ or Hg2^2+, so subscripts and charges cannot be confused.

Questions and answers

How does it balance equations?

It writes one equation per element (and one for charge) and solves them exactly with linear algebra, then scales the answer to the smallest whole numbers.

Can it balance redox equations?

Yes, if you include the ions, H+ or OH-, water and, for half equations, electrons (e-).

How do I type charges?

Use ^ or braces: Fe^3+, SO4^2-, Cr2O7{2-}. A single + or - at the end also works, as in NH4+ or Cl-.

Why do I get "cannot be balanced"?

No set of positive whole numbers makes the atoms match, usually because a formula is wrong or a substance is missing.
