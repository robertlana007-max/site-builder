Shadow-stick method: push a stick upright into flat ground and mark the tip of its shadow. Wait 15–20 minutes and mark the new tip. A line from the first mark to the second points roughly east; west is behind you. Works anywhere, best around midday.

At night: in the Northern Hemisphere, the North Star (Polaris) is within 1° of true north. In the Southern Hemisphere, extend the long axis of the Southern Cross about 4.5 times its length to find the point above due south.

What is Compass?

Compass turns your phone into a magnetic compass. It shows the direction the top of the phone points in degrees and as a direction such as N, NE or WSW, on a dial that turns as you turn.

Give it your location (typed or from your phone) and it can show true north, the north on maps, using the World Magnetic Model 2025 to work out the magnetic declination where you are. Your location stays on your device.

You can lock a bearing and follow it, see warnings when magnets or metal disturb the reading, calibrate with a figure 8 or a known landmark, and, if your device has no compass sensor, find directions from the sun instead.

What you can use it for

Hiking and walking

Follow a bearing taken from a map, and convert between map and compass bearings.

Finding a direction

Work out which way a window, balcony or garden faces.

Satellite dishes and antennas

Point a dish or antenna at the right bearing.

Solar panels

Check which way a roof or panel faces.

Prayer and orientation

Find a direction you need for a ritual or for setting up furniture.

Learning

Teach children cardinal directions, magnetic declination and the sun's path.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Compass

  1. Start the compass

    Press Start compass. On iPhone and iPad choose Allow when Safari asks about Motion and Orientation.

  2. Hold it flat

    Hold the phone flat like a plate, away from metal, magnets, speakers and cars. The number is the direction the top of the phone points.

  3. Choose the north

    Magnetic north is what an ordinary compass shows. For map north choose True north and set your location.

  4. Lock a bearing

    Point at your target and press Lock this bearing. An orange mark stays on the dial and the text tells you to turn left or right.

  5. Calibrate if needed

    If a warning appears or the reading is wrong, draw a big figure 8 in the air three times. To fine-tune, point at a landmark of known bearing, or at the sun, and press Set.

  6. No sensor?

    Set your location to see where the sun is right now, and use the shadow-stick method or the stars.

Worked example: following a map bearing

Kenji measures a bearing of 120° to a hut on his map. He types his position (46.5, 7.9) so the tool shows a declination of about 3° east, and converts 120° true to about 117° magnetic. He switches the compass to True north, turns until it reads 120°, presses Lock this bearing and follows the "Turn left / turn right" text, keeping the phone flat and away from his metal walking poles.

Tips and tricks

  • Hold the phone flat and level; tilting it makes the reading less accurate.
  • Keep at least a metre from cars, metal fences, rails, speakers and laptops.
  • Magnetic phone cases and car mounts with magnets make the compass useless; take the phone out.
  • Draw a figure 8 now and then, especially after the phone was near a magnet.
  • Check your phone against the sun on a clear day: point at the sun and use Point at the sun and set.

Problems and how to fix them

It says no compass sensor answered

Laptops, desktops and some low-cost phones have no magnetometer. Use the sun section, or another phone.

The direction is clearly wrong

Move away from metal and magnets, draw a figure 8, then fine-tune with a known landmark or the sun.

True north is not shown

Type your latitude and longitude or press Use my location. Without a location only magnetic north can be shown.

iPhone shows nothing

Close the tab, open the page again, press Start compass and choose Allow. On older iPhones turn on Settings > Safari > Motion & Orientation Access.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between true north and magnetic north?

True north is the direction to the North Pole, used on maps and GPS. A compass points to magnetic north. The angle between them is the magnetic declination, which depends on where you are and changes slowly over time.

How is the declination worked out?

With the World Magnetic Model 2025 from NOAA and the British Geological Survey, calculated on your device from the location you give. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Why does my compass point the wrong way?

Magnets and metal nearby, such as cars, speakers, laptops and magnetic phone cases, disturb the sensor. Move away from them and draw a figure 8 with the phone. You can also fine-tune with a known landmark or the sun.

Does it work on a computer?

Laptops and desktops normally have no compass sensor. The tool tells you, and shows where the sun is instead.

Does it need internet?

No. Once the page is open it works offline, including true north and the sun position. Getting your location may take longer without internet.

Is my location stored?

Only on this device, so you do not have to type it again. Press Forget location to remove it.
