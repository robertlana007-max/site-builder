What is Fraction Calculator?

The Fraction Calculator adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides fractions, including mixed numbers like 2 1/3, and shows the full working with the lowest common denominator. It also simplifies fractions and converts between fractions and decimals.

It is ideal for school maths, recipes, measurements and any time you need an exact answer instead of a rounded decimal. Repeating decimals are recognised, so 1/3 is shown as 0.(3) and 0.1(6) turns back into 1/6.

Very large numbers are handled exactly, and everything runs in your browser.

What you can use it for

Maths homework

Check fraction sums step by step, including finding the LCD and simplifying.

Cooking and baking

Add or scale recipe amounts such as 1 1/2 cups plus 3/4 cup.

Woodwork and measuring

Work with inch fractions like 5 3/8 − 2 7/16 without errors.

Repeating decimals

Turn 0.272727… or 0.1(6) into an exact fraction.

Simplifying

Reduce a fraction to lowest terms and see the greatest common factor found with Euclid's algorithm.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Fraction Calculator

  1. Pick a mode

    Calculate works with two fractions, Simplify & convert reduces one fraction, and Decimal to fraction converts decimals, including repeating ones.

  2. Type the fractions

    Write 3/4 for a fraction, 2 1/3 for a mixed number (with a space), 5 for a whole number, or 0.75 for a decimal. Negative numbers start with a minus sign, like −1 1/2.

  3. Choose the operation

    Pick +, −, × or ÷ between the two boxes. The answer updates as you type.

  4. Read the answer

    You get the simplest fraction, the mixed number, the decimal (with repeating digits marked) and the percent.

  5. Follow the working

    The steps show converting mixed numbers, finding the LCD, rewriting each fraction, adding or multiplying, flipping for division, and simplifying by the greatest common factor.

  6. Convert repeating decimals

    In Decimal to fraction, put the repeating digits in brackets: 0.(3), 0.1(6) or 1.(142857). Typing 0.1666... with three dots also works.

Features

  • Mixed numbersProper, improper and mixed fractions are all accepted and shown.
  • Step-by-step with LCDSee exactly how the common denominator is found and used.
  • Exact big numbersUses whole-number arithmetic of any size, so huge numerators never lose digits.
  • Repeating decimalsDetects repeating blocks and shows them with a bar, like 0.1(6).
  • Decimal to fractionIncluding repeating decimals, with the algebra method shown.
  • Percent tooEvery answer is also given as a percentage.

Worked example: 1 1/2 + 2/3

First write 1 1/2 as an improper fraction: (1 × 2 + 1)/2 = 3/2. The denominators are 2 and 3, so the LCD is 6. Rewrite: 3/2 = 9/6 and 2/3 = 4/6. Add the numerators: 9/6 + 4/6 = 13/6. 13 and 6 have no common factor, so it is already simplest. As a mixed number 13 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 1, so the answer is 2 1/6, which is 2.1(6) as a decimal (2.1666…).

Tips and tricks

  • Put a space between the whole number and the fraction in a mixed number: 2 1/3, not 21/3.
  • Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal; the steps show the flip.
  • 0.(9) equals exactly 1, and the calculator shows that.
  • Use Simplify & convert to check whether a fraction is already in lowest terms.
  • A fraction whose denominator has only the prime factors 2 and 5 gives a decimal that ends; any other factor makes it repeat.

Problems and how to fix them

It says the bottom is 0

A fraction cannot have 0 as its denominator. Check the number after the slash.

My mixed number was read wrongly

Make sure there is exactly one space between the whole number and the fraction, and no space inside the fraction.

The decimal stops with "…"

Some fractions repeat with a very long block. The tool shows the start and tells you the length of the repeating block.

A decimal is not recognised

Use a dot as the decimal point and brackets only around the repeating digits, like 0.1(6).

Questions and answers

How do I type a mixed number?

Put a space between the whole number and the fraction, like 2 3/4. For a negative mixed number type −2 3/4.

How are repeating decimals shown?

The repeating digits are marked with a bar, and written in brackets when copied: 1/6 = 0.1(6).

Can it turn a repeating decimal into a fraction?

Yes. In Decimal to fraction type 0.(3), 0.1(6) or 0.1666... and the algebra method is shown.

Does it handle big numbers?

Yes. It uses exact whole-number arithmetic of any size, so answers never lose digits.

What is the LCD?

The lowest common denominator is the smallest number both denominators divide into. Fractions are rewritten with it before adding or subtracting.
