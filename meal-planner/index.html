What is Meal Planner?

Meal Planner helps you decide what to eat each day of the week and turns that plan into one shopping list. Every day has breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks, and each meal can hold one or more recipes with the number of servings you need, or a note like "Eat out".

The shopping list adds up the ingredients of every recipe in the week, scaled to your servings. The same ingredients are merged, units are converted (grams with kilograms, millilitres with litres, teaspoons with tablespoons), and amounts are rounded up so you buy enough.

It comes with sample recipes from many cuisines around the world, and you can add your own. Print the weekly plan with the recipes and the shopping list, share the list, or send it to the Grocery List tool. It is a planning tool only and does not count calories or give food advice.

Trying new food

Plan dishes from other cuisines with the sample recipes.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Meal Planner

  1. Set the basics

    Type how many people are eating, choose metric or US units, and the day your week starts.

  2. Fill a meal

    Press + Add under a meal, pick a recipe and the servings, then press Add. Choose "Just a note" for things like leftovers.

  3. Move between weeks

    Use Prev and Next, or This week. Copy last week repeats a plan you liked.

  4. Check the shopping list

    It updates as you plan. Tick anything you already have at home.

  5. Add your own recipes

    In Add your own recipe, type the name, servings and one ingredient per line, like "1 tbsp olive oil". Check the "Read as" line, then Save recipe.

  6. Print and share

    Print weekly plan makes a PDF with the plan and recipes. Print shopping list, Share as text, WhatsApp or Send to Grocery List take the list with you.

Example: three dinners for two

Dal tadka (serves 4) for 2 people on Sunday, tomato and egg stir-fry (serves 2) for 2 on Monday and shakshuka (serves 2) for breakfast on Tuesday. The shopping list shows 8 eggs (4 + 4), 2 onions (half an onion + 1, rounded up), 4 cloves of garlic (1.5 + 2, rounded up), 4 tomatoes and 3 tbsp of oil (1 tbsp + 2 tbsp).

Tips and tricks

  • Plan dishes that share ingredients, like coriander or peppers, so nothing goes to waste.
  • Tick ingredients you already have before you share or print the list.
  • Write ingredients as "amount unit name", like "250 g rice", for the best merging.
  • Use Copy last week for a quick start, then change a few meals.
  • Export a backup now and then, especially after adding your own recipes.

Problems and how to fix them

An ingredient appears twice

Names must match to merge, like "tomatoes" and "tomato". Different words, such as "cherry tomatoes", stay separate. Edit the recipe to use the same name.

Weights and spoons are shown with a +

Grams and spoons cannot be added without knowing the food, so both are shown, like "200 g + 2 tbsp".

A line was read wrongly

Put the amount first, then the unit, then the name: "1 1/2 cups milk". Lines without an amount are listed as "as needed".

My week moved after changing the first day

Meals stay on their real dates; the week view just starts on a different day.

Questions and answers

How does the shopping list add things up?

Each recipe is scaled to the servings you chose. The same ingredients are merged by name (onion and onions count as one), weights are added in grams, volumes in millilitres, and counts like cloves or cans separately. Amounts are rounded up so you buy enough.

Can I use US cups and ounces?

Yes. Choose US units and the shopping list shows ounces, pounds and cups. Recipes can mix g, kg, ml, L, tsp, tbsp, cups, oz and lb.

How do I add my own recipe?

Type a name, how many it serves and the ingredients one per line, like "200 g rice" or "2 onions". The line under the box shows how each line was understood.

Which cuisines are in the sample recipes?

Indian, Mexican, Japanese, Italian, Nigerian, Middle Eastern, Lebanese, Chinese, Thai, Moroccan, Brazilian, Greek, Turkish, Korean, Pakistani, Ethiopian, Kenyan, French, Indonesian, Filipino, Peruvian, Vietnamese and more. You can edit or delete any of them.

Does it count calories?

No. This is a planner for meals and shopping only.

Where are my plans saved?

Only in this browser. Use Export plans and recipes to keep a backup or move them to another device.
