What is Compound Interest Calculator?

The Compound Interest Calculator shows how money grows when interest is added to the balance and then earns interest itself. You enter a starting amount, an optional monthly contribution, a yearly rate, the number of years and how often interest is compounded.

It helps savers and new investors see the effect of time and regular deposits. You can check how much a savings account, a deposit or a long-term investment plan might be worth in the future, and how much of that is interest.

Everything is calculated in your browser while you type. Results are estimates and not financial advice.

What you can use it for

Plan long-term savings

See what monthly deposits could grow to over 10, 20 or 30 years.

Compare interest rates

Try two rates to see how much difference a higher rate makes over time.

Show the power of starting early

Change the years and watch how interest grows faster in later years.

Compare compounding options

Switch between Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly and Daily to see how often compounding matters.

Set a savings goal

Adjust the monthly contribution until the "Future value" reaches your target.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount

    Type the money you have now in "Starting amount". It can be 0 if you only make monthly deposits, but the starting amount and monthly contribution cannot both be 0.

  2. Enter a monthly contribution

    Type how much you will add every month in "Monthly contribution", or 0 for none. Contributions are added at the end of each month.

  3. Enter the rate and years

    Type the yearly rate in "Interest rate (% per year)", for example 7. Type the number of "Years" from 1 to 100; decimals are rounded to a whole year.

  4. Choose the compounding

    In "Compounded", pick Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly or Daily. This is how often the bank adds interest. More frequent compounding gives a slightly higher result.

  5. Read the results

    "Future value" is the balance at the end. "Total you put in" is the starting amount plus all monthly contributions. "Interest earned" is the difference between the two.

  6. Read the year-by-year table

    The "Year by year" table shows, for each year, the total "Contributions" so far, the total "Interest" earned so far and the "Balance" at the end of that year.

Features

  • Month-by-month growthThe tool turns your rate and compounding choice into an equal monthly growth rate: (1 + rate ÷ n) to the power (n ÷ 12) − 1, where n is 12, 4, 1 or 365. Each month the balance grows by that rate and then the contribution is added.
  • Four compounding optionsMonthly, Quarterly, Yearly and Daily.
  • Regular contributionsAdd a fixed amount every month to see how saving and interest work together.
  • Clear split of the resultSee how much of the final balance is your money and how much is interest.
  • Year-by-year tableRunning totals for every year, up to 100 years.
  • Live resultsThe figures and table update as you type.

Worked example: 5,000 plus 100 a month

You start with 5000, add 100 every month, earn 6% a year compounded monthly, for 10 years. The monthly growth rate is 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5%. After 120 months the "Future value" is 25,484.92. "Total you put in" is 5000 + 120 × 100 = 17,000.00, so "Interest earned" is 8,484.92. If you change "Compounded" to Yearly, the future value is 25,201.58. With no monthly contribution at all, 5000 grows to 9,096.98.

Tips and tricks

  • Use a realistic rate; long-term average returns are not guaranteed every year.
  • Small monthly amounts add up; try raising the contribution by a little to see the effect.
  • Check the last rows of the table to see how interest speeds up near the end.
  • The tool does not subtract tax, fees or inflation, so real growth may be lower.
  • For a savings account, pick the compounding your bank states, often Monthly or Daily.
  • To model a one-time deposit only, set the monthly contribution to 0.

Problems and how to fix them

Results show a dash and a red message

You need an amount above 0 in at least one of the first two boxes, a rate of 0 or more, and 1 to 100 years. The starting amount and monthly contribution cannot be negative.

Changing the compounding barely changes the result

That is normal. The difference between Monthly and Daily is small; time and rate matter much more.

My deposits are made at the start of the month

This tool adds them at the end of each month, so your real balance may be slightly higher.

The years box ignores my decimal

Years are rounded to a whole number, for example 7.6 becomes 8.

Questions and answers

What is compound interest?

It is interest earned on both your original money and the interest already added, so growth speeds up over time.

When are contributions added?

At the end of each month.

Does this account for inflation or tax?

No. Results are before inflation, fees and taxes, and real investment returns vary from year to year.
