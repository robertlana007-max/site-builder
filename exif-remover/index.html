What is EXIF Remover?

EXIF Remover shows and removes the hidden information inside photos. Phones and cameras save details like the camera model, the date and time and, very often, the GPS coordinates of where the photo was taken. Anyone who gets the original file can read them.

The tool lists what each photo contains, warns you about GPS location, and removes all of it. JPG, PNG and WebP files are cleaned without re-compressing, so the picture is exactly the same; other formats are saved as a clean PNG.

Several photos can be cleaned at once and downloaded as a ZIP. Everything happens in your browser.

What you can use it for

Selling online

Remove the location of your home from photos of items you sell on marketplaces.

Sharing with strangers

Clean photos before posting in forums, dating apps or classified ads.

Journalism and activism

Strip camera serial numbers and locations that could identify a source.

Check a photo

See when and with which camera a photo was taken.

Smaller files

Removing large embedded thumbnails and maker data makes files a little smaller.

Work documents

Remove editing software names, authors and comments before sending images to clients.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use EXIF Remover

  1. Add your photos

    Click "Click or drop photos here" and choose one or many JPG, PNG or WebP files.

  2. Read the summary

    The boxes at the top count the photos, those with hidden data and those with a GPS location. Each photo shows what it contains, with a red warning if it shows where it was taken.

  3. See the details

    Open "Show what is inside" to read the camera, date, settings and the GPS coordinates.

  4. Choose about the colour profile

    Keep "Keep the colour profile" ticked for correct colours; it contains no personal data.

  5. Clean and download

    Press "Clean and download" on one photo, or "Clean all and download ZIP". Clean files end in -clean, for example IMG_2041-clean.jpg.

Worked example: selling a bike online

Jonas took three photos of his bike in his garden to sell it online. He drops them onto the page: all three are flagged "Shows where it was taken", and the details show coordinates that point to his house, plus his phone model. He presses "Clean all 3 and download ZIP" and uploads the clean copies, which look identical but no longer reveal where he lives.

Tips and tricks

  • Most social networks remove GPS data when you post, but messaging apps, email and file sharing often keep it. Clean photos before sending them.
  • You can turn off location tagging in your phone camera settings to stop it being saved in the first place.
  • Keep the originals if you want to know later where and when they were taken.
  • Screenshots usually have no location, but they can still have software names and dates.

Problems and how to fix them

My HEIC photo is not accepted

Convert it with the HEIC to JPG tool, which also drops the hidden data.

A photo shows "No hidden data found"

It has nothing to remove: many apps already strip data when they save.

My GIF or BMP became a PNG

These formats are cleaned by saving a fresh PNG copy, which keeps the picture but no hidden data.

The download did not start

Use the green Download button. Some browsers block automatic downloads.

Questions and answers

What is EXIF data?

Extra information saved inside a photo by the camera or phone: make and model, date and time, settings like exposure and ISO, and often GPS coordinates of where it was taken. Editing apps add more, such as XMP and comments.

Does removing it lower the quality?

No. JPG, PNG and WebP files are cleaned by removing the data blocks only, so the picture itself is not re-compressed. Other formats are saved as a fresh PNG.

Will my phone photos end up sideways?

No. If a photo uses the rotation setting, a tiny block with only the rotation is kept, so it still shows the right way up. It holds no personal information.

What about the colour profile?

It is kept by default because it holds no personal data and keeps colours correct. Untick the box to remove it too.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. They are read and cleaned in your browser.
