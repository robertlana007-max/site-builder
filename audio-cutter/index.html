What is Audio Cutter?

Audio Cutter lets you keep just the part of a song or recording that you want. You see the sound as a waveform, drag the start and end, listen to your selection, and save it.

It is made for ringtones and short clips: one button selects 30 seconds, you can add a soft fade in and fade out so the clip does not start or stop suddenly, and you can save as MP3 for Android or M4R for iPhone.

Your file is read and cut in your browser with the Web Audio feature and the open-source LAME MP3 encoder. Nothing is uploaded.

What you can use it for

Ringtones

Cut the chorus of a song into a 30-second ringtone for Android (MP3) or iPhone (M4R).

Alarm and notification sounds

Keep a few seconds of a sound you like for your alarm or message alert.

Voice notes and interviews

Remove silence or chatter at the start and end of a recording.

Music practice

Cut out a hard passage to listen to it again and again.

Videos and presentations

Prepare a short music clip with fades for a slideshow or video.

Sound from a video

Open an MP4 and save a part of its sound as MP3.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Audio Cutter

  1. Open your file

    Click the box or drop a file onto it. MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, FLAC and videos such as MP4 work. The name, size and length appear above the waveform.

  2. Choose the part to keep

    The highlighted area of the waveform is what you keep. Drag near its left edge to move the start and near its right edge to move the end. Drag in the middle to slide the whole selection.

  3. Fine-tune the times

    Type exact times in "Start" and "End" such as 0:45.5, or use the − and + buttons to move them by 0.1 seconds. On a keyboard, click the waveform and use the arrow keys (hold Shift for the end).

  4. Listen

    Press "Play selection" to hear exactly what will be saved, including the fades. A line shows where it is playing.

  5. Add fades

    Choose "Fade in" and "Fade out". The waveform shows the volume rising and falling at the edges.

  6. Pick the format

    MP3 plays everywhere and works as an Android ringtone. M4R is the iPhone ringtone format (up to 40 seconds). WAV keeps full quality.

  7. Save

    Press "Cut and save", then "Download". The file is named after your song with "-cut" added.

Worked example: an iPhone ringtone from a song

Sara wants the chorus of song.mp3 (3:40) as her ringtone. She drops the file on the box, presses "Ringtone: 30 seconds", then drags the highlighted part until it starts at 1:02.0, just before the chorus. She presses "Play selection", hears that it ends mid-word, and presses − next to End twice. She keeps Fade in at 2 seconds and Fade out at 3 seconds, chooses "M4R (iPhone ringtone, up to 40 s)" and presses "Cut and save M4R". After a few seconds she downloads song-cut.m4r and drags it onto her iPhone in Finder.

Tips and tricks

  • Cut a little before the beat starts: a fade in of 0.5 to 1 second keeps the start of the music.
  • For Android, save as MP3 and put the file in the Ringtones folder, or choose it from the sound settings.
  • iPhone ringtones must be 40 seconds or less; the tool will tell you if the selection is too long.
  • Use WAV if you will edit the clip again in another program, so the quality does not drop twice.
  • If a file does not open in Safari or Firefox, try Chrome or Edge, which read more formats.

Problems and how to fix them

It says the browser could not read the sound

The file may be damaged, have no sound track, or use a format your browser cannot decode, such as WMA or AMR. Convert it first with the Audio Converter, or try Chrome or Edge.

M4R is not allowed

Your selection is longer than 40 seconds, the most an iPhone accepts for a ringtone. Press "Ringtone: 30 seconds" or move the end closer.

Saving M4R takes a while the first time

The M4R format needs the free video engine (about 32 MB), which downloads once and is then kept by your browser.

There is no sound when I press Play selection

Check your volume and that your phone is not on silent. Saving still works even if you cannot hear it.

Questions and answers

Is my song uploaded?

No. The file is opened and cut inside your browser, so it never leaves your phone or computer.

How do I make a ringtone?

Open the song, press "Ringtone: 30 seconds" and drag the highlighted part to your favourite section. Save as MP3 for Android, or as M4R for iPhone.

How do I put the M4R ringtone on my iPhone?

Connect the iPhone to a computer and drag the M4R file onto it in Finder (Mac) or Apple Devices / iTunes (Windows). Then choose it in Settings, Sounds & Haptics, Ringtone. iPhone ringtones can be at most 40 seconds.

Which files can I open?

MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, FLAC and the sound of videos such as MP4 and WebM, as long as your browser can decode the sound. Chrome and Edge open the most formats.

Does cutting lower the quality?

WAV keeps the exact quality. MP3 and M4R are compressed again; at 192 kbps or more the difference is very hard to hear.

Is there a length limit?

Files up to 1 GB are accepted, but the whole sound is held in memory, so very long recordings can be slow on a phone.
