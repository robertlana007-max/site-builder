      What is Resume Maker?

      The Resume Maker builds a professional resume or CV in your browser. You fill in clear sections for contact details, summary, work experience, education, skills, projects, certificates, languages, references and interests, and a live preview shows every page exactly as it will be downloaded.

      Five templates cover most needs: Modern with a coloured header, Sidebar with two columns, Classic with a centred serif look, Compact to fit more on a page, and ATS simple, a plain one-column layout that job portals read easily. You can change the colour, font, text size, paper size, photo shape and the order of sections, and rename any heading.

      Download PDF makes a real text PDF with clickable email and web links. Print uses the same pages. Your CV is saved on this device as you type, nothing is uploaded, and you can save a backup file to continue on another computer or phone.

      What you can use it for

      Job applications

      Make a clean CV to send by email or upload to a company website.

      Online job portals

      Use the ATS simple template so applicant tracking systems read every line correctly.

      Students and graduates

      Lead with education, projects and skills when you have little work experience.

      Career changers

      Move Skills and Projects above Work experience to highlight what you can do now.

      Different countries

      Switch between A4 and US Letter, add or hide a photo and personal details to suit local habits.

      Several versions

      Save a backup file for each version, such as one per job type, and open the one you need.

      Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Resume Maker

      1. Add your contact details

        Open "Contact details" and type your name, job title, email, phone and city. Website, profile link and other personal details are optional.

      2. Add a photo if you want one

        Choose a photo file. It is cropped to a square from the centre. Leave it out for the US, UK and Canada.

      3. Fill in your sections

        Open each section and add items with the "+ Add" buttons. In job descriptions, put one achievement per line and each becomes a bullet point.

      4. Group your skills

        Type skills separated by commas. Start a line with a name and a colon, like "Tools: Excel, Figma", to make a group.

      5. Choose a design

        In Design, pick a template, colour, font, text size and paper. Use the arrows in Section order to move sections and untick any you want to hide.

      6. Check the preview and tips

        The preview updates as you type and shows the page count. The tips under it flag a missing email, very long summary or a CV over two pages.

      7. Download or print

        Press Download PDF for a text PDF, or Print to print or save as PDF from your browser.

      8. Keep a backup

        Press "Save backup file" to download your CV data. Use "Open backup" to load it again later, on any device.

      Worked example: a two-page CV down to one

      Maria pastes in three jobs, her degree and a long list of skills. The preview shows 2 pages and a tip suggests one or two pages at most. She switches to the Compact template, sets Text size to Small and moves Skills above Education. The CV now fits on one A4 page. She presses Download PDF and gets Maria-Lopez-CV.pdf with real text, ready to upload.

      Tips and tricks

      • Keep it to one page if you have under ten years of experience, and two pages at most otherwise.
      • Start each job bullet with an action verb and add a number where you can, such as "cut costs by 15%".
      • Use the ATS simple template when you apply through an online form or job portal.
      • Match the words in the job advert in your summary and skills, as recruiters and software look for them.
      • Save a backup file before big changes so you can always go back.
      • Name your file with your name; the tool does this for you, for example Alex-Morgan-CV.pdf.

      Problems and how to fix them

      Some words in the PDF cannot be selected

      Chinese, Japanese and Korean letters and emoji are added as sharp pictures, so job portals cannot read those words. Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Arabic, Urdu, Hebrew, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil and Thai are real text.

      My photo disappeared

      The ATS simple template never shows photos. Choose another template to see it.

      My CV did not come back

      It is saved in this browser only. Private windows and clearing site data remove it, so keep a backup file.

      The PDF does not download on my phone

      Some phone browsers open the PDF instead. Use the share or download button in the PDF view, or press "Download again".

      Questions and answers

      Is this resume maker really free?

      Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no paywall on the PDF. You can make as many CVs as you like.

      Is the PDF readable by applicant tracking systems (ATS)?

      Yes. The PDF contains real, selectable text, not a picture. For online job portals, the ATS simple template is the safest choice: one column, standard headings and no photo or graphics.

      Is my information uploaded anywhere?

      No. Everything runs in your browser. Your CV is saved only in this browser on this device, and you can save a backup file to move it elsewhere.

      Should I use A4 or US Letter?

      Use US Letter for jobs in the United States and Canada, and A4 almost everywhere else. The tool picks the usual size for your region, and you can change it.

      Should I add a photo?

      It depends on the country. In the US, UK, Canada and Australia, CVs usually have no photo. In much of Europe, the Middle East and Asia a photo is common. The ATS template always hides it.

      Can I write my CV in another language?

      Yes. Headings can be renamed and any language works in the PDF. Arabic, Urdu, Hebrew, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil and Thai are real text with their own font, loaded only when you use them; Chinese, Japanese, Korean and emoji are added as sharp pictures.
