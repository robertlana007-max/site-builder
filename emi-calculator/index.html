What is EMI Calculator?

The EMI Calculator shows the fixed monthly payment (EMI) on a loan, how much interest you pay in total and how each payment splits between interest and the loan balance. It works for home loans, mortgages, car loans, personal loans and education loans.

You can add an extra payment every month or a one-time lump sum to see how prepaying shortens the loan and cuts interest. A bar chart shows principal and interest for each year, and the schedule can be shown by year or by month.

Everything runs in your browser. The results are estimates to help you plan; your lender’s figures may differ slightly because of rounding, fees or how they count days.

What you can use it for

Compare loan offers

Type each bank’s rate and term to see which offer has the lower EMI and total interest.

Plan a home or car purchase

Check whether the monthly payment fits your budget before you sign.

Decide on prepaying

See how much interest an annual bonus or a small extra monthly amount would save.

Choose a loan term

Compare 3, 5 or 20 years to balance a lower EMI against more total interest.

Check a lender’s schedule

Set the first payment month and compare the monthly schedule with your loan papers.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use EMI Calculator

  1. Pick a preset or enter your loan

    Tap Home loan, Car loan, Personal loan or Education loan to fill in typical numbers, then replace them with your own amount, rate and term.

  2. Choose the currency

    Select your currency from the list, or choose Other and type your own symbol.

  3. Set the term

    Type the term and choose Years or Months. The longest term is 100 years.

  4. Add a fee or start month (optional)

    A processing fee is added to the total paid. The first payment month puts real month names in the schedule and shows the payoff month.

  5. Try prepayments

    Type an extra amount to pay every month, or a one-time prepayment and the month number when you pay it. The EMI stays the same and the loan ends sooner.

  6. Read the results

    The tiles show the EMI, total interest, total paid, interest saved and when the loan is paid off.

  7. Use the chart and schedule

    Tap a bar to see that year’s principal and interest. Switch the schedule between Yearly and Monthly, then download it as CSV or copy a summary.

Worked example: a 100,000 loan at 9% for 5 years

The monthly rate is 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075 and there are 60 months. The EMI is 2,075.84, the total interest is 24,550.13 and the total paid is 124,550.13. In year 1 you repay 16,583.00 of the loan and 8,327.02 of interest; by year 5 interest is only 1,173.03. Adding 200 extra every month ends the loan several months early and lowers the interest.

Tips and tricks

  • Use the exact rate from your loan offer; a 0.5% difference adds up over a long loan.
  • Prepaying early in the loan saves the most interest, because the balance is highest then.
  • Ask your lender whether prepayments have a fee before you plan them.
  • Keep the EMI under about a third of your take-home pay so you still have room to save.
  • Compare the total paid, not only the EMI: a longer term lowers the EMI but costs more overall.

Problems and how to fix them

The results show a dash and a red message

The loan amount and term must be above 0 and the rate must be 0 or more. Fix the box mentioned in the message.

My bank’s EMI is slightly different

Banks may round each payment, charge from the disbursal date or add fees. Small differences are normal.

The one-time prepayment is ignored

The month number is after the loan already ends. Choose an earlier month.

The schedule shows numbers instead of month names

Set the First payment month to see real months.

Questions and answers

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment: the same payment every month that covers interest and repays part of the loan until it is fully paid.

Which formula does it use?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 and n is the number of months.

How does prepayment work here?

Prepayments go straight to the balance and the EMI stays the same, so the loan finishes earlier. The tool shows the interest saved and how many months sooner you are debt-free.

Does it include fees or insurance?

Only if you add a processing fee, which is included in the total paid. Insurance, taxes and other charges from your lender are not included.

Can I use it for any currency?

Yes. Pick from about 40 currencies or type your own symbol. The maths is the same everywhere.
