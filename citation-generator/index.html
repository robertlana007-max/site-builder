    What is Citation Generator?

    The Citation Generator writes references and in-text citations in APA 7, MLA 9, Harvard (Cite Them Right) and Chicago 18th edition, both notes and bibliography and author-date. You fill in what you know about a source, such as the authors, title, date and link, and the citation appears as you type, with italics in the right places.

    It handles websites, books, journal articles, newspaper and magazine articles, YouTube and other online videos, and reports. It follows each style’s rules for two or more authors and “et al.”, organisations as authors, sources with no date, page ranges and access dates.

    Every source you add goes into a list that is saved in your browser. Change the style and the whole list is reformatted. Copy it with the italics kept, or download it for Word. Nothing is sent to a server.

    What you can use it for

    Essays and assignments

    Build the reference list for an essay as you research, then paste it into Word or Google Docs with the italics in place.

    Switching styles

    Your teacher asks for Harvard instead of APA? Change the style and every saved source is rewritten at once.

    Dissertations and theses

    Keep all your sources in one list, sort it A to Z and download it as a document.

    Footnotes

    In Chicago notes and bibliography, get the full first footnote and the shortened note for later citations.

    Citing web pages and videos

    Get the access date and the “[Video]”, “uploaded by” or “Available at” wording each style wants.

    Checking a citation

    Type in a reference you were given and compare it with the correct format.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Citation Generator

    1. Choose the style

      Pick APA 7, MLA 9, Harvard, Chicago notes and bibliography, or Chicago author-date from “Citation style”. Use the style your teacher or journal asks for.

    2. Choose the source type

      Pick website, book, journal article, newspaper or magazine article, video or report. The boxes change to show only what that type needs, such as volume and issue for a journal.

    3. Add the authors

      Type each person’s first and middle names and last name. Click “+ Add author” for more people, or “+ Add organisation” when a group such as the World Health Organization is the author. Leave the row empty if no author is named.

    4. Fill in the details

      Type the title as it appears, the website or journal name, the date and the URL or DOI. Leave the year empty if there is no date: the citation then shows “n.d.” (or “no date” in Harvard). The access date is filled with today.

    5. Check the citation

      The reference and the in-text citation appear under the form. In Chicago notes and bibliography you see the first footnote and the shortened note instead.

    6. Copy it or add it to your list

      Click “Copy reference” to copy it with italics, or “Add to my list” to keep it. The form clears so you can add the next source.

    7. Manage the list

      Use “Edit” or “Delete” on any entry, “Sort A to Z” to put it in order, and “Copy all” or a download button when you are finished.

    Features

    • Five stylesAPA 7th, MLA 9th, Harvard (Cite Them Right), Chicago 18th notes and bibliography, and Chicago 18th author-date.
    • Correct author rulesUp to 20 authors in APA (then the ellipsis rule), “et al.” for three or more in MLA and in-text citations, all names in Harvard, and up to six names in Chicago before “et al.”.
    • Italics that survive copyingCopied citations keep their italics when pasted into Word, Google Docs or Pages. Plain text is copied too for apps that do not take formatting.
    • In-text citationsShows the bracketed citation and the version you use in a sentence, with page numbers if you add one.
    • Page ranges done rightEn dashes in APA, Harvard and Chicago, and the shortened second number that MLA and Chicago use, such as 207–17.
    • Saved listYour list and the source you are typing are kept in this browser, so you can close the tab and come back later.
    • Download for WordDownload the list as a .txt file or as an .html document with hanging indents that opens in Word.

    Worked example: one journal article in APA and MLA

    Choose “Journal article”. Add three authors: Jessica S. Grady, Malena Her and Geena Moreno. Type the title “Emotions in storybooks”, the journal “Psychology of Popular Media Culture”, volume 8, issue 3, pages 207-217, year 2019 and DOI 10.1037/ppm0000185. In APA 7 the reference reads: Grady, J. S., Her, M., & Moreno, G. (2019). Emotions in storybooks. Psychology of Popular Media Culture, 8(3), 207–217. https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000185, with the journal name and volume in italics. The in-text citation is (Grady et al., 2019). Switch to MLA 9 and retype the title in title case, as MLA asks. It becomes: Grady, Jessica S., et al. “Emotions in Storybooks.” Psychology of Popular Media Culture, vol. 8, no. 3, 2019, pp. 207-17, https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000185. The in-text citation is (Grady et al.).

    Tips and tricks

    • Type titles in the capitalisation your style wants. APA and Harvard use sentence case (only the first word and names in capitals); MLA and Chicago use title case. The tool keeps your capitals as typed.
    • If an article has a DOI, add it. Every style prefers the DOI to a web address.
    • For a web page by an organisation that also owns the site, add the organisation as the author. APA then leaves out the repeated site name, and MLA starts with the title, as the rules say.
    • Your list re-formats itself when you change the style, so you only need to type each source once.
    • Always check the result against your course handbook. Some teachers ask for small changes, such as an access date in APA.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The title is not in italics in my document

    Some apps only accept plain text. Paste into Word, Google Docs or Pages, or use “Download for Word (.html)” and open that file in Word.

    It says “n.d.” or “no date”

    The year box is empty. Add the year if the source shows one. If it really has no date, this is correct.

    The author is missing from the start

    The author row is empty, or (in MLA) the organisation is the same as the website or publisher, so MLA starts with the title. Check the author row.

    My list is gone

    The list is saved in this browser only. A different browser, a private window or clearing site data will not show it. Download the list to keep a copy.

    Questions and answers

    Which editions does it follow?

    APA 7th edition, MLA 9th edition, Harvard as described in Cite Them Right, and the Chicago Manual of Style 18th edition (notes and bibliography, and author-date).

    How does it handle many authors?

    It follows each style: APA lists up to 20 authors, MLA uses et al. for three or more, Harvard lists everyone in the reference list, and Chicago lists up to six before et al. In-text citations use et al. where the style says so.

    What if the source has no date or no author?

    Leave the year empty and the citation uses n.d. (no date in Harvard). With no author, the title moves to the front as each style requires.

    Are the italics kept when I paste?

    Yes. Copying puts formatted text on the clipboard, so Word, Google Docs and Pages keep the italics. Apps that only take plain text get plain text.

    Does it change the capitals in my title?

    No. Titles are kept as you type them. APA and Harvard use sentence case and MLA and Chicago use title case, so type the title the way your style wants.

    Is my reference list saved?

    Yes, in this browser only. Nothing is uploaded. Download the list if you want a copy elsewhere.
