    What is Audio Joiner?

    Audio Joiner combines several audio files into one long track. Add the files, put them in order and save a single MP3, M4A or WAV file.

    You decide how the files meet: straight after each other, with a short silent pause, or with a smooth crossfade. You can also fade in at the start, fade out at the end and even out the volume of files recorded at different levels.

    The sound is decoded and joined in your browser. MP3 is made with the open-source LAME encoder and M4A with the FFmpeg engine, both running on your device.

    What you can use it for

    Playlists and mixes

    Join songs into one continuous mix with crossfades.

    Voice notes

    Combine several voice messages or interview parts into one file.

    Audiobooks and podcasts

    Put chapters or segments together in order.

    Workouts and dance routines

    Build one track from several songs with pauses between them.

    Ringtone and alarm sounds

    Join short clips into a single sound.

    School and church events

    Prepare all the music for a programme as one file.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Audio Joiner

    1. Add your files

      Click the box or drop files onto it. You can add more later; each one shows its length, and files that cannot be read are marked.

    2. Set the order

      Use ↑ and ↓ to move a file and ✕ to remove it. The total length is shown above the settings.

    3. Choose how they join

      Pick "Play straight on", "Add a silent pause" or "Crossfade", and set the number of seconds.

    4. Add fades

      Choose a fade in at the very start and a fade out at the very end if you like.

    5. Even out the volume

      Tick the box if some files are much quieter or louder than others.

    6. Pick the format and quality

      MP3, M4A or WAV. 192 kbps is very good for music.

    7. Join and download

      Press "Join and save", listen in the player and press Download.

    Features

    • Any orderMove files up and down, or remove them.
    • Crossfade or pauseSmooth blends or silent gaps of your chosen length.
    • Fade in and outFor a polished start and end.
    • Volume levellingMatches quiet and loud files, with clipping protection.
    • MP3, M4A or WAVChoose size or full quality.
    • Mixed formatsJoin MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG and FLAC together.
    • PrivateWorks in your browser, even offline once loaded.

    Worked example: a party mix with crossfades

    Omar has five songs for a party, about 18 minutes in total. He drops them on the box, moves the slow song to the end with ↓, and chooses "Crossfade" with 4 seconds. He adds a 2-second fade in and a 10-second fade out, ticks "Even out the volume" because one song is much quieter, and keeps MP3 at 192 kbps. He presses "Join and save" and downloads joined-audio.mp3, which is 16 seconds shorter than the total because of the four crossfades.

    Tips and tricks

    • A crossfade of 3 to 6 seconds works well for music; use a pause of 1 to 2 seconds for speech.
    • Crossfades make the total a little shorter: each joint overlaps by the crossfade length.
    • Choose WAV if you will edit the result further.
    • If a file shows "cannot be read", convert it to MP3 with the Audio Converter and add it again.
    • Very long joins (hours) work best on a computer.

    Problems and how to fix them

    A file says "cannot be read"

    The file may be damaged or in a format your browser cannot decode (such as WMA or AMR). Convert it first, or try Chrome or Edge.

    The Join button stays grey

    You need at least two readable files, and the total must be under 4 hours.

    It ran out of memory

    Join fewer or shorter files at a time, or choose MP3 instead of WAV.

    M4A takes longer the first time

    Saving as M4A downloads the audio engine (about 31 MB) once. MP3 and WAV do not need it.

    Questions and answers

    Are my files uploaded?

    No. The files are joined inside your browser, so they never leave your device.

    Can I join different formats?

    Yes. You can mix MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, FLAC and the sound of videos, as long as your browser can read them. The result is one MP3, M4A or WAV file.

    What is a crossfade?

    The end of one file fades out while the next fades in, so they blend smoothly with no silence between them.

    How many files can I join?

    Up to 50 files and 4 hours in total. Long joins need more memory, which matters most on phones.

    What does "Even out the volume" do?

    It makes quiet files louder and loud files quieter so they play at a similar level, and keeps the result from clipping.

    Which format should I choose?

    MP3 plays everywhere. M4A (AAC) is smaller for the same quality and works well on iPhone. WAV keeps full quality but is large.
