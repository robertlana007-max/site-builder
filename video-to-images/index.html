What is Video to Images?

Video to Images takes still pictures (frames) from a video and saves them as JPG or PNG files.

You can take a picture every few seconds, a set number of pictures spread evenly from start to end, or pictures at the exact times you choose while watching the video. The pictures appear as thumbnails on the page; tap one to save it, or download them all together as a ZIP.

The frames are taken in your browser. Videos your browser can play are handled instantly; other formats use FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. Nothing is uploaded.

What you can use it for

Best moment photo

Grab the perfect frame of a smile, goal or jump from a video.

Thumbnails

Pick a frame for a YouTube thumbnail or a video cover.

Lecture slides

Save the slides of a recorded class, one picture every 30 seconds.

Storyboards and reviews

Get 12 evenly spaced frames to show what a video contains.

Training data and analysis

Extract frames at a steady interval for study or comparison.

Evidence and reports

Save stills from a dash-cam or security video.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Video to Images

  1. Open your video

    Click the box or drop a video onto it. Its name, size, length and picture size appear, with a player.

  2. Choose which pictures

    "One picture every few seconds" takes a frame every N seconds (for example 1 or 0.5). "A set number, spread evenly" takes, say, 10 frames from start to end. "At times I choose" takes frames at times you type, like 0:05, 1:12.5.

  3. Pick exact moments

    With "At times I choose", play or drag the video to a moment, pause, and press "+ Add the player's current time". The time is added to the list.

  4. Choose format and size

    JPG gives small files; PNG is exact but bigger. "Picture size" can keep the video size or make the pictures smaller, down to 320 px wide thumbnails.

  5. Get the pictures

    Press "Get pictures". Thumbnails appear one by one with their time and size. Press Cancel to stop early and keep what was taken.

  6. Save

    Tap a thumbnail to download that picture, or press "Download all (ZIP)". Files are named in order with their time, such as trip-003-0m02_0s.jpg.

Features

  • Three ways to chooseEvery N seconds, a set number, or exact times.
  • JPG or PNGSmall files or exact copies.
  • Picture sizeFull size or smaller, down to thumbnails.
  • Thumbnails on the pageSee every picture with its time before saving.
  • ZIP downloadAll pictures in one file, named in order.
  • Fast and privateUses your browser's own video player; nothing is uploaded.

Worked example: slides from a recorded lecture

Priya has a 45-minute recorded lecture (lecture.mp4) and wants the slides. She drops the video on the box, keeps "One picture every few seconds" and types 30, so the hint says "This makes 90 pictures." She chooses JPG and "Up to 1280 px wide", presses "Get pictures", and after about a minute sees 90 thumbnails. She presses "Download all (ZIP)" and gets lecture-pictures.zip with lecture-001-0m00_0s.jpg to lecture-090-44m30_0s.jpg.

Tips and tricks

  • For slides or a slow video, one picture every 10 to 30 seconds is plenty.
  • Use PNG when you need text in the picture to stay perfectly sharp.
  • Pause on the exact moment and use "Add the player's current time" for the sharpest frame.
  • Choose a smaller picture size when you need many pictures, to keep the ZIP small.
  • The limit is 300 pictures at a time; increase the gap or pick a set number for long videos.

Problems and how to fix them

The hint says too many pictures

The gap is too small for the video length. Choose a bigger gap or "A set number, spread evenly".

A time is "after the end of the video"

Check the length shown above the player and type a time inside it, such as 1:05 or 65.

There is no player

Your browser cannot play this format, so the video engine is used. It downloads once (about 32 MB) and takes a few seconds per picture.

Some pictures are black

The video may start or end with black frames. Start a little later or pick exact times.

Questions and answers

Is my video uploaded?

No. The pictures are taken inside your browser, so the video never leaves your device.

What quality are the pictures?

Each picture is a full frame at the size of the video (or smaller if you choose). PNG is an exact copy of the frame; JPG is much smaller and looks almost the same.

How many pictures can I take?

Up to 300 at a time. For more, run it again on another part of the video.

How do I get a picture at an exact moment?

Choose "At times I choose", play the video, pause at the moment you want and press "Add the player's current time". Repeat for each picture.

Why are some pictures blurry?

Frames from fast movement are often blurred in the video itself. Try a time a fraction of a second earlier or later.

Which video formats work?

Anything your browser can play is handled instantly. Other formats such as some MKV or AVI files use the video engine, which downloads once (about 32 MB) and is slower.
