Guitar Tuner

What is Guitar Tuner?

The Guitar Tuner listens to your instrument through the microphone and shows which note you are playing and how far it is from the right pitch, on a needle gauge in cents.

It knows the strings of many tunings, so it finds the string you are playing automatically and tells you whether to tighten (tune up) or loosen (tune down). There are tunings for guitar, bass, ukulele and violin, and a chromatic mode for any instrument or voice.

Pitch is detected on your device with the YIN method, which is accurate even for the low strings of a bass. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.

What you can use it for

Everyday guitar tuning

Tune to standard E A D G B E before you play.

Alternate tunings

Drop D for rock, Open G for slide, DADGAD for folk.

Bass and ukulele

4 and 5 string bass and G C E A ukulele.

Violin

Tune G D A E, using the fine tuners for the last cents.

Any instrument or voice

Chromatic mode shows the nearest note for flute, piano, singing and more.

Checking intonation

Compare an open string with the 12th fret.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Guitar Tuner

  1. Start the tuner

    Press "Start tuner". Your browser asks to use the microphone; choose Allow. A level bar shows that sound is coming in.

  2. Choose the tuning

    Under "Instrument and tuning", pick your instrument and tuning, or Chromatic for any note. The string buttons change to match.

  3. Play one string

    Pluck one string firmly and let it ring. Keep other strings quiet. The big letter shows the target note, and the matching string button is outlined.

  4. Read the needle

    Left of centre means too low (flat), right means too high (sharp). The line under the note shows the frequency and cents, and the message says "Too low: tighten" or "Too high: loosen".

  5. Turn the peg

    Turn slowly and pluck again. When the needle is in the green zone and the message says "✓ In tune", move to the next string.

  6. Lock a string or hear it

    If a string is very far out, tap its button to lock the tuner to it. Tapping also plays the correct note. Tap again to go back to automatic.

Worked example: Drop D in a minute

Ali wants to play a song in Drop D. He presses "Start tuner", allows the microphone and chooses "Guitar: Drop D (D A D G B E)". He plucks the low E string: the tuner shows D, 2 with "↓ Too high: loosen (tune down)" and +200 cents. He loosens the peg while plucking until the needle moves into the green and the message says "✓ In tune". He checks the other five strings, which are already in tune, and starts playing.

Tips and tricks

  • Tune up to the note rather than down: if you go too high, go below it and come back up. Strings hold their pitch better.
  • Pluck with medium strength and let the string ring; very hard plucks start slightly sharp.
  • Hold the phone close to the sound hole, or use an electric guitar into an audio interface for the most stable reading.
  • In a noisy room, mute the other strings with your hand.
  • Re-check all strings once more after tuning, because changing one string slightly moves the others.

Problems and how to fix them

Microphone access was blocked

Click the lock or microphone icon next to the web address and allow the microphone, then press Start tuner again. On a phone, also allow the microphone for your browser in the phone settings.

The needle jumps around

Background noise or several ringing strings confuse the tuner. Play one string, mute the others, and move closer to the microphone.

It shows the wrong note

The string may be so far out of tune that it is closer to another string. Tap the string you are tuning to lock it.

Nothing happens when I play

Check that the level bar moves. If not, another app may be using the microphone, or the wrong microphone is the default in your computer settings.

Questions and answers

Is the sound from my microphone recorded or uploaded?

No. The sound is analysed live on your device and is never recorded or sent anywhere.

Which tunings are included?

Guitar standard, Drop D, half step down, full step down, Drop C, Open G, Open D, Open E and DADGAD; 4 and 5 string bass; ukulele; violin; and a chromatic mode for any note.

What does "cents" mean?

A cent is one hundredth of a semitone. Within 5 cents of the target sounds in tune to almost everyone.

The tuner shows the wrong string

It picks the string closest to the note it hears. If a string is very far out of tune, tap the string you are tuning to lock it.

Can I hear the right note?

Yes. Tap a string button to hear its reference note for two seconds.

What is the reference pitch?

Most music uses A4 = 440 Hz. Change it if you play with a piano or band tuned slightly differently, for example 432 or 442 Hz.
