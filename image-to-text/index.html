    What is Image to Text (OCR)?

    Image to Text reads the words in a picture and gives them back to you as plain text you can copy, edit and save. This is called OCR (optical character recognition). It works with photos of documents, screenshots, scanned pages, receipts, slides and signs.

    Drop one or many images, or paste a screenshot straight from your clipboard with Ctrl+V. Each image is read in turn and its text is added to one box, with the file name above it so you know which text came from where. You can keep the line breaks exactly as in the picture or join them into normal paragraphs.

    The text reader runs inside your browser. It reads 12 languages: English, Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Turkish and Urdu. The first time, it downloads the reader (about 4 MB) and the data for the language you choose (0.6 to 3 MB) from this site; after that both are reused. Your images are never uploaded anywhere.

    What you can use it for

    Copy text from a screenshot

    Take a screenshot of an error message, a chat or a web page that will not let you select text, paste it here and copy the words.

    Type up a printed page

    Photograph a letter, a page of a book or a handout and get editable text instead of retyping it.

    Grab details from a receipt or invoice

    Pull out the invoice number, totals and dates to paste into a spreadsheet or an expense form.

    Turn slides into notes

    Read the text from photos of presentation slides or a whiteboard to start your notes.

    Make a scanned document searchable

    Get the text out of scanned images so you can search, quote or translate it.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Image to Text (OCR)

    1. Pick the language of the text

      Open "Language of the text" and choose the language written in your picture, such as Spanish, Arabic, Hindi or Chinese (Simplified). The size next to each name is a one-time download. If the text mixes your language with English words, tick "The text also has English words in it". Arabic and Urdu results are shown right to left.

    2. Add your images

      Click "Click or drop images here" and choose one or more pictures, drag them onto the box, or copy a screenshot and press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac) anywhere on the page. JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP and GIF all work; for an animated GIF only the first frame is read.

    3. Watch the progress

      Each image appears in a list with its status: Waiting, Reading, then Done or No text found. A blue bar shows how far the current image has got. The first image takes longer because the text reader is loading.

    4. Check the options

      "Line breaks" keeps each line as in the picture, or joins lines into paragraphs (good for pasting into an email or document). "Orientation" is Automatic by default, which tries the picture turned sideways or upside down if it cannot read it upright.

    5. Clean up hard pictures

      "Clean up the image first" is on by default. It makes the picture grayscale, stretches the contrast, turns light-on-dark text into dark-on-light and enlarges small pictures. Turn it off only if the results get worse.

    6. Read and fix the text

      The recognised text appears in the box. Read it through and correct any mistakes; you can type in the box directly.

    7. Copy or download

      Press "Copy text" to put everything on the clipboard, or "Download .txt" to save it as a text file. "Clear" empties the box so you can start again.

    Features

    • Paste screenshotsPress Ctrl+V with a screenshot on the clipboard and it is read at once, no saving needed.
    • 12 languagesEnglish, Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Turkish and Urdu, using Tesseract's open-source language data (Apache 2.0). Each language downloads only when you choose it.
    • Many images in one goAdd several files; they are read one after another and the results are added together.
    • Keep or join linesChoose exact line breaks or tidy paragraphs, with words split by a hyphen at the end of a line put back together.
    • Turned picturesAutomatic orientation tries 90°, 180° and 270° when the upright reading looks poor, and tells you which way it used.
    • Image clean-upGrayscale, contrast stretching, dark-mode inversion and enlarging small text all help the reader.
    • Confidence shownEach file shows how sure the reader was, so you know which results to check most carefully.
    • PrivateEverything runs in your browser and works offline once the reader has loaded.

    Worked example: copying an invoice number from a screenshot

    Omar receives a PDF invoice as an image in a chat app and needs the invoice number and total for his expense form. He takes a screenshot, opens Image to Text and presses Ctrl+V. The list shows "pasted-image-1.png ... Done · 94% sure" and the box shows "Invoice number 4821" and "Total due: 315.40 dollars" on separate lines. He selects the number, copies it into his form, then presses "Download .txt" to keep a copy of the whole text.

    Tips and tricks

    • Crop the picture to just the text you need before reading it. Less clutter means fewer mistakes and faster results.
    • Photos work best when the page is flat, evenly lit and in focus. Take the picture straight on, not at an angle.
    • Screenshots give the best results of all, because the letters are perfectly sharp.
    • If a picture is sideways and Automatic does not catch it, choose the right Orientation by hand and add the file again.
    • Always check numbers, names and email addresses: a 0 and an O, or a 1 and an l, are easily confused.

    Problems and how to fix them

    It says "No text found"

    The writing may be too small, blurred, handwritten or decorative. Try a sharper or closer photo, crop to the text, or turn off the clean-up option and try again.

    The text has many mistakes

    Use a clearer picture with more contrast. Very small text in a big photo should be cropped first, so the letters fill more of the image.

    My iPhone photo will not open

    HEIC photos cannot be opened by most browsers. Change the iPhone camera setting to Most Compatible, or convert the photo to JPG first with the Image Converter.

    Pasting does nothing

    Make sure you copied an image, not a file name. On a phone, save the screenshot and choose it with the box instead.

    The first image takes a long time

    The text reader and the data for your language load the first time you use the tool. Later images and later visits are much faster.

    Questions and answers

    Are my images uploaded?

    No. The text is read by your own browser. The reader and the data for your language are downloaded from this site the first time (about 4 MB plus 0.6 to 3 MB per language) and are then kept for offline use.

    Which languages can it read?

    English, Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Turkish and Urdu. If your text mixes a language with English words, tick "The text also has English words in it".

    Can it read handwriting?

    Only very neat printed handwriting, and not reliably. It is made for printed text, screenshots and scans.

    What if my picture is sideways or upside down?

    With Orientation set to Automatic, the tool tries the picture turned 90, 180 and 270 degrees when the upright reading looks poor, and keeps the best result. You can also set the orientation by hand.

    How accurate is it?

    On clear screenshots and flat, sharp scans it is usually close to perfect. Blurry, angled or low-contrast photos produce more mistakes, so always check names and numbers.
