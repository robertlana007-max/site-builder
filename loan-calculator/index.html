What is Loan Calculator?

The Loan Calculator shows how much you will pay each month on a fixed-rate loan, how much of that is interest, and how the balance goes down year by year. You type three numbers: the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the term in years.

It is useful for car loans, personal loans, student loans and any loan where you pay the same amount every month until it is gone. You can try different rates and terms before you talk to a lender, so you know what to expect.

The tool updates as you type and all the maths runs in your browser. Results are estimates and not financial advice.

What you can use it for

Compare loan offers

Type the rate and term from each offer and see which one has the lower monthly payment and the lower total interest.

Plan a car purchase

Check if the monthly payment on a car loan fits your budget before you visit the dealer.

Choose a loan term

See how a shorter term raises the monthly payment but cuts the total interest you pay.

Check a lender’s quote

Make sure the monthly payment a lender gives you matches the amount, rate and term on the paper.

See how fast the balance falls

Use the yearly breakdown table to see how much you still owe at the end of each year.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Loan Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount

    Type the amount you will borrow in the "Loan amount" box. This is the money you receive, not the total you will pay back. The default is 250000, so replace it with your own number.

  2. Enter the interest rate

    Type the yearly rate in "Interest rate (% per year)", for example 6.5 for 6.5%. Do not type the % sign. A rate of 0 is allowed and gives an interest-free loan.

  3. Enter the term

    Type how many years you will pay in "Term (years)". You can use part years, such as 2.5; the tool turns the term into months and rounds to the nearest whole month. The longest term allowed is 100 years.

  4. Read the three results

    "Monthly payment" is the fixed amount you pay each month. "Total interest" is everything you pay above the loan amount. "Total paid" is the monthly payment times the number of months.

  5. Read the yearly breakdown

    The "Yearly breakdown" table has one row per year. "Principal" is how much of the loan you paid off that year, "Interest" is the interest paid that year, and "Balance" is what you still owe at the end of the year.

  6. Try other numbers

    Every result changes as soon as you edit a box, so you can quickly test a lower rate, a smaller amount or a shorter term and compare.

Features

  • Standard loan formulaThe monthly payment is: amount × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r) to the power −n), where r is the yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 and n is the number of months. When the rate is 0 it is simply amount ÷ months.
  • Yearly amortization tableEach month the tool works out interest on the remaining balance, takes the rest of the payment off the balance, and adds the months up into yearly rows.
  • Total interest and total paidSee the full cost of the loan, not just the monthly figure.
  • Part-year termsTerms like 1.5 or 3.25 years work, because the term is converted to whole months.
  • Clear error messageIf the amount or term is missing, the rate is negative or the term is over 100 years, a message tells you what to fix.
  • Live resultsThere is no calculate button; the numbers update while you type.

Worked example: a 20,000 car loan

You borrow 20000 at 8% per year for 5 years. The monthly rate is 8 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00667 and there are 60 months. The "Monthly payment" is 405.53, "Total paid" is 24,331.67 and "Total interest" is 4,331.67. In the table, year 1 shows Principal 3,388.80, Interest 1,477.53 and Balance 16,611.20, while year 5 shows Principal 4,661.86, Interest only 204.47 and Balance 0.00. This shows that early payments are mostly interest and later payments are mostly principal.

Tips and tricks

  • Use the exact rate from your loan offer, not a rounded number; even 0.25% changes the total interest.
  • Compare terms side by side: shorter terms cost more per month but much less in total.
  • The tool does not include fees, so add any loan setup fee to your own cost estimate.
  • If you plan to pay extra each month, the real payoff will be faster than the table shows.
  • Make sure you enter the yearly rate, not a monthly rate.
  • Results are rounded to 2 decimals, so totals can differ from a bank statement by a few cents.

Problems and how to fix them

The results show a dash and a red message

The amount and term must be above 0, the rate must be 0 or more, and the term must be 100 years or less. Fix the box that is empty or out of range.

The monthly payment looks far too high

Check that you typed the rate as a percent (6.5) and not a decimal written as a large number, and that the term is in years, not months.

My lender’s payment is a little different

Lenders may add fees, insurance or use a different day count. This tool uses a simple monthly rate of yearly rate ÷ 12.

The last year in the table is short

If the term is not a whole number of years, the last row covers only the remaining months.

Questions and answers

What formula is used?

The standard fixed-rate amortisation formula: payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly payments.

Does this include taxes, insurance or fees?

No. It covers principal and interest only. Your lender may add other charges.

Is this financial advice?

No. The figures are estimates to help you compare options. Confirm exact numbers with your lender.
