What is Excel to PDF?

Excel to PDF turns a spreadsheet into a tidy, printable PDF table. It reads Excel workbooks (.xlsx, .xlsm, .xls), OpenDocument spreadsheets (.ods) and CSV or TSV text files, and lays the rows out on real pages with borders, a bold header row and page numbers.

It is handy when you need to send figures to someone who does not have a spreadsheet program, attach a price list or timetable to an email, or print a sheet without fighting with print areas and page breaks.

Everything happens in your browser. The file is read with the SheetJS library and the PDF is built with pdf-lib on your own device, so your data is never uploaded.

What you can use it for

Share reports

Send monthly sales, budgets or survey results as a PDF that looks the same on every phone and computer.

Print a sheet neatly

Get clean pages with the header row repeated on every page and no cut-off columns.

Turn a CSV export into a document

Bank statements, app exports and system reports often come as CSV. Make them readable as a PDF table.

Price lists and timetables

Publish a list or schedule that people can open but not accidentally edit.

Attach data to forms

Many portals accept only PDF uploads. Convert the sheet first.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Excel to PDF

  1. Add your spreadsheet

    Click the box that says "Click or drop a spreadsheet here", or drag the file onto it. Excel, OpenDocument and CSV files all work.

  2. Choose the sheets

    If the workbook has more than one sheet, the Sheets list shows "All sheets" and each sheet by name. Empty sheets are left out automatically.

  3. Set paper size and orientation

    Pick A4, Letter, Legal or A3. Orientation "Automatic" switches to landscape when the table is too wide for a tall page.

  4. Decide how wide tables fit

    "Shrink to fit the page width" makes the text smaller so all columns fit across. "Keep text size, continue columns on extra pages" keeps the text readable and prints the remaining columns on following pages.

  5. Pick the look

    Choose the text size, and tick or untick the header row, cell borders, shaded rows and the sheet name with page numbers.

  6. Check the preview

    The preview shows the first 30 rows of the chosen sheet and how many rows and columns it has.

  7. Make and download

    Click "Make PDF". When the green message says "Your PDF is ready", click "Download PDF".

Features

  • Excel, ODS and CSVReads .xlsx, .xlsm, .xls, .ods, .csv, .tsv and .txt files, and detects commas, semicolons, tabs and pipes in text files.
  • Values as you see themNumbers, dates and currencies keep the formatting from your sheet, and formulas show their last calculated result.
  • Smart column widthsColumns are sized to their content, long text wraps inside its cell, and numbers line up on the right.
  • Header row on every pageThe first row is printed in bold on a shaded band and repeated at the top of each page.
  • Wide-table handlingShrink to fit the page width, or keep the text size and continue columns on extra pages.
  • Merged cells and fill coloursMerged cells span their columns and solid cell colours are kept. Hidden rows and columns are left out.
  • World languagesLetters missing from the built-in font, such as Chinese, Arabic or Hindi, are drawn by your browser so they still appear.

Worked example: a 3,000-row order list

Ana exports orders.xlsx with 3,000 rows and 9 columns. She leaves Paper size on A4, Orientation on Automatic and Wide tables on "Shrink to fit the page width". The tool sees that 9 columns are too wide for portrait, turns the pages to landscape and keeps the text at about 8 pt. She clicks Make PDF and gets about 70 pages, each starting with the bold header row and ending with "Page 12 of 70" in the corner.

Tips and tricks

  • For very wide sheets, try Landscape with "Keep text size" so the text stays easy to read.
  • Delete helper columns and empty rows in your spreadsheet first; the PDF only shows the area that holds data.
  • Use "Shade every other row" for long lists so the eye can follow each row.
  • If accents look wrong in a CSV, save it from your spreadsheet program as "CSV UTF-8" and try again.
  • To print only part of a sheet, copy that part to a new sheet and choose it in the Sheets list.

Problems and how to fix them

The message says the file is password protected

Open it in your spreadsheet program, remove the password, save, and drop it in again.

The text is very small

The table has many columns. Choose Landscape, a bigger paper size such as A3, or "Keep text size, continue columns on extra pages".

A formula column is empty

The file was saved without calculated values (some apps do this). Open and save it once in Excel or LibreOffice, then convert again.

Charts or pictures are missing

Only cell values and fill colours are copied. Use your spreadsheet program's own Save as PDF for charts.

Questions and answers

Is my spreadsheet uploaded?

No. The file is read and turned into a PDF by your browser, on your own device.

What happens to wide tables?

By default the text is made smaller so every column fits the page width, and the page turns to landscape when needed. You can instead keep the text size and let extra columns continue on more pages.

Are formulas, colours and charts kept?

Formulas show their last calculated values and cell fill colours are kept. Charts, pictures, fonts and conditional formatting are not copied into the PDF.

Does it work with Chinese, Arabic or Hindi text?

Yes. Letters that the built-in font does not have are drawn by your browser, so they show correctly in the PDF.

Is there a size limit?

Up to about 1.5 million cells at once. Big sheets with tens of thousands of rows work, but can take a minute on a phone.

Can I convert a password-protected workbook?

No. Open it in your spreadsheet program, remove the password, save, and try again.
