What is Speech to Text?

Speech to Text turns your voice into written text while you speak. It is voice typing in your browser, with no app or account needed.

You can dictate in more than 90 languages and accents, from English (US, UK, India, Pakistan and more) to Urdu, Hindi, Arabic, Spanish, French, Chinese, Bengali, Indonesian and Swahili. Words appear first in grey while you speak and are then added to the text box, where you can edit them. Punctuation can be spoken in English or added with buttons in every language.

Recognition is done by your browser's own speech service (Google in Chrome, Microsoft in Edge, Apple in Safari), so it needs an internet connection. The text stays on this page until you copy or save it.

What you can use it for

Notes and messages

Speak a long message or note instead of typing on a small phone keyboard.

Essays and homework

Get a first draft down quickly by talking, then edit it.

Urdu, Hindi and Arabic typing

Write in your language without a special keyboard.

Accessibility

Type without a keyboard if typing is hard or painful.

Meeting and lecture notes

Dictate key points as you think of them.

Language practice

Check your pronunciation by seeing whether the words are understood.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Speech to Text

  1. Choose your language

    Under "Language you will speak" pick your language and accent, such as "Urdu (Pakistan) – اردو" or "English (India)". It starts on your browser's language.

  2. Start dictation

    Press "Start dictation". The first time, your browser asks to use the microphone; choose Allow. The red dot and "Listening… speak now" show it is working.

  3. Speak

    Talk clearly at a normal speed. Words being heard appear in the grey box first, then move into the text box when the sentence is understood.

  4. Add punctuation

    In English, say "comma", "full stop", "question mark", "new line" or "new paragraph". In every language, tap the buttons under the text, such as . , ? or ۔ ، ؟ for Urdu. Sentences start with a capital letter automatically where the language uses them.

  5. Edit

    Click in the text box at any time to correct a word. The word and character count updates as you go.

  6. Stop and save

    Press "Stop dictation". Then press "Copy text" to paste it somewhere else, or "Download .txt" to save a text file.

Worked example: an Urdu message

Fatima wants to send a long message in Urdu but finds the Urdu keyboard slow. She chooses "Urdu (Pakistan) – اردو" and presses "Start dictation". She says her message in two sentences, tapping the ۔ button at the end of each sentence and ؟ after her question. She fixes one word by clicking on it in the box, presses "Stop dictation", then "Copy text", and pastes the message into WhatsApp.

Tips and tricks

  • Use a headset or speak close to the microphone in a quiet room for the best results.
  • Speak in full sentences at a normal pace; pausing between every word makes recognition worse.
  • Choose your exact accent, such as English (India) or Spanish (Mexico), for better accuracy.
  • Read the text once before sending: names and rare words are often misheard.
  • Download your text as .txt regularly when dictating something long, so nothing is lost if the tab is closed.

Problems and how to fix them

It says your browser does not have speech recognition

Firefox and some other browsers do not support it. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer or Android, or Safari on Mac or iPhone. On iPhone you can also use the microphone key on the keyboard.

Microphone access was blocked

Click the lock or microphone icon next to the web address, allow the microphone, and press Start dictation again.

The speech service could not be reached

Speech recognition needs the internet. Check your connection. The Brave browser blocks this service; use Chrome or Edge.

The words are wrong or in the wrong script

Make sure the language menu matches the language you are speaking. For Urdu choose Urdu, not Hindi, to get Urdu script.

It stopped after a silence

Browsers end listening after a long silence. Press Start dictation to carry on; your text is kept.

Questions and answers

Which browsers does it work in?

Chrome and Edge on computers and Android phones, and Safari on Mac, iPhone and iPad. Firefox does not support speech recognition yet.

Which languages can I use?

More than 90 languages and regional accents, including English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Arabic, Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Indonesian and Turkish. The list starts with your browser's language.

Is my voice sent anywhere?

Your browser's speech service turns speech into text: Chrome uses Google, Edge uses Microsoft and Safari uses Apple, so an internet connection is needed. This website does not receive or store your voice or text.

How do I add commas and full stops?

In English, say "comma", "full stop" or "period", "question mark", "new line" or "new paragraph". In any language you can tap the punctuation buttons, including ۔ ، ؟ for Urdu and । for Hindi.

Why does it stop by itself?

Browsers end listening after a pause. The tool starts listening again automatically until you press Stop, or stops after a long silence.

Can I edit the text?

Yes. The text box is a normal text area: click anywhere to fix words while you dictate or afterwards.
