What is Dead Pixel & Screen Test?

Dead Pixel & Screen Test fills your whole screen with solid colours, gradients and patterns so you can spot dead, stuck or bright pixels, uneven backlight and colour banding.

You can step through black, white, red, green, blue, grey and more with a click or the arrow keys. A stuck pixel fixer flashes colours over a small area, which sometimes revives stuck pixels; it shows a clear warning first because it flashes.

It also shows your screen resolution, pixel ratio and an estimate of the refresh rate.

What you can use it for

Check a new monitor, laptop or phone

Look for dead pixels while you can still return it.

Find stuck pixels

A pixel that stays red, green or blue shows up on a black or white screen.

Check backlight bleed

Bright patches at the edges show up on a black screen in a dark room.

See colour banding

Gradients show whether colours change smoothly.

Check your refresh rate

See whether your 120 Hz or 144 Hz screen really runs at that speed.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Dead Pixel & Screen Test

  1. Clean the screen

    Wipe off dust first so you do not mistake it for a pixel fault.

  2. Start the test

    Press "Start full-screen test" or click one of the colour tiles.

  3. Step through the colours

    Click, tap or press → or Space for the next colour, ← for the previous one.

  4. Look closely

    On black look for bright dots; on white, red, green and blue look for dark or wrongly coloured dots.

  5. Exit

    Press Esc or the Stop button.

  6. Try the fixer if needed

    Read the warning, tick the box, press "Start stuck pixel fixer" and drag the flashing square over the stuck pixel for 10 to 20 minutes.

Worked example: checking a new monitor

Maria unpacks a new 27-inch monitor. She turns off the room lights, opens the test and steps through the colours. On the black screen she sees one tiny green dot. She ticks the warning box and runs the stuck pixel fixer over it for 10 minutes. The dot is gone; if it had stayed she would have returned the monitor.

Tips and tricks

  • Test in a dark room for black screens and in normal light for white ones.
  • Use a magnifying glass or your phone camera zoom to look at a suspect dot.
  • A dead pixel (always black) usually cannot be fixed; a stuck pixel (always one colour) sometimes can.
  • Check the shop's dead pixel policy and test within the return period.
  • The refresh rate estimate can read low on battery saving or in a background tab.

Problems and how to fix them

The test does not fill the whole screen

Your browser may not allow full screen (for example on iPhone). The test still covers the page; hide the browser bars by rotating or scrolling.

The refresh rate looks wrong

Close other tabs, plug in your laptop and press "Measure refresh rate again". Check the rate in your system display settings.

The stuck pixel did not change

Run the fixer longer (20–30 minutes) or try again another day. Some pixels cannot be revived.

I feel unwell while the fixer runs

Stop at once with Esc and look away. Do not use the fixer if flashing lights affect you.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between a dead and a stuck pixel?

A dead pixel stays black on every colour. A stuck pixel stays one colour, such as red or green, and can sometimes be fixed.

Does the stuck pixel fixer work?

Sometimes. Running fast flashing colours over a stuck pixel for 10 to 30 minutes can revive it. It cannot fix dead pixels.

Is the fixer safe?

It flashes bright colours quickly, so do not use it if you or anyone watching has photosensitive epilepsy. It only starts after you confirm the warning.

How accurate is the refresh rate?

It is an estimate from how often the browser draws. Battery saving or a busy computer can make it read lower.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. Tap to change colours. On iPhone the test fills the page instead of using true full screen.
