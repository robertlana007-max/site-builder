What is Word Frequency Counter?

The Word Frequency Counter ranks the words and short phrases in a text by how often they appear. You see each word with its count, its share of all words and a bar, so the most used terms stand out at a glance.

Writers use it to spot words they overuse, students to check essays and study texts, marketers and SEO writers to check keyword use, researchers to explore interviews and survey answers, and teachers to build vocabulary lists.

It works with most of the world's languages and runs entirely in your browser, so your text stays private.

What you can use it for

Find overused words

Spot words you repeat too often in an essay, story or report and pick alternatives.

Check keywords

See how often a keyword or 2-word phrase appears in an article or product page.

Analyse feedback

Paste survey answers or reviews and see which topics people mention most.

Build vocabulary lists

Get the most frequent words from a text in a language you are learning.

Study speeches and books

Compare the favourite words and phrases of different authors or speakers.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Word Frequency Counter

  1. Add your text

    Paste it into Your text or press Open text file. The list updates as you type.

  2. Choose words or phrases

    Single words counts each word. 2-word and 3-word phrases count word pairs and triples that appear side by side.

  3. Set the filters

    Skip common English words hides words like the, and and of. Shortest word to count hides very short words. Skip numbers hides years and amounts.

  4. Skip your own words

    Type words to ignore in Also skip these words, separated by commas, such as names or words from another language.

  5. Read the results

    The table shows rank, word, count, share and a bar. The counters above show total words, unique words and average word length.

  6. Export the list

    Choose how many rows to show, then click Download CSV or Copy list.

Features

  • Words and phrasesCount single words, 2-word and 3-word phrases.
  • Works in most languagesHandles accents and non-Latin alphabets, and splits Chinese, Japanese and Thai into words.
  • Common-word filterHides about 200 frequent English words, plus any words you add.
  • Clean phrase countsPhrases never cross sentence ends, commas or line breaks.
  • CSV exportDownload a spreadsheet with rank, word, count and percentage.
  • Fast on long textsCounts whole books in a few seconds.
  • PrivateNothing is uploaded.

Worked example: checking an essay

You paste a 1,200-word essay about climate. With Single words and Skip common English words on, the top rows are climate 24, energy 15, change 14 and carbon 9. Switch to 2-word phrases and you see climate change 13 and renewable energy 6. You decide to replace a few uses of "climate change" with "global warming" and click Download CSV to keep the list.

Tips and tricks

  • Set Shortest word to count to 1 letter if short words matter in your language.
  • Untick Ignore upper/lower case to tell names like "Rose" apart from the flower "rose".
  • For non-English text, add the most common words of that language to Also skip these words.
  • Use 2-word phrases to find set expressions and keyword phrases that single words miss.
  • Choose All in Show before downloading to get every word in the CSV.

Problems and how to fix them

The list is empty

Every word may be filtered out. Lower the shortest word length or untick Skip common English words.

Words with apostrophes look split

Straight and curly apostrophes are both treated as part of the word, so don't and don’t count as the same word. Other symbols split words.

My phrase is missing

Phrases that start or end with a common word, or that cross a comma or sentence end, are not counted. Untick Skip common English words to include them.

The CSV shows strange characters

Open it with your spreadsheet's import option and choose UTF-8. Most apps detect it automatically.

Questions and answers

Does it work for languages other than English?

Yes. Words are found in any alphabet, including accented Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic, Urdu and Hindi. Chinese, Japanese and Thai, which are written without spaces, are split into words by your browser. The common-word filter is for English only, but you can add your own words to skip.

How are phrases counted?

Phrases are runs of 2 or 3 words next to each other that do not cross a full stop, comma or line break. A phrase may have a common word in the middle, like "piece of cake", but not at the start or end.

What does Share mean?

The percentage of all counted words (or phrases) that this word makes up, after filters are applied.

Will the CSV open in Excel?

Yes. The file is UTF-8 with a marker that tells Excel and other spreadsheet apps to show every alphabet correctly.

Is my text uploaded?

No. All counting happens in your browser.
