What is Inflation Calculator?

The Inflation Calculator shows how rising prices change the value of money. It answers two questions at once: what will the same things cost in the future, and what will today’s money really be able to buy?

You choose the inflation rate, so it works for any country. A chart and a yearly table show how costs climb and buying power falls over time.

What you can use it for

Plan future expenses

Estimate what school fees, a wedding or a car might cost in 10 years.

Check a savings target

See how much a retirement goal needs to grow just to keep its value.

Judge a pay rise

Compare your raise with inflation to see if you are better off.

Understand cash savings

See how money kept in cash loses value each year.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Inflation Calculator

  1. Choose your currency

    Pick it from the list or type your own symbol.

  2. Enter the amount

    Type a price or sum in today’s money, for example 1000.

  3. Enter the inflation rate

    Type the yearly rate, for example 3 for 3%. Use a recent average for your country or your own estimate.

  4. Enter the years

    Type how far ahead to look. Part years like 2.5 work.

  5. Read the results

    Future cost shows what the same things will cost. What your money will buy shows today’s value of the same amount in the future. The percentages show the price rise and the buying power lost.

  6. Use the chart and table

    Tap the chart to see any year, or scroll the table.

Features

  • Two answers at onceFuture cost and buying power from the same inputs.
  • Any rate, any countryType your own rate, including negative rates.
  • Doubling timeShows roughly how long it takes for prices to double.
  • Chart and yearly tableSee the change year by year.
  • Copy summaryShare the result easily.

Worked example: 1,000 at 3% for 10 years

The growth factor is 1.03^10 = 1.3439. Something that costs 1,000 today would cost about 1,343.92 in 10 years. Put the other way, 1,000 then buys only what 744.09 buys today, a loss of about 25.6% in buying power. At 3% a year, prices double about every 23.4 years.

Tips and tricks

  • Use a higher rate for things that usually rise faster, like school fees or health care.
  • Compare savings interest with inflation: if interest is lower, your money is losing value.
  • For long plans, try a few rates to see a range of outcomes.
  • Pair it with the SIP or Savings Goal calculators to set goals in future money.

Problems and how to fix them

The results show a dash

The amount must be 0 or more, the rate between −99 and 1000%, and years from 0 to 200.

The numbers are huge

Very high rates over many years grow extremely fast. Check the rate and years.

It does not match official figures

Official calculators use actual yearly price data. This tool uses one steady rate.

Questions and answers

How is it calculated?

Future cost = amount × (1 + rate)^years. Buying power = amount ÷ (1 + rate)^years.

Which inflation rate should I use?

Use your country’s recent average from its statistics office or central bank, or a guess for the future. Rates differ a lot between countries and years.

Can I use a negative rate?

Yes. A negative rate means deflation: prices fall and money buys more.

Does it use official price data?

No. You choose the rate, so it works for any country and any period.

How long until prices double?

The tool shows it. A quick rule: 70 ÷ the inflation rate is roughly the years for prices to double.
