What is Read Along?

Read Along reads any text out loud with your device's voices and highlights each word and sentence as it is spoken, so you can follow with your eyes.

Paste text or open a .txt, Word (.docx) or PDF file. It detects the language and picks a matching voice, and you can click any word to start reading from there.

A big, calm reading view helps people with dyslexia, low vision or reading difficulties: adjust text size, line spacing, background colour and font (including OpenDyslexic), and use a reading ruler that dims the lines around the one you are reading.

What you can use it for

Dyslexia support

Hear and see the words together, in a comfortable layout.

Learning a language

Follow the highlighted words to connect sound and spelling.

Homework and study

Listen to chapters and notes while reading along.

Proofreading

Hear your own writing to catch mistakes.

Tired eyes

Rest your eyes and listen with big text.

Children's reading

Practise reading with a voice to follow.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Read Along

  1. Add your text

    Type or paste it, or open a .txt, .docx or PDF file.

  2. Check the language and voice

    Detect automatically usually picks the right one. Choose another voice from the list if you like.

  3. Set the speed

    Slow down for learning or speed up for skimming.

  4. Press Read aloud

    Each word lights up as it is spoken, and the sentence is shaded.

  5. Jump around

    Click or tap any word to start reading from it. Press Pause to pause and Resume to carry on.

  6. Make it comfortable

    Open Reading view to change text size, spacing, colour and font, and turn on the reading ruler.

  7. Use the big view

    Press Big reading view for a full-screen page. Press Close, Escape or your back button to leave.

Features

  • Word and sentence highlightingIn sync with the voice, using its word timing.
  • Sentence fallbackVoices that do not report words still highlight each sentence.
  • Many languagesAuto-detects the language and chooses a matching voice.
  • FilesOpens .txt, Word .docx and PDF files on your device.
  • Dyslexia-friendly viewText size, line spacing, tinted backgrounds, OpenDyslexic font, extra spacing.
  • Reading rulerDims everything except the line you are reading.
  • PrivateText stays on your device and is remembered for next time.

Worked example: a school PDF

Maya opens a 4-page history PDF. The tool joins the lines into paragraphs and detects English. She sets speed to 0.8×, chooses a cream background, 28 px text and the OpenDyslexic font, and turns on the ruler. She presses Read aloud and follows the yellow word. When she loses her place she taps the sentence she wants and it carries on from there.

Tips and tricks

  • Install extra voices in your device settings for more languages and better quality.
  • Use a slower speed (0.7× to 0.9×) when learning a language.
  • Cream or soft-blue backgrounds reduce glare for many readers.
  • Wide line spacing and short lines are easier to follow.
  • For scanned PDFs, first get the text with the Image to Text tool.

Problems and how to fix them

There is no voice for my language

Your device has no voice installed for it. Add one in Settings (Windows: Time & language › Speech; Android: Text-to-speech; iPhone and Mac: Accessibility › Spoken Content).

Only the sentence is highlighted, not each word

Some voices, often on Android, do not report word timing. The sentence is highlighted instead.

Reading stops after Pause on my phone

Some phones drop paused speech. Press Resume and it starts again from the last word.

My PDF shows no text

It is probably a scan. Use Image to Text (OCR) first, then paste the text here.

Questions and answers

Which languages work?

Any language your device has a voice for. The tool detects the language of your text (for example English, Spanish, Arabic, Hindi, Chinese or Russian) and picks a matching voice. You can choose another voice too.

Why is only the sentence highlighted?

Some voices, often on Android, do not tell the browser when each word starts. Then the whole sentence is highlighted instead.

Can it read PDF and Word files?

Yes, .txt, .docx and PDF files with real text. Scanned PDFs are pictures, so use the Image to Text tool first. Old .doc files need to be saved as .docx.

Is it good for dyslexia?

It offers features many readers with dyslexia find helpful: hearing and seeing words together, big text, wide spacing, tinted backgrounds, the OpenDyslexic font and a reading ruler. Try settings to find what suits you.

Is my text uploaded?

No. Files are read on your device, and voices built into your device speak the text. A few voices marked "online" use your browser’s speech service.

Can I download the audio?

No. Browsers do not let websites record their built-in voices. Use the voices live on the page.
