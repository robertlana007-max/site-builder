How to measure

Washing machineLay the phone flat on top during the spin. If it rocks or "walks", adjust the feet until they all touch the floor, then measure again and compare RMS.
Car at idlePut the phone on the seat or a flat part of the dashboard with the engine running and nothing else on. Compare with another day or another car. A rough or uneven idle shows as a jumpy graph.
Fan wobblePut the phone on the fan's base or, for a ceiling fan, on a firm spot on the housing only if it is safe and the fan is off when you place it. The main frequency usually matches the blade speed (rpm ÷ 60).

Feeling guide for people (based on ISO 2631-1, used for comfort in vehicles; phone readings are unweighted, so treat this as rough): below 0.32 m/s² not uncomfortable · 0.32–0.63 a little uncomfortable · 0.5–1 fairly uncomfortable · 0.8–1.6 uncomfortable · 1.25–2.5 very uncomfortable · above 2 extremely uncomfortable.

Limits: phones usually report motion only 50–100 times a second, so they can only see vibrations up to about half of that (25–50 Hz, about 1,500–3,000 rpm). Faster vibrations can show up at a wrong, lower frequency. A phone is not a calibrated vibration meter; use it to compare before and after.

What is Vibration Meter?

Vibration Meter uses your phone's accelerometer to measure how strongly something shakes. It removes gravity with a high-pass filter and shows a live graph of the vibration strength in m/s² or g, with the peak and the RMS (average strength).

It also finds the main vibration frequency with an FFT over the last few seconds, shown in Hz and rpm, and draws the frequency spectrum.

Record sessions, name them, compare before and after, and export the raw samples as CSV or the session list as JSON or CSV. Everything stays in your browser.

What you can use it for

Washing machines

Check spin balance and see if levelling the feet reduced the shaking.

Car idle

Compare engine vibration at idle on different days or cars.

Fans

Find wobble in a desk, floor or ceiling fan and its speed in rpm.

Machines and tools

Compare a pump, compressor or motor before and after a repair.

Buildings

See footfall or traffic vibration on a floor or shelf.

Science lessons

Explore frequency, resonance and RMS with real data.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Vibration Meter

  1. Start

    Press Start and allow motion access if asked (iPhone: Allow when Safari asks about Motion and Orientation).

  2. Place the phone

    Lay the phone flat on the machine and let go. Holding it adds your own movement.

  3. Read the numbers

    The big number is the RMS over the last second. Peak is the strongest moment, and Main frequency is the strongest vibration in Hz and rpm.

  4. Record a session

    Type a name, press Record session, then Stop recording. The session is saved with its RMS, peak and frequency.

  5. Compare and export

    Compare sessions in the list. Download the last recording as CSV, or export all sessions as JSON or CSV, and print the list.

  6. Calibrate

    Put the phone on a still table and press Calibrate: it removes sensor noise and corrects the scale using gravity.

Features

  • Live graphVibration strength for the last 10 seconds.
  • Peak and RMSIn m/s² or g, with sensor noise removed after calibration.
  • Main frequencyFFT with a Hann window, shown in Hz and rpm.
  • Spectrum viewSee all frequencies at once.
  • Session recordingUp to 20 minutes, with raw CSV export.
  • Saved sessionsName, compare, export, import and print, stored only on your device.
  • CalibrationNoise floor and gravity-based scale correction.

Worked example: a wobbling washing machine

Tom's washing machine walks across the floor on spin. He lays his phone on top, names the session "Spin before" and records 30 seconds: RMS 1.9 m/s², peak 4.7 m/s², main frequency 13.3 Hz (800 rpm), which matches the spin speed. After adjusting the feet so all four touch the floor he records "Spin after": RMS 0.6 m/s². He exports both sessions as CSV for his notes.

Tips and tricks

  • Always place the phone the same way on the same spot when comparing.
  • Use the 1 Hz filter for machines; use 2–5 Hz if slow rocking hides the vibration.
  • An 8-second frequency window gives a more precise frequency for steady machines.
  • Calibrate once on a solid table to remove the sensor's own noise.
  • Keep phones safe: never place one on a fan blade or any moving part.

Problems and how to fix them

It says no motion sensor answered

Computers usually have no accelerometer. Open the page on a phone or tablet.

The frequency looks wrong

Phones sample about 50–100 times a second, so vibrations faster than about half of that show up at a wrong, lower frequency.

Main frequency says none clear

The vibration is too weak or too irregular. Record longer, or use a longer frequency window.

Import says it is not a sessions file

Only JSON files made with Export sessions (JSON) can be imported. Your existing sessions are not changed.

Questions and answers

How accurate is a phone vibration meter?

Good enough to compare before and after, or one machine with another. Phone accelerometers are not calibrated instruments, and calibration here only removes sensor noise and corrects the scale using gravity.

What is RMS?

Root mean square is the average strength of the vibration over time. It is steadier than the peak and is what most vibration standards use.

What frequencies can it measure?

Phones report motion about 50 to 100 times a second, so up to about 25 to 50 Hz (1,500 to 3,000 rpm). Faster vibrations can appear at a wrong, lower frequency.

How is gravity removed?

A high-pass filter removes slow changes such as gravity and the phone tilting, leaving only the vibration. You can choose where it starts (0.5 to 5 Hz).

Where are my sessions saved?

Only in this browser. Export them as JSON to keep a backup or move them to another device, and import them again later.

Does it work on a computer?

Most laptops and desktops have no accelerometer, so the tool says so. You can still view, import and export saved sessions.
