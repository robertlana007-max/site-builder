What is Reading Speed Test?

The Reading Speed Test measures how many words per minute you read and how much you understand. You read a short passage at your normal pace, then answer three to five questions about it without looking back.

You get three results: your reading speed in words per minute (WPM), your comprehension score, and your effective reading speed, which is your speed multiplied by your comprehension. A guide shows how your speed compares with typical readers, and your history shows your progress over time.

There are 13 original passages at Easy, Medium and Hard levels, from a story about a lighthouse keeper to articles on bees, coffee and the history of zero.

What you can use it for

Knowing your baseline

Find out how fast you read before an exam with a lot of reading, such as IELTS, SAT or a university entrance test.

Tracking progress

Test yourself every week and watch your effective speed change in the history table.

Second-language reading

English learners can see how their speed grows as their vocabulary grows.

Classroom activity

Teachers can use the passages for a quick reading and comprehension check.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Reading Speed Test

  1. Choose a passage

    Pick a passage from the list. Each shows its level and length. Or click Random passage I have not read.

  2. Choose a text size

    Pick Small, Medium or Large so the text is comfortable to read.

  3. Start reading

    Click Start reading. The passage appears and the timer starts. Read at your normal pace, the way you would read an article.

  4. Stop the timer

    Click I have finished as soon as you read the last word. The text is then hidden.

  5. Answer the questions

    Choose an answer for every question from memory, then click See my result.

  6. Read your results

    You see your WPM, comprehension percentage, effective speed and time. Right answers are marked in green and wrong ones in red, and your band in the comparison guide is highlighted.

  7. Check your history

    Each result is added to Your history with your averages. Click Clear history to start over.

Features

  • 13 original passagesStories and articles at three levels, about 180 to 320 words each.
  • Words per minuteYour speed is worked out from the exact word count and your reading time.
  • Comprehension questionsThree to five multiple choice questions per passage, with options shuffled each time.
  • Effective reading speedCombines speed and understanding into one number.
  • Comparison guideSee how your speed compares with typical readers.
  • History and averagesResults are saved in your browser.

Example: one test

You choose "The Language of Bees" (Medium, 312 words) and click Start reading. You finish in 1 minute 20 seconds, so your speed is 312 ÷ 1.33 = 234 WPM. You answer 4 of the 5 questions correctly, which is 80% comprehension. Your effective speed is 234 × 0.8 = 187 WPM. The guide highlights "200 to 300 WPM", typical adult reading.

Tips and tricks

  • Read the way you normally do. Rushing raises WPM but lowers comprehension and effective speed.
  • Compare results on passages of the same level for a fair view of your progress.
  • Do not reread sentences unless you really lost the meaning; going back is the biggest thing that slows readers down.
  • Read regularly. Wider reading builds vocabulary, and vocabulary is what makes reading faster.
  • Take the test in a quiet place, at the same time of day, for consistent results.

Problems and how to fix them

My result says it is faster than anyone can read

You probably clicked I have finished too early. Results over 1,000 WPM are not saved. Try again at a natural pace.

It will not show my result

Every question needs an answer. Guess if you are not sure.

I have read all the passages

Random passage then picks from all of them. Results are more reliable on a passage you have not seen, so consider waiting a few weeks before repeating one.

My history is gone

History is stored in your browser and is removed if site data is cleared.

Questions and answers

What is a good reading speed?

Most adults read everyday non-fiction at about 200 to 300 words per minute. Students and keen readers are often faster. Speed only matters if you understand what you read, which is why comprehension is measured too.

What is effective reading speed?

It is your reading speed multiplied by your comprehension score. Reading 300 WPM with 60% understanding gives 180 effective WPM, which is less useful than 240 WPM with full understanding.

Why can I not look back at the text?

The questions check what you took in during one normal reading. Being able to look back would measure searching rather than reading.

Are the passages the same difficulty?

No. There are Easy, Medium and Hard passages of about 180 to 320 words. Compare results on passages of the same level for a fair picture of your progress.

Is my history saved?

Yes, in this browser only. You can clear it at any time.
