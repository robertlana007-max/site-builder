What is Image Text Editor?

The Image Text Editor lets you change words that are already written inside a picture. It reads the picture, draws pink boxes around each line of writing it finds, and when you click a box it covers the old words with the background colour and puts editable text in the same place, colour and a similar font. You can also add new text anywhere and hide any area by hand.

It is useful for fixing a typo on a poster or flyer, updating a price or date on a graphic, translating a short label, or adding a caption to a photo, without installing a photo editor. It works best on clear printed English letters and numbers on a fairly plain background.

Everything, including the text reading, runs inside your browser. The picture is not uploaded.

What you can use it for

Fix a typo on a graphic

Correct a spelling mistake on a banner, flyer or social media post when you no longer have the original design file.

Update prices and dates

Change "$20" to "$25" or an old event date to a new one on a menu or poster image.

Add captions and labels

Use "+ Add new text" to put a title, a name or a note on a photo or screenshot.

Hide private details

Use "Hide text by hand" to cover a phone number, email or name before you share a screenshot.

Translate short text

Replace a short English label with words in another language that your device has fonts for.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Image Text Editor

  1. Open your picture

    Click "Click or drop a picture here" or drag an image onto the box. The picture appears in the editor and the name and size are shown below it.

  2. Wait for the text scan

    A bar shows "Getting the text reader ready…" and then "Reading the picture… 45%". The first time takes a little longer because the reader has to load. When it is done, the message says how many lines it found, and each line has a pink dashed box on it.

  3. Click a pink box

    Click the line you want to change. The tool covers the old words with the colour found around the edge of the box and puts new text on top in the closest matching font (Arial, Times New Roman or Courier New, bold or regular) and the ink colour it detected.

  4. Type the new words

    The "New text" box is selected for you. Type the new words and watch them change on the picture as you type. Drag the text on the picture if you want to move it a little.

  5. Fine-tune the look

    Open "More options: font, size, colour". Choose another Font from the list, move the "Size" slider (8 to 400 px), set the Colour, and tick Bold, Italic or Outline. Click "Pick colour from picture", then click a spot on the image to copy its exact colour.

  6. Add text or hide areas by hand

    Click "+ Add new text" to place a new white, bold, outlined "Your text" label that you can edit and drag. Click "Hide text by hand", then drag a box over anything you want to cover; it is filled with the colour around its edge, and you can change that colour in the options.

  7. Correct mistakes

    Click any change you made to select it (a blue dashed outline appears), then click "Remove this change" to delete it. "Undo" removes the most recent change.

  8. Save and download

    In "Save as" choose "PNG (best quality)" or "JPG (smaller file)". Click "Save picture", then "Download". The file is named with "-edited" added, for example poster-edited.png. The pink boxes and outlines are not saved.

Worked example: changing a price on a menu card

You have menu.jpg, a 1080 × 1350 px graphic with the line "Lunch special $12" in dark letters on a cream background. You drop it in and, after a few seconds, the message says "Found 6 lines of text". You click the pink box on "Lunch special $12". The old words vanish under a cream patch and editable text appears in the same spot. You change it to "Lunch special $14", open the options and tick Bold because the original looked a bit heavier. You choose PNG, click "Save picture" and then "Download" to get menu-edited.png.

Tips and tricks

  • This works best on flat, plain backgrounds. On photos, gradients or patterns the cover patch may show as a visible box.
  • If the text looks slightly too big or small, use the Size slider, then drag it so it lines up with nearby words.
  • Use "Pick colour from picture" on the old letters if the detected colour is a little off.
  • When a line is not found, combine "Hide text by hand" and "+ Add new text" to do the same job manually.
  • Choose PNG when you save if the picture has small text or sharp edges; JPG can make letters look fuzzy.
  • The same font can look slightly different on another device, because the tool can only use fonts installed on your computer or phone.

Problems and how to fix them

No pink boxes appear

The reader only understands clear printed English letters and numbers. Handwriting, very small text, curved or stylised writing may not be found. Use "Hide text by hand" and "+ Add new text" instead.

The message says the text reader could not load or run

Your browser or device blocked or ran out of memory for the reader. Reload the page and try again, or edit by hand with the two manual buttons.

The "Save picture" button is grey

You have not made any change yet. It turns on after you edit a line, add text or hide an area.

A click changed a colour or started a box instead of selecting text

You were still in "Pick colour from picture" or "Hide text by hand" mode. Each mode ends after one use, or click the same button again to leave it. Use "Undo" if a box was added by mistake.

The old text still shows around the edges

Select the cover patch by clicking its edge and change its Colour, or drag a slightly larger box with "Hide text by hand" over it.

Questions and answers

How does it edit text that is part of a picture?

It reads the picture to find each line of writing. When you click a line, it paints over the old words with the colour around them and writes your new text in the same position, size and colour.

Will the new text be in exactly the same font?

Not always. A picture holds only pixels, with no font information, so the editor picks the closest of a few plain fonts by comparing shapes and sizes. You can switch to another font from the list if one fits better.

It did not find the writing in my picture. Why?

Automatic finding works for printed Latin letters and numbers that are reasonably sharp. Handwriting, very small or stylised text, and other scripts such as Urdu or Arabic may be missed. Use Cover old text and Add text to do those by hand.

Why can I see a box where the old text was?

Hiding works best on a plain background. On a photo or a pattern the covered patch is one flat colour, so it shows.

Is my picture uploaded?

No. Finding and editing the text both happen on your own device.
