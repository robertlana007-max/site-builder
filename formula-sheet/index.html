What is Formula Sheet?

The Formula Sheet is a searchable reference of more than 230 essential formulas for school and college, in seven sections: Algebra, Geometry, Trigonometry, Calculus, Statistics & probability, Physics and Chemistry. Every formula is shown in proper maths layout with fractions, powers and roots, and lists what each letter means and its unit.

More than 100 formulas have a "Use it" calculator. Pick the letter you want to find, type the others and get the answer straight away, so v = u + at can be solved for v, u, a or t, and PV = nRT for any of P, V, n or T.

It works offline in your browser without loading anything from other sites. You can star favourites, search across all sections and print a clean sheet of any section.

What you can use it for

Homework and exam revision

Look up a formula, check what each letter means and the correct units.

Checking an answer

Use the calculator to see if your worked answer to a physics or chemistry problem is right.

Rearranging formulas

Solve for any letter without rearranging by hand, for example the time in s = ut + ½at².

Making a cheat sheet

Star the formulas you need and print one section as a two-column sheet.

Lab work

Work out moles, molarity, dilutions or gas volumes during a practical.

Quick geometry

Get areas, volumes and surface areas of circles, cylinders, cones and spheres.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Formula Sheet

  1. Find a formula

    Type in "Search formulas", for example "ohm", "cone" or "molarity". Every word must match, so "volume cone" finds the cone volume. The line below says how many formulas are shown.

  2. Pick a section

    Use the buttons: All, ★ Favourites, With calculator, or a section like Physics. Formulas are grouped under headings such as "Motion (kinematics)" and "Electricity".

  3. Read the formula

    Each card shows the formula, then each symbol with its meaning and unit in brackets, such as "I – current (A)".

  4. Open the calculator

    Click "🧮 Use it" on a card. Choose the letter you want after "Solve for", type the other values and the answer appears in the highlighted box with its unit. "Fill example" puts in sample values and "Clear" empties the boxes.

  5. Understand two answers

    Some equations have more than one answer, such as x² − 3x + 2 = 0 or the sine rule. The tool then says "More than one answer: x = 1 or x = 2".

  6. Save favourites

    Click ★ on any card. Favourites are saved in your browser and appear under "★ Favourites".

  7. Print a section

    Click "🖨 Print" next to a section heading. Only the formulas shown in that section are printed, in two columns with their symbols explained.

  • 109 solve-for-anything calculatorsEach one can find any of its letters from the others, using a numerical solver that finds every real answer in range.

  • Symbols and unitsEvery letter is explained with its SI unit, and constants such as G, R and Avogadro’s number are listed.
  • Angles in degreesTrig and physics calculators take angles in degrees and only return sensible angles, such as 0° to 90° for a launch angle.

  • Print-friendlyPrint any section as a compact two-column sheet.

Worked example: how long does the car take?

A car starts at 5 m/s and speeds up at 3 m/s² until it reaches 20 m/s. Search "final velocity" and open "Final velocity (SUVAT)", v = u + at. Click "🧮 Use it", choose t after "Solve for", and type v = 20, u = 5 and a = 3. The answer box shows 5 and the message reads "t = 5 s", because t = (20 − 5) ÷ 3. To find the distance, open "Average velocity displacement (SUVAT)", s = (u + v)t ÷ 2, keep s selected and type u = 5, v = 20 and t = 5. The answer is s = 62.5 m.

Tips and tricks

  • Use SI units in the calculators: metres, kilograms, seconds, kelvin and pascals, unless the card says otherwise.
  • Type very large or small numbers with e, such as 6.67e-11 or 3e8.
  • Percentages in compound interest go in as decimals: 5% is 0.05.
  • Gas law temperatures must be in kelvin. Use the "Celsius to kelvin" card first.
  • Search for a symbol meaning, such as "wavelength" or "resistance", if you do not remember the formula's name.
  • The thin lens equation uses the real-is-positive rule: a virtual image has a negative image distance.

Problems and how to fix them

The calculator says there is no real answer

The numbers cannot fit the formula, for example a side longer than the hypotenuse or a negative value under a square root. Check the values and units.

My answer is 1000 times off

Check units: centimetres instead of metres, grams instead of kilograms, or millilitres instead of litres are the usual causes.

Some formulas have no Use it button

Identities and rules such as the product rule or Bayes' theorem have no single answer to calculate, so they are reference only.

My favourites are gone

Favourites are saved in this browser. Private browsing, clearing site data or another device starts with none.

Printing shows the whole page

Use the "🖨 Print" button next to a section heading rather than the browser menu, so only that section is printed.

Questions and answers

Which formulas are included?

More than 230: algebra, areas, volumes and surface areas, trig identities and the sine and cosine rules, derivative and integral rules, statistics and probability, physics from motion to optics and thermodynamics, and chemistry from moles to gas laws and pH.

Can the calculator solve for any variable?

Yes. Over 100 formulas have a calculator where you choose which letter to find. It solves numerically, so you do not need to rearrange the formula, and it shows every real answer when there is more than one.

What units should I use?

Use the units shown next to each symbol, which are SI units such as metres, kilograms, seconds and kelvin. Angles in the calculators are in degrees.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The formulas are drawn with plain HTML and CSS and nothing is loaded from other websites, so the page works once it has loaded.

Can I print a formula sheet?

Yes. Click Print next to any section heading to print that section as a compact two-column sheet.
