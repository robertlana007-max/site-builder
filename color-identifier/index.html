    What is Color Identifier?

    Color Identifier tells you what colour something is. Point your phone camera at it, or open a photo and tap a spot, and it names the colour in plain words such as "dark navy blue" or "light olive green".

    It also gives a simple colour family (for example dark blue, light green, beige or grey) and the HEX and RGB codes. A small area is averaged to reduce camera noise, and colours are matched the way the eye sees difference, using CIEDE2000.

    Press Say it to hear the colour named aloud, which helps colour-blind and low-vision users. Freeze the picture, keep a history of colours, and export it as CSV or JSON. Nothing is uploaded.

    What you can use it for

    Colour blindness

    Check the colour of clothes, food, cables or map lines.

    Low vision

    Hear colour names spoken aloud.

    Shopping

    Name the colour of paint, fabric or furniture.

    Photos

    Tap any spot in a picture to name its colour.

    Learning colours

    Help children or language learners learn colour names.

    Design

    Get HEX and RGB codes of real-world objects.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Color Identifier

    1. Start

      Press Use camera and allow it, or press Open a photo.

    2. Aim

      Point the circle at the colour. On a photo, tap the spot.

    3. Read the result

      The name, colour family and HEX and RGB codes appear under the picture.

    4. Listen

      Press Say it, or tick Say the name when I tap or freeze.

    5. Freeze

      Press Freeze to hold the picture, then tap other spots.

    6. Keep colours

      Press Save to history. Export the list as CSV or JSON, and import it on another device.

    Worked example: matching socks

    Tom is colour-blind and cannot tell his navy and black socks apart. He turns on Say the name when I tap or freeze, points the camera at one sock in daylight and presses Freeze. The phone says "Dark navy blue. Colour family: dark blue." The other sock is "Black". He pairs them correctly.

    Tips and tricks

    • Use daylight or white light. Warm bulbs make colours look more yellow or orange.
    • Fill the circle with the colour and avoid shadows and shiny reflections.
    • Use a larger area for fabrics and textured surfaces.
    • Lighter or darker light changes the name: compare items in the same light.
    • For exact codes from screenshots, use the Color Picker.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The name keeps changing

    Hold the phone still, use a larger area, or press Freeze.

    The colour looks wrong

    Camera colour depends on light. Move to daylight or white light and try again.

    Say it does not speak

    Your browser or device may have no voice installed, or the sound may be off. The name is always shown on screen.

    Camera access was blocked

    Allow the camera for this site in the browser or phone settings, or open a photo instead.

    Import says it is not a colour history file

    Choose a colours.json file exported by this tool.

    Questions and answers

    How accurate is it?

    The maths is exact for the picture, but cameras change colours with the light. Use daylight or a white light, avoid shadows and coloured lamps, and hold the camera close. Use the Area setting to average out noise.

    Where do the colour names come from?

    From the xkcd colour survey, a large online survey in which many thousands of people named colours (public domain, with joke and rude names removed). The nearest name is found with CIEDE2000, a formula for how different two colours look to the eye.

    What is the colour family?

    A simple everyday name such as dark blue, light green, beige or grey, for when the detailed name is unusual.

    Can it help with colour blindness?

    Yes. Point at clothes, food, cables or charts to hear the colour named aloud. It is a helper, not a medical test.

    How is this different from Color Picker?

    Color Picker gives exact colour codes from images and palettes for design. Color Identifier uses the camera, names colours in plain words and speaks them.

    Is my camera picture saved?

    No. The picture stays in your browser. Only the colours you save are kept, on this device.
