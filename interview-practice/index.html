    What is Interview Practice?

    Interview Practice helps you get ready for a job interview with 123 common questions in ten groups: general, behavioural (STAR), teamwork, problem solving, customer service, sales, tech basics, leadership, first job or student, and good questions to ask the employer.

    Every question has a short tip on what the interviewer really wants to know and an outline of a strong answer.

    Practice mode shows a random question with a thinking timer and an answer timer. You can record your answer to listen back and, in browsers that support it, see your words as text with a count of filler words like "um" and "like".

    What you can use it for

    Before a job interview

    Rehearse the questions you are most likely to get.

    First job or internship

    Practise answers when you have little work experience.

    STAR answers

    Build clear stories for behavioural questions.

    Speaking confidence

    Hear how you sound and cut filler words.

    Second-language interviews

    Practise answering out loud in English or another language.

    Coaches and teachers

    Run mock interviews with students.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Interview Practice

    1. Pick the questions

      Choose All categories, one category, or your favourites.

    2. Set the timers

      Choose thinking time (for example 20 seconds) and answer time (for example 2 minutes).

    3. Turn on extras

      Tick Record my answer to hear yourself, and Show what I said for speech to text. Allow the microphone when asked.

    4. Press Start

      Think while the orange bar fills, then answer out loud when the beep sounds. Press Start answering now to skip ahead.

    5. Review

      Play back or download your recording, read your words, and check your speaking speed and filler words.

    6. Use the tips

      Open Show tip and answer outline, then try again.

    7. Save what helps

      Tap the star to save a question and write notes for it. Export them as a backup or print them.

    Worked example: tell me about yourself

    Sam picks General, 20 seconds to think and 2 minutes to answer, and turns on recording and speech to text. The question is "Tell me about yourself." After answering, Sam sees 210 words in 1:35 (133 words per minute) and 7 filler words, mostly "um" and "like". Sam stars the question, writes "Present, past, future: sales job, team award, why this role" in the notes and tries again with fewer fillers.

    Tips and tricks

    • Use STAR for behavioural questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
    • Prepare three or four stories you can adapt to many questions.
    • Aim for 1 to 2 minutes per answer.
    • A short pause is better than "um". Breathe and continue.
    • Practise out loud, not just in your head.
    • Always have two questions ready to ask the employer.

    Problems and how to fix them

    The microphone does not work

    Allow the microphone for this site in your browser settings and close other apps using it. Try the Mic Test tool on this site.

    Speech to text is missing or stops

    It works in Chrome, Edge and Safari and needs an internet connection there. In other browsers, type your answer into the box to count words and fillers.

    The filler count looks low

    Speech to text often leaves out "um" and "uh". Listen to your recording to check.

    My recording is gone

    Recordings are kept only while the page is open. Download any you want to keep.

    Questions and answers

    What kinds of questions are included?

    123 questions in ten groups: general, behavioural (STAR), teamwork, problem solving, customer service, sales, tech basics, leadership, first job or student, and questions to ask the employer.

    Is my recording uploaded?

    No. The recording is made and kept in your browser’s memory only. It disappears when you leave the page unless you download it.

    How does the filler-word count work?

    With speech to text on, your answer is turned into text and the tool counts words like um, uh, like, you know, basically and actually. Speech to text often skips "um", so listen to the recording too.

    Why is speech to text not available?

    It needs the Web Speech API, found in Chrome, Edge and Safari. These browsers use their online speech service, so it needs an internet connection. You can always type your answer to analyse it.

    What is the STAR method?

    A way to answer "tell me about a time" questions: Situation, Task, Action and Result. The tips and outlines show how to use it.

    Are my favourites and notes saved?

    Yes, in this browser on this device. Use Export to keep a backup or move them to another device, and Print to make a study sheet.
