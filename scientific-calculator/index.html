    What is Scientific Calculator?

    The Scientific Calculator works out full expressions with trigonometry, logarithms, powers, roots, factorials, percentages and constants such as π and e. You can type with your keyboard or tap the on-screen keys.

    It is built for school and university maths, science and engineering homework, and everyday calculations that need more than + − × ÷. It follows the standard order of operations and shows a live preview of the answer as you type.

    It has degree and radian modes, a memory (MC, MR, M+, M−), an Ans key for the last answer and a history of your last 50 calculations, all kept on your device.

    What you can use it for

    Trigonometry homework

    Find sin, cos and tan of angles in degrees, or work in radians for calculus.

    Science and engineering

    Use scientific notation like 6.022E23, logarithms and powers in formulas.

    Exam practice

    Check answers worked out by hand, step by step, using Ans and memory.

    Statistics and counting

    Work out factorials like 10! for permutations and combinations.

    Everyday maths

    Percentages, square roots and long expressions with brackets in one go.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Scientific Calculator

    1. Choose DEG or RAD

      For angles in degrees (like 30° or 90°) keep DEG. For radians (like π/6) click RAD. The choice is remembered.

    2. Type the expression

      Type with your keyboard (* and / become × and ÷) or tap the keys. Function keys like sin add an opening bracket for you: sin(30).

    3. Use powers and roots

      Press xʸ for powers (2^10), x² to square, 1/x for the reciprocal, √ for square roots and n! for factorials.

    4. Get the answer

      Press = or Enter. The answer replaces the expression and the calculation moves to the line above. The grey line under the display previews the answer while you type.

    5. Keep going with Ans

      Right after an answer, typing an operator like + continues from it as Ans + …; typing a number starts a new calculation. Ans can also be used anywhere, like Ans÷2.

    6. Use memory and history

      M+ adds the current answer to memory, M− subtracts it, MR inserts it and MC clears it. Click a past calculation in History to load it again, or its answer to insert the number.

    Features

    • Correct order of operationsBrackets, then powers right to left (2^3^2 = 512), then × and ÷, then + and −. A leading minus applies after powers: −2^2 = −4.
    • Implicit multiplication2π, 3(4+5), (1+2)(3+4) and 2sin(30) work without a × sign.
    • Clean answersResults are rounded to 12 significant digits, so 0.1+0.2 shows 0.3 and sin(180°) shows 0.
    • Clear error messagesDivision by zero, square roots of negative numbers, logs of zero, sin⁻¹ outside −1 to 1, tan(90°) and too-large results each get a plain-English message.
    • Full keyboard supportType anywhere on the page; Enter evaluates, Backspace deletes, Esc clears.
    • Safe parserExpressions are read by a purpose-built parser, never run as code.
    • History and memory savedThe last 50 calculations, the memory value and DEG/RAD are kept in this browser.

    Worked example: the height of a tree

    You stand 20 metres from a tree and the top is at an angle of 35° above you. With DEG selected, type 20tan(35) and press Enter: the answer is 14.0041507641 metres. To add your eye height of 1.6 m, press + (the display shows Ans+), type 1.6 and press Enter to get 15.6041507641 metres.

    Tips and tricks

    • Check DEG or RAD before any trigonometry; it is the most common source of wrong answers.
    • Use brackets when in doubt: 1/(2π) is not the same as 1/2π, which means (1/2)×π.
    • Use EXP for scientific notation: 3E8 is 3 × 10⁸.
    • Missing closing brackets at the end are added automatically, so sin(45 works.
    • On a phone, the system keyboard stays hidden; use the keypad, which has every function.

    Problems and how to fix them

    "You cannot divide by zero."

    Something in the expression divides by 0, including 0⁻¹ or 0 to a negative power. Check the denominator.

    "tan is undefined at 90°."

    tan has no value at 90°, 270° and so on. In RAD mode the same happens at π/2.

    "A negative number cannot be raised to a fractional power."

    For example (−8)^(1/3). Calculate the root of the positive number and add the minus sign yourself: −(8^(1/3)) = −2.

    "n! needs a whole number from 0 to 170."

    Factorials only work on whole numbers, and above 170! the result is too large for a computer to hold.

    The answer is not what I expected for sin or cos

    You are probably in the wrong angle mode. Switch between DEG and RAD and press = again.

    Questions and answers

    In what order are operations done?

    Brackets first, then powers (right to left, so 2^3^2 is 2^9), then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction, all left to right. A minus sign in front applies after powers, so −2^2 is −4.

    Why does 0.1 + 0.2 show 0.3?

    Computers store decimals in binary, which creates tiny errors like 0.30000000000000004. Answers are rounded to 12 significant digits to hide that noise.

    How do I enter numbers like 3 × 10⁵?

    Press EXP and type the power: 3E5 means 300,000 and 1.5E-3 means 0.0015. A lowercase e is Euler's number 2.718…

    Can I leave out the multiplication sign?

    Yes. 2π, 3(4+5), (1+2)(3+4) and 2sin(30) all multiply. Missing closing brackets at the end are added for you.

    What does % do?

    It divides the number before it by 100, so 50% is 0.5 and 200×15% is 30.

    Is my history saved?

    The last 50 calculations, the memory value and the angle mode are kept in this browser only. Clear history removes them.
