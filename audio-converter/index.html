    What is Audio Converter?

    Audio Converter changes audio files from one format to another: MP3, WAV, M4A (AAC), OGG Vorbis and FLAC. It also takes the sound out of video files.

    You can add one file or a whole batch, choose the quality (bitrate) and whether the result is stereo or mono, and download each converted file or all of them together as a ZIP.

    The conversion runs in your browser using FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, so your music and recordings are never uploaded.

    What you can use it for

    Play anywhere

    Turn WAV, FLAC, OGG or WMA files into MP3 that every phone, car and player can play.

    Make files smaller

    Convert large WAV recordings to MP3 or M4A to share them by email or chat.

    Voice notes

    Convert OPUS, OGG or AMR voice messages into MP3.

    Editing and archiving

    Convert to WAV or FLAC for an audio editor, or to keep a lossless copy.

    Apple devices

    Make M4A (AAC) files for iTunes, Apple Music or iMovie.

    Audio from videos

    Save the sound of MP4 or MKV videos as audio files.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Audio Converter

    1. Add your files

      Click the box or drop files onto it. You can add more later. Each file appears in the list with its size; files that are not audio or video are skipped with a note.

    2. Choose the format

      Under "Convert to" pick MP3, M4A / AAC, WAV, FLAC or OGG Vorbis.

    3. Choose the quality

      For MP3, M4A and OGG, choose a bitrate. 128 kbps is good for speech and everyday music, 192 kbps is very good, 320 kbps is the best MP3 quality.

    4. Optional settings

      "Channels" can make the files mono (half the size, fine for voice) or stereo. "Sample rate" can change to 44.1 kHz for music, 48 kHz for video or 22.05 kHz for small voice files.

    5. Convert

      Press "Convert". The files are converted one by one; each row shows its progress and then the new size.

    6. Download

      Press "Download" next to a file, or "Download all (ZIP)" when there are several. If a file could not be converted, the reason is shown under its name.

    Features

    • Five output formatsMP3, M4A (AAC), WAV, FLAC and OGG Vorbis.
    • Many input formatsIncluding WMA, AMR, OPUS, AIFF and the sound of videos.
    • Batch conversionUp to 50 files at once, with a ZIP of the results.
    • Quality controlBitrate from 64 to 320 kbps, mono or stereo, and sample rate.
    • Keeps tagsTitle, artist and album information is copied when the format allows.
    • PrivateRuns in your browser, so nothing is uploaded.

    Worked example: a folder of WAV recordings to MP3

    Bilal recorded 12 practice takes as WAV files, 40 MB each, and wants to send them to his teacher. He selects all 12 files in the file picker, chooses "MP3 (plays everywhere)" and "128 kbps (good)", sets Channels to "Mono (smaller)" because it is a single voice, and presses "Convert 12 files". After about a minute every row says Done with sizes such as 40.4 MB → 2.3 MB. He presses "Download all (ZIP)" and gets converted-mp3.zip, small enough to email.

    Tips and tricks

    • Choose mono for speech recordings: the file is half the size and sounds the same.
    • Keep a lossless copy (WAV or FLAC) of important recordings, and share MP3 copies.
    • Converting a file that is already MP3 to a higher bitrate does not improve it; keep the original bitrate or lower.
    • On a phone, convert a few files at a time so the browser does not run out of memory.
    • Keep the tab open while converting; the work stops if the tab is closed.

    Problems and how to fix them

    A file says it could not be read

    It may be damaged, empty, or protected (for example songs bought with copy protection). Try playing it in another app first.

    It says the file has no sound track

    The video or file contains no audio, so there is nothing to convert.

    The engine could not be downloaded

    The converter needs a one-time 32 MB download. Check your connection and try again; after that it works offline too.

    The browser ran out of memory

    Very long files (several hours) can be too much for a phone. Try a computer, or convert fewer files at once.

    Questions and answers

    Are my files uploaded?

    No. The files are converted inside your browser by a WebAssembly build of FFmpeg, so they stay on your device.

    Which formats can I convert from?

    MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC, OGG, OPUS, FLAC, WMA, AMR, AIFF and the sound of videos such as MP4, MOV, MKV and WebM.

    Which format should I choose?

    MP3 plays everywhere. M4A (AAC) sounds a little better at the same size and suits iPhone and Apple Music. FLAC and WAV keep the full quality; FLAC files are about half the size of WAV.

    Can I convert many files at once?

    Yes, up to 50 files at a time. They are converted one after another, and you can download them all as one ZIP.

    Why is the first conversion slow?

    The converter engine (about 32 MB) downloads the first time you use it. After that your browser keeps it, so it starts quickly.

    Does converting MP3 to WAV or FLAC improve quality?

    No. It only makes a bigger file without loss from now on; detail already removed by MP3 cannot come back.
