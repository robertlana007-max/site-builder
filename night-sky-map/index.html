    What is Night Sky Map?

    Night Sky Map is a live star chart for any place and time. It draws the brightest stars, the constellation figures and names, the planets, the Sun and the Moon with its real phase, together with the horizon and compass points.

    You can drag to look around, zoom, move through the night with a time slider, or hold up your phone and let the map follow where it points. Tap anything to see its name, brightness, height above the horizon and its rise, highest and set times.

    All positions are calculated on your device from a star catalogue and planet formulas, so it works outdoors with no signal. A red night mode keeps your eyes used to the dark.

    What you can use it for

    Stargazing

    Find out which star or planet you are looking at.

    Spotting planets

    See which planets are up tonight and where to look.

    Learning constellations

    Turn lines and names on to learn the shapes.

    Planning a night out

    Slide the time to see what will rise later.

    Teaching

    Show children how the sky turns during the night.

    Special dates

    See the sky on a birthday or historic night.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Night Sky Map

    1. Set your place

      Search for your city, press Use my location, or type coordinates. The sky depends on where you are.

    2. Check the time

      The map starts at the time now. Use the date box for another day, and Now to come back.

    3. Move through the night

      Drag the Move time slider up to 12 hours either way and watch the stars turn.

    4. Look around

      Whole sky shows the full dome with the horizon as a circle. Look N, E, S and W show one direction. Drag to pan and pinch or use + and − to zoom.

    5. Use your phone

      Press Point phone at sky, allow motion access if asked, and hold the phone up towards the sky.

    6. Tap for details

      Tap a star, planet or the Moon, or pick one from the Up in the sky list, to see what it is and when it rises and sets.

    7. Go red at night

      Press Red night mode outdoors. Press Leave night mode or Escape to go back.

    Example: a January evening in London

    At 22:00 on 15 January 2026 in London, Jupiter is the brightest point in the sky, high in the south-east among the stars of Gemini. Sirius sparkles 20° up in the south-south-east, below Orion. Tapping Sirius shows it is in Canis Major, magnitude −1.4, and gives its rise, highest and set times for that night.

    Tips and tricks

    • Give your eyes 15 to 20 minutes in the dark to see fainter stars.
    • Planets shine steadily while stars twinkle, which helps tell them apart.
    • Use Whole sky to get your bearings, then Look S or Look N to match the view in front of you.
    • Turn off Names when the map looks busy.
    • Calibrate your phone's compass by moving it in a figure 8 before using phone mode.

    Problems and how to fix them

    Phone mode does not move

    Your phone or browser may not share motion sensors, or permission was refused. On iPhone, reload and allow motion access when asked. Otherwise drag the map.

    The map is pointing the wrong way

    Phone compasses are disturbed by metal and magnets. Move away from cars and buildings and calibrate with a figure 8.

    I cannot see the stars on the map

    If the Sun is up, the stars are dimmed. Move the time slider to the evening.

    A planet is not in the list

    It is below the horizon at that time. Slide the time forward to see when it rises.

    Questions and answers

    How do I find a planet?

    Look at the Up in the sky list under the map. Tap a planet to centre the map on it and see its direction and height above the horizon, then look that way.

    How does phone mode work?

    Press Point phone at sky and hold your phone up. The map follows your phone's compass and motion sensors. On iPhone you will be asked to allow motion access. The compass can be a few degrees off; move the phone in a figure 8 to calibrate it.

    What is red night mode for?

    Red light keeps your eyes adjusted to the dark, so you can still see faint stars after looking at the screen. Turn your screen brightness down as well.

    Which stars are shown?

    The 300 brightest stars plus all the stars that make up the constellation figures, from the Hipparcos catalogue, with the 88 constellations.

    Why are no stars shown in the daytime?

    When the Sun is up the sky is drawn blue and the stars are dimmed, because you could not see them. Move the time slider to the evening.

    Does it need the internet?

    No. All positions are calculated on your device, so it works outdoors without a signal once the page has loaded.
