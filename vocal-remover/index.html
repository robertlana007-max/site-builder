What is Vocal Remover?

Vocal Remover splits a song into two tracks: the music without the voice, for karaoke or an instrumental, and the voice on its own, called an acapella.

The main method uses Spleeter, an open-source AI model released by Deezer that was trained to tell singing apart from instruments. It runs in your browser with ONNX Runtime, so the song is never uploaded. A quick classic method, which removes sound panned exactly to the centre of a stereo mix, is included too.

Separation is never perfect: how clean the result is depends on the song, the mix and the recording.

What you can use it for

Karaoke

Make a sing-along track of a song you like for a party or practice.

Singing practice

Hear the vocals alone to learn a melody, harmony or lyrics.

Covers and remixes

Get an instrumental to sing or rap over, or an acapella to remix, where you have the right to use the song.

Music study

Listen closely to the backing instruments without the singer.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Vocal Remover

  1. Choose your song

    Click the box or drop an MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG or FLAC file, or a music video, onto it. Songs up to 10 minutes are accepted.

  2. Choose the method

    Keep "AI separation (best)" for most songs; the first time it downloads about 54 MB. "Quick centre cut" needs no download and is instant but only works on some stereo studio songs.

  3. Separate

    Press "Separate vocals". A progress bar shows the time left; a 4-minute song usually takes under a minute on a computer. Press "Stop" to cancel.

  4. Listen

    Press "Play" and switch between "Music only (karaoke)", "Vocals only" and "Original" while it plays to compare.

  5. Save

    Under "Track to save" choose the music or the vocals, pick MP3 (128, 192 or 320 kbps) or WAV, press "Make file" and then "Download". Files are named, for example, song-instrumental.mp3 or song-vocals.mp3.

Features

  • AI source separationSpleeter separates vocals from accompaniment on most songs, including mono recordings.
  • Karaoke and acapellaBoth tracks come from one run and add up exactly to the original.
  • Quick centre cutA no-download classic method limited to the voice range, so bass and cymbals stay.
  • Instant comparisonSwitch between music, vocals and original at the same moment.
  • MP3 or WAVSave at up to 320 kbps MP3, or lossless WAV for editing.
  • PrivateThe song is processed on your device and never uploaded.

Worked example: a karaoke track for a birthday

Kenji wants to sing his friend's favourite song at a party. He drops the 3:45 song.mp3 on the box, keeps "AI separation (best)" and presses "Separate vocals". After the one-time download, it finishes in about 40 seconds. He plays "Music only (karaoke)": the singer is gone apart from a faint echo in the chorus, which is fine for karaoke. He keeps "Music only" and MP3 192 kbps, presses "Make file" and downloads song-instrumental.mp3.

Tips and tricks

  • Use the best-quality file you have: WAV, FLAC or a high-bitrate MP3 separates more cleanly than a small, compressed file.
  • If AI separation leaves a faint voice, that is normal for songs with heavy reverb or backing vocals; it is usually not noticeable when you sing over it.
  • Try Quick centre cut on old stereo recordings where the singer is in the middle; it keeps the full sound of the instruments.
  • Save the acapella as WAV if you will edit or remix it.

Problems and how to fix them

I can still hear the singer

Some voice traces remain with almost every method, especially echoes, backing vocals and live recordings. Try the other method and compare.

Quick centre cut says the song is mono

Centre cut needs a stereo song with different left and right channels. Use AI separation instead.

The AI model does not download

Check your internet connection and try again. After the first download, the model is kept by your browser.

The song is too long

Songs up to 10 minutes are supported to stay within the browser's memory. Cut it first with the Audio Cutter.

Questions and answers

Is my song uploaded?

No. The AI model downloads to your browser once (about 54 MB) and the separation happens on your device.

How good is the result?

For most studio songs the voice becomes much quieter or disappears, but traces and echoes of the voice can remain, and some instruments may sound slightly watery. Live recordings and songs with heavy reverb are harder.

What is the difference between the two methods?

AI separation uses Spleeter, a model trained to tell voices from instruments, and works on most songs, even mono ones. Quick centre cut removes only what is exactly in the middle of a stereo mix; it is instant but works on fewer songs.

Can I get just the vocals?

Yes. Choose Vocals only (acapella) under Track to save. It is approximate: some drums or instruments can leak in.

How long can the song be?

Up to 10 minutes. A 4-minute song usually takes under a minute on a computer and a few minutes on a phone.

Can I use the result commercially?

The tool itself places no limits, but the song still belongs to its rights holders. Use karaoke tracks of other people’s songs only where you have the right to.
