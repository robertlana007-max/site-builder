What is Health Log?

The Health Log is a private diary for blood pressure and pulse, blood sugar, weight and temperature. Each reading is saved with the date, time and a note.

It draws charts over time, works out averages for the last 7, 30 and 90 days, and places each reading in clearly cited ranges: 2017 ACC/AHA blood pressure categories (with the European ESC/ISH view) and blood sugar ranges from the ADA and WHO.

You can make a PDF report with tables and charts to take to your doctor, and export or import CSV files. It is not a diagnosis: always talk to your doctor about your results.

What you can use it for

Home blood pressure

Log morning and evening readings and see your average.

Diabetes

Track fasting and after-meal sugar in mg/dL or mmol/L.

Weight goals

See the trend and change over 30 and 90 days.

Fever

Record a child's temperature through an illness.

Doctor visits

Bring a tidy report with charts and every reading.

New medicines

See how readings change after a dose change.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Health Log

  1. Choose what to record

    Tap Blood pressure, Blood sugar, Weight or Temperature.

  2. Type the reading

    The date and time are filled in; change them if you are adding an older reading.

  3. Press Add reading

    You see the reading's range and what it means, with a reminder to talk to your doctor.

  4. Set your units

    In Settings choose mg/dL or mmol/L, kg or lb, and °C or °F.

  5. Look at the trend

    Check the averages and the chart for 7 days up to everything.

  6. Fix mistakes

    Use Edit or ✕ next to a reading.

  7. Share with your doctor

    Choose a period and download the PDF report.

  8. Back up

    Export a CSV or JSON backup; importing skips readings you already have.

Features

  • Four kinds of readingsBlood pressure with pulse, blood sugar with timing, weight and temperature.
  • Cited rangesACC/AHA 2017 with ESC 2024 and ISH 2020 notes; ADA 2025 and WHO for sugar; WHO/NHS/CDC for fever.
  • Correct unit conversionmmol/L = mg/dL ÷ 18.016, with each unit's own published cut-offs.
  • Diabetes targets optionCompare sugar with common ADA treatment targets.
  • ChartsDrawn on your device, with guide lines for the main cut-offs.
  • Doctor report PDFAverages, charts and a full table of readings.
  • CSV export and importOpens in any spreadsheet; duplicates are skipped on import.

Worked example: a week of blood pressure

Lee measures every morning: 132/84, 128/82, 135/86, 130/80, 127/81, 133/85 and 129/83. The 7-day average is 131/83, in the stage 1 range of the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline. Under the European home threshold (135/85) it is below the hypertension cut-off, so Lee downloads the report and talks it through with the doctor.

Tips and tricks

  • Measure blood pressure at the same times each day, after sitting quietly for 5 minutes.
  • Take two readings a minute apart and record both, or their average.
  • Mark blood sugar readings as fasting or after a meal so they can be compared.
  • Weigh yourself at the same time of day on the same scale.
  • Export a CSV now and then as a backup.

Problems and how to fix them

My reading was refused

It is outside a possible range, or the top number is not bigger than the bottom one. A sugar like 126 in mmol/L or 5.4 in mg/dL means the unit is wrong; change it in Settings.

The import skipped some rows

Rows need a date (YYYY-MM-DD), a type (bp, sugar, weight or temp) and possible values. Readings already in your diary are skipped too.

My readings are gone

They are saved in this browser only. Private windows and clearing site data remove them; import your CSV or JSON backup.

The ranges differ from what my doctor said

Guidelines differ, and your doctor may set personal targets. Follow your doctor's advice.

Questions and answers

Which blood pressure categories does it use?

The 2017 ACC/AHA categories: normal below 120/80, elevated 120–129/<80, stage 1 130–139 or 80–89, stage 2 140+/90+, and the crisis range above 180/120. A note explains the European (ESC 2024) and ISH 2020 thresholds, which start at 140/90 in the clinic or 135/85 at home.

How do mg/dL and mmol/L convert?

mmol/L = mg/dL ÷ 18.016 (and mg/dL = mmol/L × 18.016). You can switch units at any time; all readings are converted, and each unit uses its own published cut-offs, such as 126 mg/dL and 7.0 mmol/L.

Can it tell me if I have diabetes or high blood pressure?

No. Home readings help you and your doctor see a pattern, but a diagnosis needs proper tests. Talk to your doctor about your results.

I have diabetes. Can I use my targets?

Tick "I have diabetes" to compare sugar readings with common ADA treatment targets (80–130 mg/dL or 4.4–7.2 mmol/L before meals, below 180 mg/dL or 10.0 mmol/L after meals). Your own targets may differ.

Where are my readings stored?

Only in this browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded. Export a CSV or JSON backup to keep a copy or move to another device; importing skips readings you already have.

What is in the doctor report?

For each type of reading: averages for 7, 30 and 90 days, a chart, and a table of every reading in the chosen period with its range and your notes, plus the sources of the ranges.
