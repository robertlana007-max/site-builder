Face Blur

Hide the faces of people in your photos before you share them. A small AI face finder runs in your browser and covers every face with a blur, pixels, an emoji or a solid colour. Tap faces to keep them visible, draw extra boxes, and process many photos at once.

    What is Face Blur?

    Face Blur finds the faces in your photos automatically and hides them with a blur, a pixel mosaic, an emoji or a solid colour. It is a quick way to protect the privacy of people in pictures before you share them online, with a client or in a report.

    A small open-source AI face detector (YuNet, under 1 MB) runs in your browser, so your photos are never uploaded. You can switch any face on or off with a tap, draw your own boxes over faces or other things it missed, such as name badges or car number plates, and process many photos at once.

    Saved photos are re-drawn from scratch, so hidden camera and location details (EXIF data) are left out too.

    What you can use it for

    Share event and school photos safely

    Hide the faces of children or guests who have not agreed to be posted online.

    Street and travel photography

    Blur passers-by before you publish a photo on social media or a blog.

    Reports and evidence

    Anonymise people in photos for incident reports, research, news or real-estate listings.

    Screenshots of video calls

    Cover participants' faces before you share a screenshot of a meeting.

    Hide other details

    Draw boxes over name badges, screens, house numbers or number plates.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Face Blur

    1. Add your photos

      Click the box or drop one or many photos onto it. The first time, the face finder downloads (about 15 MB) and is then kept by your browser. Each photo appears as a small picture with the number of covered faces.

    2. Choose how to cover faces

      In "Cover faces with" pick Blur, Pixels (mosaic), Emoji or Solid colour. "Strength" sets how strong the blur or mosaic is, "Shape" chooses an oval or a rectangle, and "Cover size" makes the covers larger so hair and ears are hidden too.

    3. Check every face

      Green outlines show covered faces. Tap a face to leave it visible (it turns red and dashed) or to cover it again. Use "Cover all" or "Uncover all" to change every face at once.

    4. Add faces it missed

      Choose "Draw a new box" and drag over anything else you want to hide. Your own boxes are orange. "Remove my last box" undoes the latest one.

    5. Look through the other photos

      Tap the small pictures above the editor to move between photos. The cover style applies to all of them.

    6. Save

      Press "Download this photo", or "Download all (ZIP)" to save every photo at full size in one ZIP file.

    Features

    • Automatic face detectionFinds faces from large close-ups to small faces in crowds, using several passes over big photos.
    • Four cover stylesBlur, pixel mosaic, emoji or a solid colour, with adjustable strength, shape and size.
    • Tap to toggleKeep your own face or a speaker visible while hiding everyone else.
    • Manual boxesCover anything the detector missed, or other private details.
    • Batch processingAdd up to 100 photos and download them all in one ZIP.
    • Covers cannot be undoneBlur and mosaic are made from a shrunken copy of the face, so the original detail is not in the saved photo.
    • Location data removedSaved photos contain no EXIF camera or GPS information.
    • PrivateDetection runs on your device; nothing is uploaded.

    Worked example: a class photo for a newsletter

    A teacher wants to post a photo of 24 pupils in the school newsletter, but three families have not given permission. She drops the photo in and the face finder outlines all 24 faces in green. She presses "Uncover all", then taps only the three faces that must stay hidden so they turn green again. She chooses Emoji with 🙂, turns off "Show outlines" to check the final look, presses "Download this photo" and adds it to the newsletter.

    Tips and tricks

    • Always look over the result before sharing. Faces that are tiny, in profile, behind sunglasses or partly hidden can be missed.
    • Use Pixels or Solid colour when privacy really matters; a light blur can still let people recognise a face they know.
    • Increase Cover size to hide hair and ears too, which helps make people harder to recognise.
    • Draw a box over name badges, school logos and number plates as well; they can identify people just as easily as faces.
    • Turn off "Show outlines" to see exactly what the saved photo will look like.

    Problems and how to fix them

    A face was not found

    Choose "Draw a new box" and drag over it. Very small, dark, blurred or side-on faces are harder to detect.

    Something that is not a face was covered

    Tap it to switch the cover off. It turns red and dashed and stays visible in the saved photo.

    The face finder does not start

    It needs a modern browser with WebAssembly and a one-time download of about 15 MB. Check your connection and reload. You can still draw boxes by hand.

    The emoji looks different on another device

    Emoji are drawn with your device's emoji font, so their style follows your phone or computer. The saved photo keeps the look you see.

    My iPhone photo will not open

    HEIC photos cannot be opened by most browsers. Convert them with the HEIC to JPG tool first.

    Questions and answers

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. The face finder is downloaded to your browser once and runs on your device. Your photos are never sent anywhere.

    Does it find every face?

    It finds most faces, from close-ups to small faces in crowds, but very small, blurred, side-on or partly hidden faces can be missed. Always check, and draw a box over any face it missed.

    Can the blur be removed later?

    No. The blur and mosaic are made from a shrunken copy of the face, so the detail is not in the saved photo. For the strongest privacy choose Pixels or Solid colour.

    Can I keep some faces visible?

    Yes. Tap a face to switch its cover off; it shows a red dashed outline and stays visible in the saved photo.

    Is the location data removed?

    Yes. Saved photos are drawn again from scratch, so camera and GPS details (EXIF) are not included.

    How many photos can I do at once?

    Up to 100. Use Download all (ZIP) to save them together.
