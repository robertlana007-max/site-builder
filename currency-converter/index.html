    What is Currency Converter?

    The Currency Converter changes an amount from one currency to another using daily mid-market reference rates. It covers more than 170 currencies, including the Pakistani rupee (PKR), Indian rupee (INR), UAE dirham (AED), Saudi riyal (SAR) and Bangladeshi taka (BDT), plus Bitcoin, Ethereum and gold.

    The result updates as you type, in both directions. You can also see a rate history chart for any pair over 7 days, 30 days, 90 days or a year, and convert one amount into many currencies at once.

    Rates come from ExchangeRate-API, with the free currency-api project as a backup and for crypto, metals and history. They are reference rates published about once a day, not live trading prices. The last rates are saved on your device so the converter still works offline.

    What you can use it for

    Sending money home

    Check how many rupees, taka or pesos a transfer from dirhams, riyals, pounds or dollars should give, before you pay a transfer service.

    Comparing bank and exchange shop rates

    Compare the rate you are offered with the mid-market rate to see how much margin the bank or exchange shop is taking.

    Travel budgets

    Turn your trip budget into the local currency, or work out what a price you see abroad costs at home.

    Online shopping and freelancing

    See what a price in US dollars or euros costs in your own currency, or what a client's payment is worth after conversion.

    Watching a rate over time

    Use the 30-day, 90-day or 1-year chart to see whether a currency is getting stronger or weaker before you exchange money.

    Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Currency Converter

    1. Choose the currency you have

      Click the From box. A list opens with a search field. Type a code such as USD, a name such as dirham, or a country such as Pakistan, then pick the currency.

    2. Choose the currency you want

      Do the same in the To box. Popular pairs such as USD → PKR or AED → PKR are one click away under the result.

    3. Type the amount

      Type in the box under From and the converted amount appears under To straight away. You can also type in the To box to work backwards, for example to find how many dollars give 100,000 rupees.

    4. Read the rate lines

      Under the big result you see the rate both ways, for example 1 USD = 276.39 PKR and 1 PKR = 0.0036 USD.

    5. Check when the rates were updated

      The grey line under the result says when the rates were published and where they came from. If you are offline, it shows that saved rates are being used and their date.

    6. Look at the rate history

      Pick 7D, 30D, 90D or 1Y above the chart. Move your mouse over the chart, or tap it on a phone, to read the rate on any day. High, Low and Change sum up the period.

    7. Convert into several currencies

      In Convert to many currencies at once, click + Add a currency to add more. Every row updates when you change the amount or the From currency.

    8. Add your own rate if needed

      If a currency is missing, or you want to use your own rate, open the section at the bottom, enter the rate and a 3-letter code, and click Save my rate.

    Worked example: checking a money transfer from the UAE to Pakistan

    You want to send 2,000 AED to Pakistan. Pick AED in From and PKR in To (or click the AED → PKR button) and type 2000. Suppose the result line shows 1 AED = 75.26 PKR, so 2,000 AED is about 150,520 PKR at the mid-market rate. A transfer service offers 74.10 PKR per dirham, which gives 148,200 PKR. The difference, 2,320 PKR, is the service's margin, about 1.5%. Open the 30D chart to see whether the rate has been rising or falling before you decide.

    Tips and tricks

    • The rate you get from a bank or exchange shop is usually a little worse than the mid-market rate shown here. Use the converter to compare offers.
    • Rates are published about once a day, so checking every few minutes will not show a new number. The update time under the result tells you how fresh they are.
    • Click the ⇄ button to swap the two currencies and see the reverse rate.
    • Add the currencies you use most to the multi-currency table. The list is remembered on this device.
    • Open the page once while online before a trip, so the latest rates are saved for offline use.
    • Crypto prices move much faster than daily rates. Treat Bitcoin and Ethereum values as a rough guide only.

    Problems and how to fix them

    It says it could not download rates

    Check your internet connection and click Check for new rates. If you used the converter before, the last saved rates are still shown with their date.

    My currency is not in the list

    Search by its code or country name first. If it is really missing, open the section at the bottom of the tool and enter your own rate.

    The chart says history could not be loaded

    The history comes from a separate free service. Try again later, or pick a different period. Days already loaded are saved, so the chart fills in faster next time.

    The result is different from Google or XE

    Each source collects rates at a different time of day, so small differences are normal. Check the update time under the result.

    Some flags show as letters

    Some computers, especially Windows, do not show flag emojis. The currency code and name are still shown next to them.

    Questions and answers

    Where do the exchange rates come from?

    Daily mid-market reference rates from ExchangeRate-API, with the free currency-api project as a backup and for crypto, gold and the history chart. The page shows when the rates were last updated.

    Are these live market rates?

    No. They are reference rates published about once a day. The page checks for new rates every 10 minutes while it is open, but the rates themselves change once a day.

    Why does my bank give a different rate?

    Banks, exchange companies and card providers add a margin to the mid-market rate, and some charge fees. Use this converter to see how big their margin is.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. The last rates you downloaded are saved on your device and used when you are offline, with a clear note showing their date.

    Is Pakistani rupee (PKR) included?

    Yes. PKR, INR, AED, SAR, BDT and about 160 other currencies are included, plus Bitcoin, Ethereum and gold. You can also add your own rate for a currency that is missing.
