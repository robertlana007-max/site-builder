What is Cooking Converter?

The Cooking Converter is three kitchen tools in one. The ingredient converter changes cups, spoons, milliliters, grams, ounces and pounds using the density of common ingredients like flour, sugar, butter, rice and milk.

The oven converter changes temperatures between °C, °F, fan (convection) and gas mark. The recipe scaler multiplies a pasted ingredient list for more or fewer servings.

What you can use it for

Use recipes from other countries

Turn US cups into grams or metric cups.

Bake by weight

Get grams for flour, sugar and butter.

Set your oven

Convert °F, °C, fan and gas mark.

Cook for more people

Scale a recipe from 4 to 6 servings in one step.

Halve a recipe

Set new servings to half to cut every amount.

Step-by-step tutorial: how to use Cooking Converter

  1. Enter an amount

    Type it in "Amount", for example 2, 1.5, 3/4 or 1 1/2.

  2. Choose unit and ingredient

    Pick the unit and the ingredient, such as "Flour, all-purpose / plain".

  3. Choose the cup standard

    Pick US, metric, Australian or old UK cups and spoons.

  4. Read the conversion

    The big line shows the main answer and the boxes show every unit.

  5. Convert oven temperature

    Type a temperature, pick °C, °F or gas mark, and say whether the recipe is for a fan oven.

  6. Scale a recipe

    Enter "Recipe makes" and "I want" servings and paste your ingredients one per line. Copy the scaled list.

Worked examples

1 US cup of all-purpose flour is about 125 g; 200 g of sugar is about 1 US cup. 180 °C is 356 °F, gas mark 4, or 160 °C in a fan oven. Scaling from 4 to 6 servings multiplies by 1.5: "2 cups flour" becomes "3 cups flour" and "1 1/2 tsp baking powder" becomes "2 1/4 tsp".

Tips and tricks

  • Spoon flour into the cup and level it; scooping packs in more.
  • Use weights for baking when you can.
  • Round scaled eggs sensibly, or beat and measure part of an egg.
  • Baking times and pan sizes may change when you scale a recipe.

Problems and how to fix them

"Enter an amount" appears

Use numbers and fractions only, like 1 1/2 or 0.5, without units.

A line was not scaled

Only numbers at the start of a line are scaled. Tick the box to scale numbers later in the line too.

No gas mark is shown

Gas marks only cover about 110–260 °C.

Questions and answers

Why does a cup of flour weigh less than a cup of sugar?

Different ingredients have different densities. A US cup of flour is about 125 g, sugar about 200 g and butter about 227 g.

How big is a cup?

A US cup is 236.6 ml, a metric cup (Australia, Canada, New Zealand and others) is 250 ml, and old UK recipes used 284 ml.

How much should I lower the oven for fan?

About 20 °C (25 °F) lower than the conventional temperature.

How does the recipe scaler work?

It multiplies the number at the start of each line by new servings ÷ original servings, including fractions like 1 1/2 and ½.

Are the weights exact?

They are averages. How you fill a cup changes dry ingredients by 10–15%, so a scale is most accurate.
